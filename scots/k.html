Scots Words

Scots Words: K

ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWYZ

kail

colewort, cabbage; broth

10 in 5 novels. Forms: kail (9), kail's (1).

jist Meg Horn, the auld kail-runt, an’ Sanny Graham, the stickit minister. Malcolm

kailyard

cabbage garden

2 in Robert Falconer. Forms: kailyard (2).

a wheen hooses, and a kailyard or twa, wi' a bit fairmy on the tap o' a cauld hill near the sea-shore Robert Falconer

kaim

comb

4 in Heather and Snow and Sir Gibbie. Forms: kaims (2), kaimin (1), kaimbt (1).

rejoined Kirsty, with a smile, ‘the catacombs has naething to du wi’ cats or kaims! Heather and Snow

keek

peep, peek

7 in 5 novels. Forms: keek (4), keekin' (2), keekit (1).

Jist open the door, mem, gien ye please, an lat’s hae a keek intill ’t. Malcolm

keepit

kept

29 in 10 novels. Forms: keepit (29).

I hae keepit it ready rowed up, ever sin’ my daddy washed it oot. Malcolm

keeriosity

curiosity

1 in Malcolm. Forms: keeriosities (1).

keeriosities, ye ken. Malcolm

keerious

curious

3 in Malcolm, Sir Gibbie and Warlock o’ Glenwarlock. Forms: keerious (3).

But what’s he gaein’ to du wi’ ye, Maister Cosmo, gien a body micht speir ’at has nae richt to be keerious? Warlock o’ Glenwarlock

kelpie

water horse (a malign river spirit)

163 in 6 novels. Forms: kelpie (159), kelpies (2), kelpy (2).

An’ I wad fain ken what ye’ll say til ‘t, for ye never wad alloo o’ kelpies Warlock o’ Glenwarlock

ken

know

1,908 in all 11 novels. Forms: ken (1,365), kens (193), kent (174), kenned (73), kenna (63), kennin' (19), kennin (9), ken't (8), kentna (1), kensna (1), and 2 more. One of the 100 commonest.

but jist say a fair word for the livin' ower the deid, ye ken. Alec Forbes of Howglen

kenspeckle

conspicuous, easily recognised

1 in Sir Gibbie. Forms: kenspeckle (1).

A description of him was added, and the stray was so kenspeckle, that Mistress Croale saw the necessity of haste to any hope of advantage. Sir Gibbie

kep

catch, intercept

8 in 6 novels. Forms: kep (5), keppit (3).

MacDonald glosses it: “intercept”; “caught in passing”.

She’ll be no ill guidit, and I’m no gaeing to kep her at ilka turn. David Elginbrod

kettlefu'

kettleful

1 in Alec Forbes of Howglen. Forms: kettlefu' (1).

Up comes the lass, and says I, 'Bell, lat's hae a kettlefu' o' het water.' And to mak' a lang story short, I could never … Alec Forbes of Howglen

keye

key

2 in Malcolm. Forms: keye (2).

Weel, my lord, I hae her unner lock an’ keye. Malcolm

kickit

kicked

2 in Robert Falconer and Warlock o’ Glenwarlock. Forms: kickit (2).

Ye wad fain hae the auld man’s shune, an’ mak sur o’ them afore he kickit them frae him! Warlock o’ Glenwarlock

killogue

plot, confer secretly

1 in Alec Forbes of Howglen. Forms: killoguin (1).

She was stan'in' killoguin wi' a bonnie young lad an' a horse. Alec Forbes of Howglen

killt

killed

15 in 7 novels. Forms: killt (14), kill't (1).

There can be no doobt it was his father oor lord killt Warlock o’ Glenwarlock

kilt

kilt; to tuck up

1 in Malcolm. Forms: kilt's (1).

a kilt’s no seafarin’ claes. Malcolm

kin'

kind

187 in all 11 novels. Forms: kin' (182), kinde (3), kin's (2).

He's a kin' o' librarian at yer ain college i' the noo, Mr Forbes. Alec Forbes of Howglen

kin'ly

kindly

7 in 5 novels. Forms: kin'ly (5), kin'list (1), kin'liest (1).

an’ that kin’ly wi’ a’ cratur, baith man an’ beast! Malcolm

kin'ness

kindness

14 in 9 novels. Forms: kin'ness (14).

but it’s little kin’ness my gran’father has expairienced frae Cawmill o’ Glenlyon, mem. Malcolm

kintra

country

1 in David Elginbrod. Forms: kintra (1).

There’s twa as fine lads an’ gude scholars as ye’ll fin’ in the haill kintra-side, no to mention the laird and mysel’. David Elginbrod

kirk

church

128 in all 11 novels. Forms: kirk (122), kirks (3), kirk's (1), kirkies (1), kirkie (1).

Do ye think I wad tak frae the kirk to pit into your till? Alec Forbes of Howglen

kirkyaird

churchyard

17 in 8 novels. Forms: kirkyard (8), kirkyaird (7), kirkyairds (1), kirkyards (1).

the place was as dacent an’ still as ony kirkyard i’ the munelicht! Donal Grant

kirn

churn

2 in David Elginbrod. Forms: kirn (2).

Jist pit yer han’ to this kirn noo, like a gude bairn. David Elginbrod

kirsten

christen

3 in Alec Forbes of Howglen and Robert Falconer. Forms: kirsten (1), kirstenin' (1), kirstened (1).

Here's a glass o' whisky I got frae my mither to kirsten the boat. Alec Forbes of Howglen

kist

chest, trunk; coffin

24 in 8 novels. Forms: kist (20), kists (4).

MacDonald glosses it: “chest”.

He may turn ’t intil a breid-kist, or what he likes, the gomf! Salted with Fire

kitchie

kitchen; anything eaten with bread or potatoes as a relish

12 in 7 novels. Forms: kitchie (12).

'Weel, I s' hae nae sic things gang by my kitchie door. Robert Falconer

kittlin

kitten

5 in 4 novels. Forms: kittlin' (3), kittlins (1), kitlins (1).

Or I kent a laddie ance 'at keepit a haill faimily o' kittlins. Robert Falconer

knacker

horse-slaughterer

1 in Malcolm. Forms: knacker's (1).

A meagre cat, an over-fed pet spaniel, a ditchless frog, a horse whose days hung over the verge of the knacker’s yard Malcolm

kneip

knock

1 in Robert Falconer. Forms: kneipit (1).

'Ane in ilka han', jist for the fun o' 't, he kneipit their heids thegither, as gin they hed been twa carldoddies (stalks of ribgrass). Robert Falconer

knicht

knight

4 in Heather and Snow and Sir Gibbie. Forms: knicht (3), knichtly (1).

I saw the bonnie sicht come up o’ a knicht in airmour, helmet an’ shield an’ iron sheen an’ a’ Sir Gibbie

knockit

knocked

5 in Donal Grant, The Marquis of Lossie and Warlock o’ Glenwarlock. Forms: knockit (5).

I reckon ye wadna care though the she-deevil knockit oot yer harns The Marquis of Lossie

knops

knobs

1 in Alec Forbes of Howglen. Forms: knops (1).

… young larches crowded them in, their rich brown cones hanging like the knops that looped up their dark garments fringed with paler green. Alec Forbes of Howglen

koft

bought

1 in Sir Gibbie. Forms: koft (1).

… believe me, whan he comes to un’erstan’ ’at ye want that hoose koft, he’ll no be a day ohn gane to somebody or anither … Sir Gibbie

kolledg

college

1 in Sir Gibbie. Forms: kolledg (1).

my dear minister, If you will teak Donal too, and lett him go to the kolledg, I will go with you as seens ye like Sir Gibbie

kye

cows, cattle

4 in 4 novels. Forms: kye (4).

His horse war aye swifter, an’ his kye aye better milkers nor ither fowk’s Malcolm
