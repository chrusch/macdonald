A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

see

sight · vision · behold

Frequency in the non-fiction: see, sees, seen, seeing and saw about 1,000; vision and visions 98; sight 86. The central passage is “God’s Family” in The Hope of the Gospel, on Blessed are the pure in heart.

In brief. For MacDonald seeing is the highest form of knowing: an inward sight, given to the pure in heart, by which a truth or God himself is known as plainly as a face. It is distinct from proof and from signs, which only produce probability. To see a truth and to love it are one act, and to see God is the end of creation. Sight can be lost to the cares and things of this world, and the will can hold to God even when it cannot see him. The Beatific Vision is the final condition of man; the vision on the way there comes in glimpses.

Shades of meaning

  1. The cry to see God. “The cry of the deepest in man has always been, to see God. It was the cry of Moses and the cry of Job, the cry of psalmist and of prophet” (The Hope of the Gospel, “God’s Family”). It was Christ’s own bliss: “To see God was the Lord’s own, eternal, one happiness; therefore he knew that the essential bliss of the creature is to behold the face of the creator” (same). In that face “lies the mystery of a man’s own nature, the history of a man’s own being” (same).

  2. The end of creation. “To see God is to stand on the highest point of created being” (same). “That there we should stand, is the end of our creation” (same). Yet the seeing never closes: “Then shall we know him with the infinitude of an ever-growing knowledge” (same). See Maker · creation.

  3. Only the pure in heart. “None but the pure in heart see God; only the growing-pure hope to see him” (same). The alternative is not blankness but the self: “If you care to see God, be pure. If you will not be pure, you will grow more and more impure; and instead of seeing God, will at length find yourself face to face with a vast inane” (same), filled by “your own false self” (same). See self.

  4. To see a truth. Seeing is the way any truth is known, and it is final: “To see a truth, to know what it is, to understand it, and to love it, are all one” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Consuming Fire”). “But once beheld it is for ever. To see one divine fact is to stand face to face with essential eternal life” (same). See truth and understand.

  5. Sight against signs. Miracles cannot do what seeing does: “Inward sight alone can convince of truth; signs and wonders never” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Temptation in the Wilderness”). The vision “was in the Lord’s face and form to the pure in heart who were able to see God; but not in his signs and wonders to those who sought after such” (same). See miracle.

  6. Seeing and loving, revealed to the child. Representation can be refused, revelation cannot: “the revealed God no one can refuse; to see God and to love him are one. He can be revealed only to the child; perfectly, to the pure child only” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Yoke of Jesus”). Obedience serves the same end: “it is for the sake of obedient vision that our Lord commands us. For then our heart meets his: we see God” (Unspoken Sermons, “Love Thy Neighbour”). See child and obedience.

  7. Sight lost to care. Once seen is not always seen: “the cares of this world—with no right to any part in our thought, seeing either they are unreasonable or God imperfect—so blind the eyes of the soul to the radiance of the eternally true, that they see it only as if it ought to be true, not as if it must be true” (The Hope of the Gospel, “God’s Family”). See stupidity.

  8. Holding God unseen. Christ on the cross lost “the vanishing vision of the Father” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Eloi”), and in that loss the will shows its height. The highest condition of the being “lies in the Beatific Vision, in the sight of God” (same); but the highest condition of the will “is when, not seeing God, not seeming to itself to grasp him at all, it yet holds him fast” (same). See will and faith.

Neighbouring words

  • understand, know (its fruit). Sense 4; see understand.
  • pure in heart, purity (its condition). Sense 3.
  • revelation, reveal (what is seen). Sense 6; see revelation.
  • face (its object). Senses 1 and 3: “face to face” is MacDonald’s commonest figure for it.
  • signs, wonders, proof (its substitutes). Sense 5.
  • faith, trust (what holds when sight fails). Sense 8.
  • care, things (what blinds it). Sense 7; see stupidity.
  • imagination (its human form). The imagination sees the forms of thought; see imagination.

Scripture. Matthew 5:8, Blessed are the pure in heart: for they shall see God, the text of “God’s Family”; 1 John 3:2, we shall see him as he is; Job 42:5, now mine eye seeth thee, behind sense 1; John 14:9, he that hath seen me hath seen the Father; Matthew 27:46, My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?, the text of “The Eloi” (sense 8).
