A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

being

existence · self-existent

Frequency in the non-fiction: being (as noun and participle) 758; beings 19; existence and exist 232. Most uses of “being” are participles; the noun is rarer and weightier.

In brief. What a creature or God fundamentally is, as distinct from what he does, feels or knows. MacDonald uses being in three linked ways. God is being itself, the only self-existent. A man’s being is the deepest layer of himself, below consciousness and below his acts, where only God can work. That being is meant to come into its truth, and the man must will it. Existence is often the lower word: bare continuance, which without life or right is not worth having.

Shades of meaning

  1. God as being. God is not one being among others but being itself: “In the very nature of being—that is, God—it must be hard—and divine history shows how hard—to create that which shall be not himself, yet like himself” (Unspoken Sermons, “Life”). “God is being; death is nowhere!” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Fear of God”). Even nothing depends on him: “If God were not, there would not even be nothing. Not even nothingness preceded life. Nothingness owes its very idea to existence” (Unspoken Sermons, “Life”). See life and death.

  2. Self-existence. The one thing in God that cannot be understood: “how he can be self-existent, and next, how he can make other beings exist: self-existence and creation no man will ever understand” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Creation in Christ”). God is “a self-existent Love, creative in virtue of its being love” (Miracles, “Introduction”). Man’s deepest need follows from not being self-existent: “the need itself is that of existence not self-existent for the consciousness of the presence of the causing Self-existent. It is the man’s need of God” (The Hope of the Gospel, “God’s Family”).

  3. The Son’s choice of his own being. Jesus’ answer to the Father’s self-existence is to will his sonship: “This choice of his own being, in the full knowledge of what he did; this active willing to be the Son of the Father, perfect in obedience—is that in Jesus which responds and corresponds to the self-existence of God” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Creation in Christ”). His being and doing are one across time: “The whole being and doing of Jesus on earth is the same as his being and doing from all eternity” (same sermon). See child · son · sonship.

  4. A share in one’s own being. Man too must take part in his existence: in us “must be the life that wills itself—a life in so far resembling the self-existent life and partaking of its image, that it has a share in its own being” (Unspoken Sermons, “Life”). The Diary prays for this consent: “Willing to be because thy will is pure; / Willing thy will beyond all bounds of hope” (Diary of an Old Soul, December). See will, sense 7.

  5. The roots of being. A man’s being lies deeper than he can reach, and only God works there. The cure of sin lies “in the very roots of his own being, where, knowing nothing of the secrets of his essential existence, he can immediately do nothing, where the maker of him alone is” able to work (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Remission of Sins”). True knowledge of oneself is to find “the source of his being, with a Will by which his own will exists, with a Consciousness by and through which he is conscious” (A Dish of Orts, “A Sketch of Individual Development”). See heart, sense 1.

  6. The truth of one’s being. Each person has a true being, God’s idea of him, that is meant to come out. When a man rests in God’s fatherhood, “then the truth of his being, the eternal fact at the root of his new name, his real nature, his idea—born in God at first, and responsive to the truth, the being of God, his origin—begins to show itself” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Truth”). Recognizing “that part of his being where God is not” is the beginning: “then indeed is the man in the region of truth, and beginning to come true in himself” (same sermon). “God and man can meet only by the man’s becoming that which God meant him to be” (A Dish of Orts, “Essays on Some of the Forms of Literature”). See truth and self, sense 4.

  7. The law of one’s being. Freedom is obedience to what one is made to be: “that I be free by obedience to the law of my being, the live and live-making will by which life is life, and my life is myself” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Inheritance”). The end of it is sonship: “For this are we created; it is the one end of our being, and includes all other ends whatever” (Unspoken Sermons, “Abba, Father!”). See freedom.

  8. Mere existence. Existence without life or right is a lesser and finally an unbearable thing: “It is life they must have; there is no enduring of existence without life” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Hardness of the Way”). The man who fails his relations is “undoing the right of his own existence, destroying his raison d’etre” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Truth”). In outer darkness he hangs “in a ceaseless vertigo of existence upon the verge of the gulf of his being” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Consuming Fire”). Righteousness is its ground: “To be right is no adjunct of completeness; it is the ground and foundation of existence” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Remission of Sins”).

  9. The whole being. In the commonest phrase, being means the whole person answering God: “The man who responds to this with his whole being, is of the truth” (Unspoken Sermons, “Kingship”). God’s word is his being uttered: man lives “by the Truth, that is, the Word, the Will, the uttered Being of God” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Temptation in the Wilderness”).

Neighbouring words

  • life (its living form). Being is what life is the life of; existence without life is sense 8. See life.
  • self-existence, creation (God’s being and its gift). Sense 2.
  • will, willing to be (man’s share in it). Sense 4; see will.
  • nature, idea, new name (its truth). Sense 6.
  • consciousness (what lies above it). Sense 5.
  • doing (its outward side). Sense 3. A man must “know his being, himself”, not Christ’s merits (Unspoken Sermons, “The Hardness of the Way”).
  • nothingness, death (its negation). Sense 1; see death.
  • righteousness (its foundation). Sense 8; see righteousness.

Scripture. Exodus 3:14, I AM THAT I AM, behind sense 1; Acts 17:28, in him we live, and move, and have our being; John 5:26, as the Father hath life in himself; so hath he given to the Son to have life in himself (sense 3); Revelation 2:17, a new name written, which no man knoweth saving he that receiveth it, behind sense 6.
