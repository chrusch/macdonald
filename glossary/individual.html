A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

individual

individuality · apartness

Frequency in the non-fiction: individual, individuals, individuality and individually 202. The main sources are “The New Name”, “Love Thy Neighbour”, “Life” and “The Eloi”.

In brief. The separate, particular being of each person, which God made deliberately and at cost. MacDonald holds that individuality is not opposed to God or to other people: it is the condition of choosing God freely, the ground of love between persons, and something perfected, not dissolved, in union with God. Each individual has a relation to God that no one else shares, and men are gathered not as a mass but as a body. True individuality comes only through surrender of the self-will; Christ is its generator and perfecter.

Shades of meaning

  1. Made for free choice. God’s problem in creation is “so far to separate from himself that which must yet on him be ever and always and utterly dependent, that it shall have the existence of an individual, and be able to turn and regard him—choose him” (Unspoken Sermons, “Life”). “For God made our individuality as well as, and a greater marvel than, our dependence; made our apartness from himself, that freedom should bind us divinely dearer to himself” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Eloi”). See freedom and Maker · creation, sense 2.

  2. A means to oneness. Individuality is not the last word: “the final end of the separation is not individuality; that is but a means to it; the final end is oneness” (Unspoken Sermons, “Life”). A man comes to see “that his individuality can be perfected only in the love of his neighbour, and that his being can find its end only in oneness with the source from which it came” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Fear of God”). See oneness · harmony.

  3. The bond of love, not a barrier. The isolated thinker says “I am an individual; he is an individual” (Unspoken Sermons, “Love Thy Neighbour”), and concludes that love is impossible. MacDonald answers that he “falsely judges the individuality a separation. On the contrary, it is the sole possibility and very bond of love” (same). See neighbour, sense 5.

  4. Each man’s own relation to God. The white stone of Revelation 2:17 means “the personal individual relation of every man to his God” (Unspoken Sermons, “The New Name”). There is a chamber in God “into which none can enter but the one, the individual, the peculiar man,—out of which chamber that man has to bring revelation and strength for his brethren” (same). See name · new name and revelation, sense 7.

  5. A body, not a mass. “There is no massing of men with God. When he speaks of gathered men, it is as a spiritual body, not a mass. For in a body every smallest portion is individual, and therefore capable of forming a part of the body” (same). So too humanity is “the humanity of the individual endlessly repeated” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Voice of Job”). See humanity.

  6. Perfected by Christ, through surrender. Christ is each man’s root, “the generator and perfecter of his individuality” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Truth”). In “Kingship” Christ says that “my perfect freedom, my pure individuality, rests on the fact that I have not another will than his” (Unspoken Sermons, “Kingship”). The self yielded to God is raised “then first and only to its true individuality, freedom, and life” (Unspoken Sermons, “Self-Denial”). See self and will.

  7. Born of God’s individuality. MacDonald prays “that my individuality have the freedom that belongs to it as born of his individuality” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Inheritance”). Even judgment works with it: the divine fate “works along with the human individuality which the divine individuality has created in them” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Consuming Fire”).

  8. Sin cured in individuals. Against thinking of sin only in the race: “wrong is always generated in and done by an individual; that the wrongness exists in the individual, and by him is passed over, as tendency, to the race; and that no evil can be cured in the race, except by its being cured in its individuals” (The Hope of the Gospel, “Salvation from Sin”). See sin · evil.

Neighbouring words

  • self (its false and true forms). Sense 6; see self.
  • otherness, apartness (its relation to others). Senses 1 and 3.
  • oneness, harmony (its end). Sense 2.
  • freedom, choice (its purpose). Senses 1 and 6; see freedom.
  • new name, white stone (its seal). Sense 4; see name · new name.
  • body, race, humanity (the whole it belongs to). Senses 5 and 8.

Scripture. Revelation 2:17, I will give him a white stone, and in the stone a new name written, which no man knoweth saving he that receiveth it, the text of “The New Name” (sense 4); 1 Corinthians 12:27, Now ye are the body of Christ, and members in particular (sense 5); John 17:21, that they all may be one (sense 2); Luke 15:18, I will arise and go to my father (sense 1).
