A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

essential

essence · essential life

Frequency in the non-fiction: essential and essentially 138; essence 48. The word is most frequent in “Life”, the first sermon of the first series, and “The Creation in Christ”.

In brief. Essential in MacDonald most often means what a thing truly is, as against its accidents, and above all what God is. God’s essential being is love, his fire, his life; Christ is the essential Life and men are made to share in essential being. So essential attaches to life, joy, love, humanity, childhood and bliss to mark the real thing, the thing God means, beneath the blurred form we see. It also keeps its common sense of what cannot be done without.

Shades of meaning

  1. The real beneath the blurred. Of the child set in the midst: “It is the essential of which God speaks, the real by which he judges, the undying of which he is the God” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Child in the Midst”). The disciples were meant to see “that the essential childhood was meant, and not a blurred and half-obliterated childhood” (same). See child.

  2. God’s essential being. “The fire of God, which is his essential being, his love, his creative power, is a fire unlike its earthly symbol in this, that it is only at a distance it burns” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Fear of God”). The consuming fire “is essential love” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Consuming Fire”). Love is the essence of Godhead, not power: “a being whose essence was only power would be such a negation of the divine that no righteous worship could be offered him” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Creation in Christ”). See consuming fire and love.

  3. Essential life. Eternal life is “a being that is one with the essential Life” (Unspoken Sermons, “Life”). “All the being must partake of essential being; life must be assisted, upheld, comforted, every part, with life” (same). The man who depends for well-being on anything “but life, the life essential, is a slave” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Hardness of the Way”). See life and eternal.

  4. Essential joy and sorrow. “Less than God just as he is will not comfort men for the essential sorrow of their existence. Only God the gift can turn that sorrow into essential joy” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Cause of Spiritual Stupidity”). The neighbour-lover’s “essential joy” lies “abroad in the liberty of the All” (Unspoken Sermons, “Love Thy Neighbour”). See joy.

  5. Essential humanity. We call our enemies men and women “in virtue of the divine essence which is in them, that pure essential humanity” (Unspoken Sermons, “Love Thine Enemy”). Evil is no part of it: “no evil is, or ever could be, of the essential being and nature of the creature God made” (Unspoken Sermons, “Man’s Difficulty Concerning Prayer”). See humanity and sin.

  6. The essence of a thing. The white stone holds “the essence of religion” (Unspoken Sermons, “The New Name”); an ordinary name “has nothing essential in it” (same). Communion with God “that asks for nothing, yet asks for everything” is “the very essence of prayer” (Unspoken Sermons, “Man’s Difficulty Concerning Prayer”). See prayer.

  7. Necessary. In its ordinary sense: life in Jesus is “imperative, essential, to every man, woman, and child” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Creation in Christ”); the love of neighbour is “an essential of their being” (Unspoken Sermons, “Love Thy Neighbour”); in honest opinion “the one only essential point” is to walk by what we have seen (Unspoken Sermons, “The Truth in Jesus”). See opinion · doctrine · theology.

Neighbouring words

  • real, reality, true (near synonyms). Senses 1 and 6; see truth.
  • accidents, forms, imitations (its opposites). “The essential thing, and not any of its accidents or imitations, is intended” (Unspoken Sermons, “The New Name”).
  • being, self-existent (what it qualifies in God). Senses 2 and 3; see being.
  • life, joy, love, bliss (its common nouns). Senses 2 to 4.
  • humanity, childhood (its human objects). Senses 1 and 5.
  • idea (its counterpart). The essential child is “the divine idea of childhood” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Child in the Midst”).

Scripture. 1 John 4:8, God is love (sense 2); Hebrews 12:29, our God is a consuming fire (sense 2); John 1:4, In him was life, the text of “The Creation in Christ” (senses 3 and 7); 2 Peter 1:4, partakers of the divine nature (sense 3).
