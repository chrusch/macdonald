A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

duty

duties

Frequency in the non-fiction: duty 93; duties 13.

In brief. What is owed, and the first thing to be done. MacDonald gives duty two values. It is imperative and the only road forward: the duty of the moment is the door to light, and the man who has begun to recognize it is on the path of life. But duty done only as duty is a slave’s righteousness, which must give way to love. Duty is the way to freedom, and freedom is what lies beyond duty.

Shades of meaning

  1. Imperative. “Duty is imperative; it must be done. It is useless to think to escape the eternal law of things” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Last Farthing”).

  2. The duty of the moment. MacDonald’s practical rule. “If a man lay himself out to do the immediate duty of the moment, wonderfully little forethought, I suspect, will be found needful” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Cause of Spiritual Stupidity”). In Robert Falconer the question “What is my next duty?” (Robert Falconer, ch. X, “A Neophyte”) gets the answer: “Your next duty is just to determine what your next duty is” (Robert Falconer, ch. X, “A Neophyte”). In The Princess and Curdie, “the road to the next duty is the only straight one” (The Princess and Curdie, ch. 1, “The Mountain”).

  3. The way into light. Doing duty opens understanding: “the doing of duty is the shortest—in very fact, the only way into the light” (The Seaboard Parish, ch. II). “He who goes about his everyday duty as the work the Father has given him to do, is he who lets his light shine” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Salt and the Light of the World”). See obedience and light.

  4. A beginning only. “He who has begun to recognize duty, and acknowledge the facts of his being, is but a tottering child on the path of life. He is on the path” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Way”).

  5. The slave’s motive. Duty without love is servitude: those who serve God “from duty, and do not know him as their father, the joy of their being, they are slaves—good slaves, but slaves” (Unspoken Sermons, “Freedom”). The power to keep the law is “a power of life, not of struggle; the strength of love, not the effort of duty” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Way”). In Paul Faber: “Duty is the only path to freedom, but that freedom is the love that is beyond and prevents duty” (Paul Faber, Surgeon, ch. XXX, “The Pony-Carriage”). See freedom, sense 2.

  6. No merit. Duty done earns nothing: “Duty is a thing prepaid: it can never have desert. There is no claim on God that springs from us: all is from him” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Voice of Job”).

  7. The first duty. Faith in God: “while faith in God is the first duty, and may therefore well be called righteousness in the man in whom it is operative, even though it be imperfect” (Unspoken Sermons, “Righteousness”). It lies deeper than feeling: “The first duty is too high and too deep to come first into consciousness” (same sermon).

  8. God’s duties. God too is bound by what he has made: “he can have no duty that is not both just and merciful. More is required of the maker, by his own act of creation, than can be required of men” (Unspoken Sermons, “Justice”). See justice, sense 5.

Neighbouring words

  • obedience (its act). Sense 3; see obedience.
  • love (what supersedes it). Sense 5; see love.
  • slave, law (its lower form). Sense 5.
  • freedom (where it leads). Sense 5; see freedom.
  • merit, desert, reward (what it cannot earn). Sense 6.
  • conscience (its herald). See conscience, sense 5.
  • privilege (its opposite pole). Commending one’s spirit to God is not to be pressed “as a duty: that is to turn the highest privilege we possess into a burden grievous to be borne” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Hands of the Father”).

Scripture. Luke 17:10, We are unprofitable servants: we have done that which was our duty to do, quoted in “The Voice of Job” and “The Reward of Obedience”; Matthew 6:34, Take therefore no thought for the morrow, behind sense 2; Ecclesiastes 12:13, Fear God, and keep his commandments: for this is the whole duty of man.
