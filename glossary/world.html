A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

world

the world · worldly · the earth

Frequency in the corpus: the world in about 180 paragraphs of Unspoken Sermons, The Hope of the Gospel and Miracles of Our Lord, worldly and worldliness in about 30. The main sources are “Jesus in the World” and “The Heirs of Heaven and Earth” in The Hope of the Gospel.

In brief. For MacDonald the world is first the Father’s house, and Jesus was at home in it because he was at home with the Father. We are not yet at home in it, because we are not yet true children, and so we strive with Nature. The earth as God sees it is very good, fit for the meek to inherit, and they inherit it by joy in it, not by holding title to it. The world in the bad sense is the worldly spirit: worldly wisdom that ages a child’s face, the slavish worldly idea of freedom, the world-flattering servant. It is overcome not by fleeing it but by ceasing to believe in outside appearances. Jesus found his symbols in the world, not in the temple.

Shades of meaning

  1. The Father’s house. When the boy Jesus stayed behind in the temple he meant that he was among his Father’s belongings: “The world was his home because his father’s house. He was not a stranger who did not know his way about in it” (The Hope of the Gospel, “Jesus in the World”). See Father and home.

  2. Not yet at home. “We are not at home in this great universe, our father’s house. We ought to be, and one day we shall be, but we are not yet” (The Hope of the Gospel, “Jesus in the World”). “We are not at home in it, because we are not at home with the lord of the house, the father of the family, not one with our elder brother who is his right hand” (same). Hence “the hard struggle, the constant strife we hold with Nature—as we call the things of our father” (same). See nature and child.

  3. The world, not the temple. Jesus found his symbols “in the world and its lovely-lowly facts; on the roadside, in the field, in the vineyard, in the garden, in the house; in the family, and the commonest of its affairs” (The Hope of the Gospel, “Jesus in the World”). “What was his place of prayer? Not the temple, but the mountain-top” (same). See symbol and church.

  4. The earth the meek inherit. “The earth as God sees it, as those to whom the kingdom of heaven belongs also see it, is good, all good, very good, fit for the meek to inherit” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Heirs of Heaven and Earth”), not as men of the world count inheritance. “Which of the two possessed the earth—king Agrippa or tent-maker Paul?” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Heirs of Heaven and Earth”). “We cannot see the world as God means it, save in proportion as our souls are meek” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Heirs of Heaven and Earth”). See having and inheritance.

  5. Worldliness. The bad sense of the word is a spirit, not a place. “One of the saddest and not least common sights in the world is the face of a child whose mind is so brimful of worldly wisdom that the human childishness has vanished from it, as well as the divine childlikeness” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Child in the Midst”). “It is the vulgar, slavish, worldly idea of freedom, that it consists in being bound to nothing” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Voice of Job”). To wait for the other world to know Christ’s mind “seems to me the foolishness of a worldly and lazy spirit” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Higher Faith”). See freedom and self.

  6. Overcoming the world. When Jesus said he had overcome the world “he had overcome the cause of all doubt, the belief in the outside appearances and not in the living truth” (Miracles, “The Raising of the Dead”). A man’s first business in righting the world is “that integral part of the world where lies his business, his first business—namely, his own character and conduct” (The Hope of the Gospel, “Salvation from Sin”). See doubt and salvation.

  7. The world to come. MacDonald’s hope extends to the creatures: “To those who expect a world to come, I say then, Let us take heed how we carry ourselves to the creation which is to occupy with us the world to come” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Hope of the Universe”). See heaven and Maker.

Neighbouring words

  • house · home (image). The world as the Father’s house.
  • Nature (the things of our father). What we strive with until we are true children.
  • earth (inheritance). What the meek inherit.
  • worldliness · worldly wisdom (corruption). The spirit that ages the child.
  • temple (foil). Jesus’ symbols come from the world, not the temple.

Scripture. Wist ye not that I must be about my Father’s business? (Luke 2:49). Blessed are the meek: for they shall inherit the earth (Matthew 5:5). Be of good cheer; I have overcome the world (John 16:33). Love not the world, neither the things that are in the world (1 John 2:15).
