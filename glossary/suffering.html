A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

suffering

pain · sorrow · mourning

Frequency in the corpus: suffer, suffering, pain, sorrow and their forms in several hundred paragraphs. The main sources are “Sorrow the Pledge of Joy” in The Hope of the Gospel, “The Eloi” and “Justice” in Unspoken Sermons, and the healing chapters of Miracles.

In brief. Suffering is a means, never an end. It is against the first and last will of God, and it will pass, but it is a real good while it does its work: it brings the wanderer home, burns out selfishness, and opens the heart’s door to God. It is never payment for sin. Punishment weighs nothing against wrong, and to call Christ’s suffering a satisfaction is a pagan idea. God shares our sorrow, though he is not sorrowful in himself, and Christ’s suffering was the greater because his nature was finer. Sorrow is love’s child, and mourning will end in comfort that is more than restoration.

Shades of meaning

  1. A means, not an end. “Is it nothing, I answer, to be reminded that suffering is in its nature transitory—that it is against the first and final will of God—that it is a means only, not an end?” (Miracles, “The Cure of Simon’s Wife’s Mother”). “Pain is an evil; but a good as well, which it would be a great injury to take from the man before it had wrought its end. Then it becomes all evil, and must pass” (Miracles, “Miracles of Healing Unsolicited”). See Miracles, where healing waits on readiness.

  2. God suffers with us. “While they suffer he is brooding over them an eternal day, suffering with them but rejoicing in their future” (Miracles, “The Cure of Simon’s Wife’s Mother”). Yet “although man must sorrow, and God share in his sorrow, yet in himself God is not sorrowful, and the ‘glad creator’ never made man for sorrow” (The Hope of the Gospel, “Sorrow the Pledge of Joy”). See Father.

  3. No offset for sin. “Punishment, deserved suffering, is no equipoise to sin. It is no use laying it in the other scale. It will not move it a hair’s breadth. Suffering weighs nothing at all against sin” (Unspoken Sermons, “Justice”). The gospel is not “founded on the pagan notion that suffering is an offset for sin, and culminating in the vile assertion that the suffering of an innocent man, just because he is innocent, yea perfect, is a satisfaction to the holy Father” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Heirs of Heaven and Earth”). See justice and punishment.

  4. The door left on the latch. “God loves not sorrow, yet rejoices to see a man sorrowful, for in his sorrow man leaves his heavenward door on the latch, and God can enter to help him” (The Hope of the Gospel, “Sorrow the Pledge of Joy”). “The gladsome child runs farther afield; the wounded child turns to go home” (same). “There is no evil in sorrow” (same).

  5. Love’s child. “Grief is an ill-favoured thing, but she is Love’s own child, and her mother loves her” (The Hope of the Gospel, “Sorrow the Pledge of Joy”). “Love modelled by faith, is hope; love shaped by wrong, is anger—verily anger, though pure of sin; love invaded by loss, is grief” (same). See love, hope and wrath.

  6. The cure of selfishness. “Whatever selfishness clouds the love that mourns the loss of love, that selfishness must be taken out of it—burned out of it even by pain extreme, if such be needful.” God’s “cure for thy selfishness at once and thy suffering, is to make thee love more—and more truly” (The Hope of the Gospel, “Sorrow the Pledge of Joy”). See self and consuming fire.

  7. The suffering of redemption. Corruption “is met by the suffering which itself causes. That suffering is for redemption, for deliverance. It is the life in the corrupting thing that makes the suffering possible; it is the live part, not the corrupted part that suffers” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Hope of the Universe”). Children and fathers suffer for one another: “For so are men bound together, that no good can come to one but all must share in it” (Miracles, “Miracles Granted to the Prayer of Friends”). See salvation.

  8. The sufferings of Christ. “Let no one think that those were less because he was more. The more delicate the nature, the more alive to all that is lovely and true, lawful and right, the more does it feel the antagonism of pain” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Eloi”). The cry of forsakenness “comes not out of happiness, out of peace, out of hope. Not even out of suffering comes that cry. It was a cry in desolation, but it came out of Faith” (same). The redeemed call him “Monarch of Suffering” (Unspoken Sermons, “Love Thine Enemy”). See Jesus and will.

  9. Comfort more than restoration. “Much of what goes by the name of comfort, is merely worthless; and such as could be comforted by it, I should not care to comfort” (The Hope of the Gospel, “Sorrow the Pledge of Joy”). The mourner will find the lost “not merely more than the lost, but more the person lost than he or she that was lost” (same). “No man will carry his mourning with him into heaven” (same). See heaven and death.

In the fiction and poetry. In At the Back of the North Wind the North Wind sinks a ship and tells Diamond that she must do it and that she trusts a far-off song she hears, which says that all will be well. It is MacDonald’s best-known picture of sense 1. This is general knowledge, not drawn from the corpus.

Neighbouring words

  • pain, agony, torture (its forms). Senses 1 and 8.
  • sorrow, grief, mourning (its inward forms). Senses 4, 5 and 9.
  • punishment (deserved suffering). Sense 3; see justice.
  • selfishness (what it burns out). Sense 6.
  • comfort (its answer). Sense 9.
  • joy, gladness (its end). God “makes the joy the last in every song” (The Hope of the Gospel, “Sorrow the Pledge of Joy”); see joy.

Scripture. Matthew 5:4, Blessed are they that mourn: for they shall be comforted (senses 4, 5 and 9); Romans 8:20–22, the creature was made subject to vanity … in hope (sense 7); Hebrews 2:10, perfect through sufferings (sense 8); Matthew 27:46, My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me? (sense 8); Isaiah 63:9, In all their affliction he was afflicted (sense 2); Revelation 21:4, God shall wipe away all tears (sense 9).
