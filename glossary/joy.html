A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

joy

gladness · the glad creator

Frequency in the corpus: joy, joyful, glad and gladness in about 300 paragraphs, spread through Unspoken Sermons, The Hope of the Gospel and Miracles.

In brief. Joy is nearer the nature of God than sorrow: God is “the glad creator”, and he made his children to be joyful. Joy is the harmony of the spirit, and its deepest form is the joy of being itself, which is the Father known as Father. Christ’s joy was the Father. Joy is not the reward of selfishness, and the child must be ready to give joy up for truth before he can enter into God’s joy. It comes as the fruit of loving, of obedient labour and of a clear conscience, and it ends every song.

Shades of meaning

  1. More divine than sorrow. “It is true also that joy is in its nature more divine than sorrow” (The Hope of the Gospel, “Sorrow the Pledge of Joy”). MacDonald borrows the phrase “the glad creator” against the “miserable, puritanical martinet of a God, caring not for righteousness, but for his rights” (Unspoken Sermons, “Justice”). See Father.

  2. The harmony of the spirit. “For what is joy but the harmony of the spirit! The good Father made his children to be joyful; only, ere they can enter into his joy, they must be like himself, ready to sacrifice joy to truth” (Unspoken Sermons, “Self-Denial”). See oneness · harmony and self.

  3. The joy of being. Those who serve God from duty, “and do not know him as their father, the joy of their being, they are slaves—good slaves, but slaves” (Unspoken Sermons, “Freedom”). “To our whole nature the life of the Father must be the joy of the child” (Unspoken Sermons, “Self-Denial”). See freedom and being.

  4. The joy of the Lord. “The joy of the Lord’s life, that which made it life to him, was the Father; of him he was always thinking, to him he was always turning” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Way”). Under his yoke a man learns “to walk step for step with him, drawing, drawing the cart laden with the will of the father of both, and rejoicing with the joy of Jesus” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Yoke of Jesus”). See yoke · burden · rest.

  5. Essential joy. “Less than God just as he is will not comfort men for the essential sorrow of their existence. Only God the gift can turn that sorrow into essential joy” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Cause of Spiritual Stupidity”). “If the joy that alone makes life worth living, the joy that God is such as Christ, be a true thing in my heart, how can I but believe in the atonement of Jesus Christ?” (Unspoken Sermons, “Justice”). See essential.

  6. Not life itself. “Many doubtless mistake the joy of life for life itself; and, longing after the joy, languish with a thirst at once poor and inextinguishable; but even that thirst points to the one spring” (Unspoken Sermons, “Life”). No promise of joy “is an appeal to selfishness” (Unspoken Sermons, “Self-Denial”). See life.

  7. Abroad in the All. The true man’s life “is not in knowing that he lives, but in loving all forms of life”. “The essential joy of his life lies abroad in the liberty of the All” (Unspoken Sermons, “Love Thy Neighbour”). “Let the joy of thy hope stream forth upon thy neighbours” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Salt and the Light of the World”). See neighbour.

  8. The joy of labour and of confession. “The joy of health is labour. He who is restored must be fellow-worker with God” (Miracles, “The Cure of Simon’s Wife’s Mother”). It is “the joy of any man who hates the wrong the more because he has done it” to confess it (Unspoken Sermons, “The Inheritance”). The peacemakers “share in the joy of God, in the divine fruition of victorious endeavour” (The Hope of the Gospel, “God’s Family”). See repentance.

  9. The tears of the gladness of the universe. In the inheritance there will be no room for religion as a separate thing, “where every song-throat is eager with thanksgiving! where such a tumult of glad waters is for ever bursting from beneath the throne of God, the tears of the gladness of the universe!” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Inheritance”). See inheritance and heaven.

Neighbouring words

  • gladness, delight (its near synonyms). Senses 1 and 4.
  • harmony (what it is). Sense 2.
  • life, being (its ground). Senses 3 and 6.
  • pleasure, happiness (lesser goods). Pleasures centred in the senses are a treasure on earth (Unspoken Sermons, “The Heart with the Treasure”).
  • sorrow (its pledge). See suffering · sorrow.
  • glory (its outward shining). “The glory of existence is to take up its burden, and exist for Existence eternal and supreme” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Yoke of Jesus”).

Scripture. John 15:11, that my joy might remain in you, and that your joy might be full (sense 4); Matthew 25:21, enter thou into the joy of thy lord (sense 2); Hebrews 12:2, who for the joy that was set before him endured the cross (sense 4); Nehemiah 8:10, the joy of the LORD is your strength; John 16:20, your sorrow shall be turned into joy (see suffering, sense 9).
