A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

church

churches · church-going · the true church

Frequency in the corpus: church and churches in about 35 paragraphs of the non-fiction.

In brief. MacDonald’s church is first of all the people the Lord heals and claims: its foundation was laid in his miracles, it is the purer family within the larger, and it grows like leaven. The church as an institution gets hard words. It is not the place of divine service, which is done in the world, in serving the neighbour. Its great heresy is to put something other than obedience in the place of faith, and to disbelieve in the Spirit’s teaching of each man. Its main fault is separation: those who tear the body of Christ apart for the sake of uniformity, and those who care more for the church than for Christ.

Shades of meaning

  1. Founded in the healed. “In these miracles of healing our Lord must have laid one of the largest of the foundation-stones of his church. The healed knew him henceforth, not by comprehension, but with their whole being” (Miracles, “Miracles of Healing Unsolicited”). “Every man had a cure of his own; every woman had a cure of her own—all one and the same in principle, each individual in the application of the principle. This was the foundation of the true church” (Miracles, “Miracles of Healing Solicited by the Sufferers”). See miracle and individuality.

  2. The purer family. The church is that “whose idea is the purer family within the more mixed, ever growing as leaven within the meal by absorption, but which itself is, alas! not easily distinguishable from the world it would change” (The Hope of the Gospel, “God’s Family”). It is “one of the passing means” for bringing the world’s relation to God into consciousness (same). “The crowd must be dispersed that the church may be formed” (Miracles, “The Raising of the Dead”). See Father and neighbour.

  3. Not the place of divine service. “Church or chapel is not the place for divine service. It is a place of prayer, a place of praise, a place to feed upon good things, a place to learn of God, as what place is not? It is a place to look in the eyes of your neighbour, and love God along with him. But the world in which you move, the place of your living and loving and labour, not the church you go to on your holiday, is the place of divine service” (Unspoken Sermons, “Righteousness”). See neighbour and duty.

  4. Church-going. The heaven of the children is not “a continuous church-going” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Inheritance”). For the man who serves Mammon in the week, “His very church-going is but a further service of Mammon!” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Salt and the Light of the World”). “The man who goes to church every Sunday, and yet trembles before chance, is a Christian only because Christ has claimed him” (Miracles, “The Cure of Simon’s Wife’s Mother”). See having · possession.

  5. The heresy of the church. “It is the one terrible heresy of the church, that it has always been presenting something else than obedience as faith in Christ” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Truth in Jesus”). “The great heresy of the Church of the present day is unbelief in this Spirit. The mass of the Church does not believe that the Spirit has a revelation for every man individually” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Higher Faith”). “The root of every heresy popular in the church draws its nourishment merely and only from the soil of unbelief” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Voice of Job”). See obedience and Spirit.

  6. The church more than Christ. Many “seem capable of anything for the sake of the church or Christianity, except the one thing its Lord cares about—that they should do what he tells them!” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Displeasure of Jesus”). The disuniters of souls are “those powers of the church visible who care for canon and dogma more than for truth, and for the church more than for Christ; who take uniformity for unity” (The Hope of the Gospel, “God’s Family”). The world in the church accepted the “cruel martinet” of pagan Rome “as the portrait of its God!” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Mirrors of the Lord”). See opinion · doctrine.

  7. Separation the main fault. “The one main fault in the Christian Church is separation, repulsion, recoil between the component particles of the Lord’s body” (A Dish of Orts, “A Sermon”). “What have not the ‘good church-man’ and the ‘strong dissenter’ to answer for” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Salt and the Light of the World”), each hiding his light under the bushel of party spirit. The healed of the true church “will try to separate upon individual and unavoidable differences!” (Miracles, “Miracles of Healing Solicited by the Sufferers”). See oneness.

  8. No authority over belief. The babes “must yield no claim to authority over their belief, made by man or community, by church any more than by synagogue. That alone is for them to believe which the Lord reveals to their souls as true” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Yoke of Jesus”). Of a liberal churchman: “a man whom I knew and love, and hope ere long to meet where there are no churches” (Unspoken Sermons, “Righteousness”). See freedom and faith · trust.

Neighbouring words

  • chapel, dissenter (its rivals, no better). Senses 3 and 7.
  • body of Christ (what it should be). Sense 7.
  • family (its true idea). Sense 2.
  • heresy (its unbelief). Sense 5.
  • uniformity, unity (false and true union). Sense 6; see oneness.
  • divine service (what is done elsewhere). Sense 3.

In the fiction and poetry. From general knowledge rather than the corpus: the novels are full of ministers good and bad. Walton, the vicar in Annals of a Quiet Neighbourhood, and Wingfold, the curate in Thomas Wingfold, Curate, find the gospel for themselves inside the church. The Missionar Kirk of Alec Forbes and the grandmother’s Calvinism in Robert Falconer show its narrower side.

Scripture. Matthew 25:40, Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these (sense 3); Matthew 13:33, like unto leaven (sense 2); Matthew 5:15, a candle … under a bushel (sense 7); 1 Corinthians 1:10–13, Is Christ divided? (sense 7); John 4:21–24, neither in this mountain, nor yet at Jerusalem (sense 3); Matthew 7:21, Not every one that saith unto me, Lord, Lord (sense 5).
