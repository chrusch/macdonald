A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

know

knowledge

Frequency in the non-fiction: know and its forms 1,127; knowledge 129.

In brief. To know, for MacDonald, is above all to be acquainted with a person, as a friend is known, and in the highest case to know God. That knowledge is life itself. Knowledge in the sense of information, facts held in the mind, is real but of a lower order, and set up alone it becomes a rival to life.

Shades of meaning

  1. Knowing a person: acquaintance. The governing sense. MacDonald repeatedly glosses knowing by acquaintance and friendship: “You do not know what a man is, but you know how to make his acquaintance—perhaps even how to gain his friendship. Your brain does not know what purity is; your heart has some acquaintance with purity itself” (The Hope of the Gospel, “God’s Family”). So of God: “What have you to do with his merits? You have to know his being, himself” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Hardness of the Way”).

  2. Knowing God as eternal life. After John 17:3. “The knowledge of the living God is eternal life” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Hardness of the Way”). In Donal Grant the narrator draws out the whole of it: “To know God as the beginning and end, the root and cause, the giver, the enabler, the love and joy and perfect good, the present one existence in all things and degrees and conditions, is life” (Donal Grant, ch. I, “Foot-Faring”).

  3. Knowing as seeing and loving. Knowing a truth is not separate from seeing and loving it: “To see a truth, to know what it is, to understand it, and to love it, are all one” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Consuming Fire”). Its perfection is the beatific sense of see: “Nor shall we ever see, that is know God perfectly”; yet “we may know God as we never can know human being—as we never can know ourselves” (The Hope of the Gospel, “God’s Family”).

  4. Knowing by doing. The epistemological rule that ties know to obey. Asked how a man is to know that a thing is true, he answers: “By doing what you know to be true, and calling nothing true until you see it to be true” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Knowing of the Son”). Repentance is the start of it: “setting himself to do the truth, he is on the way to know all things. Real knowledge has begun to grow possible for him” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Remission of Sins”).

  5. Knowing through the Son. Knowledge of the Father is mediated by the elder brother, and received by the child: “The Son alone can reveal God; the child alone understand him” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Yoke of Jesus”).

  6. Knowledge from within. Knowledge of God opens a different kind of knowledge of everything else: “he who knows God, will find that knowledge open the door of his understanding to all things else. He will become able to behold them from within, instead of having to search wearily into them from without” (Unspoken Sermons, “Self-Denial”).

  7. Knowledge as information, and its limits. The lower sense, often with the Genesis image of the two trees: “The tree of knowledge will never prove to man the tree of life. There is no law says, Thou shalt know; a thousand laws cry out, Thou shalt do right” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Hope of the Universe”). Its moral danger is pride: “the puffing-up of knowledge” appears in a list of the forms of “Protean self-worship” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Cause of Spiritual Stupidity”).

  8. Knowing that one does not know. Ignorance held in trust is a form of faith: “the faith of ignorant but hoping children, who know that they do not know, and believe that thou knowest” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Higher Faith”). In the Diary of an Old Soul the same humility is addressed to God: “I cry to one whom I can never know, / Filling me with an infinite overflow” (Diary of an Old Soul, January). The line does not contradict sense 3; it denies only that God can be known as an object enclosed by the mind.

Neighbouring words

  • understand (consequence; rival). Usually the fruit of knowing, not its means; MacDonald also uses it of the mind’s comprehension of a thing, which may run ahead of obedience and so go wrong. See understand.
  • see, vision, behold (figure). The perceptual figure for knowing (sense 3). “Inward sight alone can convince of truth; signs and wonders never” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Temptation in the Wilderness”).
  • acquaintance, friendship (glosses). The words that fix sense 1, as in the “God’s Family” passage quoted there.
  • obey, do (condition). The condition of knowing (sense 4). See obedience.
  • fact, truth (the lower and the higher object). Knowledge of facts is “of no value” in itself; knowledge of the truth concerns the man: “The correct word would be a fact, not a truth” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Truth”). See truth.
  • think, imagine (lesser kinds). Lesser than knowing. “To think a thing is only to look at it in a glass; to know it as God would have us know it, and as we must know it to live, is to see it as we see love in a friend’s eyes” (Donal Grant, ch. I, “Foot-Faring”).
  • heart, brain, intellect (organs). The heart knows by acquaintance; the brain seeks definitions (sense 1), and “in seeking to know what it is, may even obstruct your heart” (The Hope of the Gospel, “God’s Family”).

Scripture. John 17:3, this is life eternal, that they might know thee the only true God, and Jesus Christ, whom thou hast sent (KJV), behind sense 2; Matthew 11:27, neither knoweth any man the Father, save the Son, and he to whomsoever the Son will reveal him, behind sense 5 and the whole of “The Yoke of Jesus”.
