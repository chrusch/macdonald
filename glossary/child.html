A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

child

son · sonship · childlike

Frequency in the non-fiction: child 477, children 335, son and sons 582, sonship 39, childhood 59, childlike and child-like 48; son or sons of God 88, child or children of God 45; adoption 31, most of them in “Abba, Father!”, where MacDonald argues against the word.

In brief. Every human being is God’s child by being made. To become a son or daughter is something more: to grow up into the Father’s likeness by willing it, as Jesus did. Sonship is therefore both the end of creation and the content of salvation. Childlike names something different again, not immaturity but a quality of the divine nature itself, which the child shows and the grown man must recover.

Shades of meaning

  1. Child by making. The given relation, which no sin undoes: God is “the father of Jesus and of us—of every possibility of our being” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Creation in Christ”). But it is only a beginning: “To be a child is not necessarily to be a son or daughter. The childship is the lower condition of the upward process towards the sonship” (Unspoken Sermons, “Abba, Father!”).

  2. Son by likeness. Sonship is a coming of age in which the child comes to mean what the Father means. “None but a child could become a son; the idea is—a spiritual coming of age” (Unspoken Sermons, “Abba, Father!”). “His children are not his real, true sons and daughters until they think like him, feel with him, judge as he judges, are at home with him, and without fear before him” (same paragraph).

  3. Son by will. No one is a son by necessity, not even Jesus. “He was not the son of God because he could not help it, but because he willed to be in himself the son that he was in the divine idea. So with us: we must be the sons we are” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Creation in Christ”). In the same paragraph Christ’s part is defined: “while God is the father of his children, Jesus is the father of their sonship”.

  4. Christ’s sonship. The Son is son in obeying: “He is the Son of the Father as the Son who obeys the Father” (Unspoken Sermons, “Self-Denial”). His truth is “the flower of his sonship which is his nature” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Truth”). In The Seaboard Parish this is said to need confidence rather than knowledge: “for the Divine Sonship on earth perfect knowledge was not necessary, only perfect confidence, absolute obedience, utter holiness” (The Seaboard Parish, ch. VII, “Another Sunday Evening”).

  5. Not slaves, not adopted. MacDonald rejects adoption as a translation of Paul’s word, since it makes God take in strangers: “It is the slave yet left in the sons and daughters of God that has betrayed them into even permitting the word adoption to mislead them!” (Unspoken Sermons, “Abba, Father!”). He calls the doctrine “merest poison to the child-heart”, and the alternative is plain: “he will have children, not slaves” (Unspoken Sermons, “Freedom”).

  6. Sonship in full. Sonship is received by degrees and completed only with the body and the creation. “So long as there is in us the least taint of distrust, the least lingering of hate or fear, we have not received the sonship” (Unspoken Sermons, “Abba, Father!”). The whole creation shares the movement: “As the whole creation passes on towards the sonship, death must ever be doing its sacred work about the lower regions” (Miracles, “Miracles of Destruction”).

  7. Childlike, and the childhood of God. In “The Child in the Midst” childlikeness is a divine attribute: “God is child-like” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Child in the Midst”); “Childhood belongs to the divine nature” (same sermon). It is not the same as being young: “it must be confessed that there are children who are not childlike” (same sermon). And it is a way of knowing: “Until doubt is impossible, we are lacking in the true, the childlike knowledge of God” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Voice of Job”).

In the fiction. Annals of a Quiet Neighbourhood puts senses 1, 2 and 7 together in two old men: “while the childlikeness of Weir was the childlikeness of a child, that of Old Rogers was the childlikeness of a man” (Annals of a Quiet Neighbourhood, ch. XXVIII, “Old Mrs Tomkins”).

Neighbouring words

  • Father, fatherhood (correlate). “The Fatherhood and the Sonship are one, save that the Fatherhood looks down lovingly, and the Sonship looks up lovingly” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Child in the Midst”). See Father.
  • obedience (its substance). Sense 4.
  • will (its means). Sense 3 and will.
  • slave, servant (opposite). Sense 5.
  • adoption (rejected rendering). Sense 5.
  • freedom (its consequence). “The Son makes free with sonship” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Mirrors of the Lord”).
  • heir, inheritance (its right). The obedient son becomes “a true son of the living God, and an heir of all he possesses” (Unspoken Sermons, “Life”).
  • brother, brethren (its corollary). The sons are brothers of the Son and of one another; see Father, sense on the family.

Scripture. Romans 8:15, 23 and Galatians 4:1–7, where the KJV and RV both have adoption and MacDonald wants sonship (sense 5); John 1:12, to them gave he power to become the sons of God; John 8:35, the servant abideth not in the house for ever: but the Son abideth ever; Matthew 18:3, Except ye be converted, and become as little children (sense 7).
