A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

obedience

obey · obedient

Frequency in the non-fiction: obedience 132, obey and its forms 117.

In brief. The doing of the will of the Father because it is his will. For MacDonald it is not one virtue among others but the hinge of everything else: the way a man becomes a son, the only way he comes to know God, and the form his faith takes. It is never obedience to men.

Shades of meaning

  1. The way to knowledge. The most frequent and the most characteristic use: obedience comes first and understanding follows it. “Obedience is the opener of eyes” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Way”). “He that will understand first will believe a lie—a lie from which obedience alone will at length deliver him” (Unspoken Sermons, “Justice”). “Upon obedience our energy must be spent; understanding will follow” (The Hope of the Gospel, “Salvation from Sin”).

  2. Faith in act. Obedience is what faith is when it is real, as against belief about Christ. “Faith in what he is, in what he says—a faith which can have no existence except in obedience—a faith which is obedience” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Truth in Jesus”). More bluntly: “Your faith is your obedience; your theory I know not what” (Unspoken Sermons, “Justice”). In the same sermon it is set against the church’s characteristic error: “It is the one terrible heresy of the church, that it has always been presenting something else than obedience as faith in Christ” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Truth in Jesus”).

  3. The son’s side of the divine life. Obedience belongs to God himself, as the Son’s answer to the Father. “Obedience, then, is as divine as Will, Service as divine as Rule” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Child in the Midst”). “The obedient Jesus is Jesus the Truth” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Truth”). Hence the phrase “God the ordaining, and God the obedient” (Unspoken Sermons, “Life”).

  4. The meeting of two wills. In “Life” obedience is the point at which the created will closes the circle with the creating will: “Obedience is the joining of the links of the eternal round. Obedience is but the other side of the creative will. Will is God’s will, obedience is man’s will; the two make one” (Unspoken Sermons, “Life”).

  5. Trying, not achievement. Obedience is measured by the direction of the will, not by success: “Obedience is not perfection, but trying” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Truth in Jesus”). It is therefore always concrete and always the next thing: “let it be at once, and in the very next thing that lies at the door of our conscience!” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Truth in Jesus”).

  6. Not submission to men. MacDonald fences the word off from ecclesiastical or social authority: “By obedience, I intend no kind of obedience to man, or submission to authority claimed by man or community of men. I mean obedience to the will of the Father, however revealed in our conscience” (The Hope of the Gospel, “Salvation from Sin”).

  7. The body and nature obeying. A weaker, literal sense, used of the healed body and of creation answering Christ: “the withered hand obeyed, and was no more withered” (Miracles, “Miracles of Healing Unsolicited”). It matters because MacDonald reads the obedience of things and the obedience of sons as one order.

In the fiction and poetry. Donal Grant gives the doctrine in a sentence: “Obedience is the road to all things—the only way in which to grow able to trust him. Love and faith and obedience are sides of the same prism” (Donal Grant, ch. XVIII, “A Clash”), and tells his pupil, “first of all, and before everything else, mind, Davie, OBEDIENCE!” (ch. XL, “A Religion-Lesson”). A roadside poem puts sense 1 in four lines: “Gather wouldst thou the perfect grains, / And Jesus fully understand? / Thou must obey him with huge pains, / And to God’s will be as Christ’s hand” (Poetical Works I, “Roadside Poems: After Thomas Kempis”).

Neighbouring words

  • understand, know (consequence). Obedience is prior to both, and the relation is causal, not merely sequential: “Had he done as the Master told him, he would soon have come to understand” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Way”). See know.
  • faith, trust, belief (equivalent; consequence; counterfeit). Faith is obedience (sense 2); trust grows out of it; belief in the sense of opinion is its counterfeit.
  • will (ground). Obedience is the human will freely choosing the divine (sense 4). See will.
  • freedom, liberty (consequence). Obedience is the condition of freedom, not its limit: “the creature’s liberty is what his obedience to the law of his existence, the will of his maker, effects for him” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Hope of the Universe”).
  • son, sonship, child (the relation obedience fulfils). Obedience is the act by which a child becomes a son: “by the obedience of a son, he receives into himself the very life of the Father” (Unspoken Sermons, “Life”).
  • opinion, theory, speculation, doctrine (opposites). The usual opposites: “if, instead of speculation, we gave ourselves to obedience, what a difference would soon be seen in the world!” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Word of Jesus on Prayer”).
  • conscience, duty (the place it is heard; its object). Conscience is where the will of the Father is heard (sense 6); duty is the thing it names to be done next.

Scripture. John 7:17 underlies sense 1 throughout: If any man will do his will, he shall know of the doctrine (KJV). MacDonald paraphrases it in “Light”: “He who is willing to do the will of the Father shall know the truth of the teaching of Jesus” (Unspoken Sermons, “Light”).
