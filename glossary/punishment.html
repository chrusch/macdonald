A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

punishment

punish · penalty · retribution

Frequency in the corpus: punish and punishment in about 70 paragraphs. The main source is the sermon “Justice”, with “Light”, “The Last Farthing” and “The Final Unmasking” in Unspoken Sermons and “Salvation from Sin”.

In brief. Punishment, for MacDonald, is never an offset to sin, and it makes no atonement for the wrong done. Suffering weighs nothing against sin. God is not bound to punish sin but to destroy it, and he punishes where punishment can help: to make the sinner know what he has done and to soften his heart. So all punishment is kindness. Mere retribution is human, indeed inhuman. The worst punishment is to let a man see what he is, and the moment that sight takes effect he has begun to be righteous. Jesus came to save his people from their sins, not from punishment.

Shades of meaning

  1. No offset to sin. “Punishment is nowise an offset to sin.” Of those who say they have sinned but have suffered: “Yes, verily, but what of that? What merit is there in it?” “Punishment, deserved suffering, is no equipoise to sin. It is no use laying it in the other scale. It will not move it a hair’s breadth” (Unspoken Sermons, “Justice”). “How could it make up to me for the stealing of my watch that the man was punished?” (Unspoken Sermons, “Justice”). See justice.

  2. Bound to destroy, not to punish. “Primarily, God is not bound to punish sin; he is bound to destroy sin” (Unspoken Sermons, “Justice”). “Punishment, I repeat, is not the thing required of God, but the absolute destruction of sin” (Unspoken Sermons, “Justice”). See sin and consuming fire.

  3. Not spared where it helps. “Where punishment can do anything to this end, where it can help the sinner to know what he has been guilty of, where it can soften his heart to see his pride and wrong and cruelty, justice requires that punishment shall not be spared” (Unspoken Sermons, “Justice”). “The more we believe in God, the surer we shall be that he will spare nothing that suffering can do to deliver his child from death” (same). “I believe and pray that he will give me what punishment I need to set me right, or keep me from going wrong” (Unspoken Sermons, “Justice”).

  4. All punishment is kindness. “Ignorance may be at once a punishment and a kindness: all punishment is kindness, and the best of which the man at the time is capable” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Last Farthing”). Those who will not do “are punished in having the way closed before them; they punish themselves; their own doing results as it cannot but result on them” (same). “If I am not honest enough, if the eye in me be not single enough to see thy light, thou wilt punish me, I thank thee” (Unspoken Sermons, “Light”). See mercy and stupidity.

  5. Three kinds. “There are three conceivable kinds of punishment—first, that of mere retribution, which I take to be entirely and only human—therefore, indeed, more properly inhuman”, second “that which works repentance; and third, that which refines and purifies, working for holiness” (Unspoken Sermons, “Light”). See humanity, sense 2.

  6. Punished until he yields. “He who refuses must be punished and punished—punished through all the ages—punished until he gives way, yields, and comes to the light” (Unspoken Sermons, “Light”). “Who knows but such sin may need for its cure the continuous punishment of an aeon?” (same). See hell.

  7. The sight of himself. “The severest punishment that can be inflicted upon the wrong-doer is simply to let him know what he is; for his nature is of God, and the deepest in him is the divine” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Final Unmasking”). “For the moment that the sole adequate punishment, a vision of himself, begins to take true effect upon the sinner, that moment the sinner has begun to grow a righteous man” (same). See consciousness and see · sight.

  8. Saved from sin, not from punishment. “Jesus did not die to save us from punishment; he was called Jesus because he should save his people from their sins” (Unspoken Sermons, “Justice”). From all our sins “Jesus was born to deliver us; not, primarily, or by itself, from the punishment of any of them. When all are gone, the holy punishment will have departed also” (The Hope of the Gospel, “Salvation from Sin”). To punish the innocent “and let the guilty go free is unjust! It wrongs the innocent, the guilty, and God himself” (Unspoken Sermons, “Justice”). See salvation.

Neighbouring words

  • retribution, revenge (its human corruption). Senses 5 and 7: “a satisfied revenge is an eternal impossibility” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Final Unmasking”).
  • penalty, desert (the legal frame). Sense 1.
  • suffering (its matter). Senses 1 and 3; see suffering · sorrow.
  • destruction of sin (its true aim). Sense 2.
  • forgiveness (its partner). See forgiveness, sense 7.
  • purification (its highest kind). Sense 5.

Scripture. Matthew 1:21, he shall save his people from their sins (sense 8); Matthew 5:25–26, till thou hast paid the uttermost farthing (sense 6); Hebrews 12:6–11, whom the Lord loveth he chasteneth (senses 3 and 4); Ezekiel 18:20, The son shall not bear the iniquity of the father (sense 8); Psalm 62:12, thou renderest to every man according to his work (sense 1); Malachi 3:2–3, like a refiner’s fire (sense 5).
