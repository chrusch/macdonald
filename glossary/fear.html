A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

fear

afraid · terror · dread

Frequency in the non-fiction: fear 148; fears 16; afraid 26; terror 51. The sermon “The Fear of God”, on Revelation 1:17–18, is the main source.

In brief. The dread a creature feels before a power it does not know, or knows only as a demand it cannot meet. MacDonald does not despise this fear. It is natural and, for the man far from God, reasonable. But it belongs to imperfect love and distance, and only love can cast it out. Its cure is not less of God but more: the nearer presence of the very Power that is feared.

Shades of meaning

  1. Natural and reasonable fear. In the man who does not know God, “fear towards God is as reasonable as it is natural, and serves powerfully towards the development of his true humanity” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Fear of God”). It should not be soothed away too early: “Where it is possible that fear should exist, it is well it should exist, cause continual uneasiness, and be cast out by nothing less than love” (same). It is “a bond, however poor, between that which is and that which creates” (same).

  2. The fear of what God will make of us. Men fear God because he will not leave them as they are: “They love their poor existence as it is; God loves it as it must be—and they fear him” (same). See being, sense 8.

  3. The fear of a God imagined like ourselves. The worst terror comes from a low idea of God: “those fear him most who most imagine him like their own evil selves, only beyond them in power, easily able to work his arbitrary will with them” (same). God does not argue it away: “He will not abolish their fear except with the truth of his own being” (same).

  4. Fear as a sign of imperfect love. Fear lasts as long as love is unfinished: “So long as love is imperfect, there is room for torment” (same). Even John’s fall at the feet of the risen Christ was “still and only the imperfection of the disciple, unfinished in faith, so unfinished in everything a man needs, that was the cause of his terror” (same). See love.

  5. Fear and distance. God’s fire burns only those who stand off: it “is a fire unlike its earthly symbol in this, that it is only at a distance it burns” (same). So: “Endless must be our terror, until we come heart to heart with the fire-core of the universe, the first and the last and the living one!” (same). See consuming fire.

  6. The cure is Power itself. The Lord’s “Fear not” teaches “that the cure for trembling is the presence of Power; that fear cannot stand before Strength” (same). The thing to be afraid of is “weakness, not strength” (same). The ground of fearlessness is God’s perfection: “The reason then for not fearing before God is, that he is all-glorious, all-perfect” (same).

  7. Who should fear. Fear of power is reasonable only for the man of ill will: “It is the man of evil, the man of self-seeking design, not he who would fain do right … who is reasonably afraid of power” (same). The sermon asks of those who trust that good is the one power: “what place can there be for fear?” (same).

In the fiction and poetry. The older Scots usage, to “fear God and keep his commandments”, survives as plain piety in the novels: the crofter Robert Grant had one anxiety, that his children “should be honest and honourable, fear God and keep his commandments” (Sir Gibbie, ch. XXII, “Refuge”).

Neighbouring words

  • love (what casts it out). Senses 1 and 4; see love.
  • terror, dread, trembling (its stronger forms). Senses 4 to 6.
  • distance (its condition). Sense 5.
  • power, strength (what it fears and what cures it). Sense 6.
  • conscience (what sharpens it). An unclear conscience turns anxiety to “terror” (sense 3); see conscience.
  • consuming fire, wrath (what is feared). See consuming fire and wrath.
  • reverence, worship (what it becomes). Fear ruled by servility makes “a worship without devotion” (same).

Scripture. Revelation 1:17–18, Fear not; I am the first and the last: I am he that liveth, and was dead, the text of the sermon; 1 John 4:18, There is no fear in love; but perfect love casteth out fear: because fear hath torment, behind senses 1 and 4; Ecclesiastes 12:13, Fear God, and keep his commandments.
