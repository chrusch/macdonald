A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

righteousness

righteous

Frequency in the non-fiction: righteousness 286; righteous 109.

In brief. Being right in oneself: the fairness of a heart that gives every one his due. MacDonald insists that righteousness is a real condition of character, the same in kind in God and in man, and never a legal status transferred from one person to another. The righteousness “which is of God by faith” (Unspoken Sermons, “Righteousness”) is God’s own righteousness worked out in a man by obedient contact with him. He reserves some of his sharpest language for the doctrine of imputed righteousness.

Shades of meaning

  1. Fairness. The plain definition: “Righteousness is just fairness—from God to man, from man to God and to man; it is giving every one his due—his large mighty due” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Last Farthing”). See justice, sense 1.

  2. A fact of character. “What, then, is the righteousness which is of God by faith? It is simply the thing that God wants every man to be, wrought out in him by constant obedient contact with God himself. It is not an attribute either of God or man, but a fact of character in God and in man” (Unspoken Sermons, “Righteousness”).

  3. Beyond law-keeping. Keeping every law would not be righteousness. “To be righteous is to be such a heart, soul, mind, and will, as, without regard to law, would recoil with horror from the lightest possible breach of any law” (Unspoken Sermons, “Righteousness”).

  4. Not imputed. The legal sense is rejected outright: “Pray God I have no righteousness imputed to me. Let me be regarded as the sinner I am; for nothing will serve my need but to be made a righteous man, one that will no more sin” (Unspoken Sermons, “Righteousness”). Elsewhere it is “that poorest of legal cobwebs spun by spiritual spiders” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Last Farthing”), and the saved soul “would rather sink into the flames of hell than steal into heaven and skulk there under the shadow of an imputed righteousness” (Unspoken Sermons, “Justice”).

  5. A road of growth. Faith starts a man on a road “on which he cannot stop, in which he must go on growing more and more righteous, discovering more and more what righteousness is, and more and more what is unrighteous in himself” (Unspoken Sermons, “Righteousness”). See faith, sense 5.

  6. Hunger for it. The beatitude is for the not-yet-righteous: what such men desire is “Righteousness itself, God’s righteousness, rightness in their own being, in heart and brain and hands” (The Hope of the Gospel, “God’s Family”). Its fulfilment is forgetfulness of it: “The thought of righteousness will vanish in the fact of righteousness” (same chapter).

  7. The kingdom itself. Righteousness is not a condition of entry but the substance of the kingdom: the Baptist “did not yet perceive that righteousness is the kingdom of heaven” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Remission of Sins”).

Neighbouring words

  • justice, fairness (near-synonym). Sense 1; see justice.
  • faith (what it is counted). Sense 5; see faith.
  • law (what it exceeds). Sense 3.
  • imputation, legal fiction (its counterfeit). Sense 4.
  • obedience (its means). Sense 2; see obedience.
  • purity (its fruit). “Filled with righteousness, they are pure; pure, they shall see God” (The Hope of the Gospel, “God’s Family”). See heart, sense 4.
  • kingdom (its realm). Sense 7.

Scripture. Philippians 3:9, not having mine own righteousness, which is of the law, but … the righteousness which is of God by faith, the text of “Righteousness”; Romans 4:3, it was counted unto him for righteousness; 2 Corinthians 5:21, that we might be made the righteousness of God in him, discussed in “Righteousness”; Matthew 5:6, Blessed are they which do hunger and thirst after righteousness.
