A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

forgiveness

forgive · unforgiving · remission

Frequency in the corpus: forgive, forgiven and forgiveness in about 85 paragraphs. The sermon “It Shall Not be Forgiven” is the main source, with “Light” and “Justice” in Unspoken Sermons and “Salvation from Sin” and “The Remission of Sins” in The Hope of the Gospel.

In brief. Forgiveness, for MacDonald, is more than the remission of penalties. It is the contact of God’s heart and ours in spite of the wrong between, and the destruction of that wrong. God forgives every day and passes by all he can. His love goes ahead of his forgiveness, and that forgiveness creates ours. But forgiveness needs the human side for its completion, so it cannot reach the one who chooses to stay in his sin. That is the unpardonable thing, and even it is not final. Forgiveness and punishment are not opposed; one may be the condition of the other. To refuse to forgive a brother is spiritual murder, worse than the act of a moment’s passion.

Shades of meaning

  1. More than remission of penalties. Of the man who says he forgives but cannot forget, and never wants the fellow in his sight again, MacDonald asks, “To what does such a forgiveness reach?” Only “to the remission or sending away of the penalties which the wronged believes he can claim from the wrong-doer” (Unspoken Sermons, “It Shall Not be Forgiven”). To find what divine forgiveness means, we begin with the human, “for, if there be any meaning in the Incarnation, it is through the Human that we must climb up to the Divine” (same). See Jesus · the Son.

  2. The contact of hearts. Forgiveness is “the perfecting of a work ever going on, as the contact of God’s heart and ours, in spite and in destruction of the intervening wrong” (Unspoken Sermons, “It Shall Not be Forgiven”). “God’s love is ever in front of his forgiveness. God’s love is the prime mover, ever seeking to perfect his forgiveness, which latter needs the human condition for its consummation” (same). “God loves where he cannot yet forgive” (same). See love and oneness.

  3. Forgiving every day. “God is forgiving us every day—sending from between him and us our sins and their fogs and darkness. Witness the shining of his sun and the falling of his rain” (Unspoken Sermons, “It Shall Not be Forgiven”). He forgives us “ere we are, and that we may be, forgiven”, so that his forgiveness may “by causing our repentance destroy the wrong, and make us able even to forgive ourselves” (same). “God passes by all he can. He passes by and forgets a thousand sins, yea, tens of thousands, forgiving them all—only we must begin to be good, begin to do evil no more” (Unspoken Sermons, “Light”). See repentance.

  4. Divine forgiveness creates ours. “It is the Divine forgiveness that, originating itself, creates our forgiveness, and therefore can do so much more. It can take up all our wrongs, small and great, with their righteous attendance of griefs and sorrows, and carry them away from between our God and us” (Unspoken Sermons, “It Shall Not be Forgiven”). See Maker.

  5. Only come out. God speaks, as it were: “I forgive you everything. Not a word more shall be said about your sins—only come out of them; come out of the darkness of your exile; come into the light of your home, of your birthright, and do evil no more” (Unspoken Sermons, “Light”). The good news is “of forgiveness, not of vengeance; of deliverance, not of evil to come”; not for the worst sin that is past “needs he dread remaining unforgiven” (The Hope of the Gospel, “Salvation from Sin”). See salvation and light.

  6. What cannot be forgiven. “The one thing that cannot be forgiven is the sin of choosing to be evil, of refusing deliverance. It is impossible to forgive that sin. It would be to take part in it” (Unspoken Sermons, “Light”). Yet “never can it be forgiven until he repents”, and there may be “a whole age or era in the history of the universe during which his sin shall not be forgiven” (Unspoken Sermons, “Light”), but no fixed or final condition: “If you say it is a worse offence, I say, Is it too bad for the forgiveness of God?” (Unspoken Sermons, “It Shall Not be Forgiven”). See hell.

  7. Not opposed to punishment. “God does punish sin, but there is no opposition between punishment and forgiveness. The one may be essential to the possibility of the other” (Unspoken Sermons, “Justice”). A father may say “My boy, I forgive you; but I must punish you, for you have done the same thing several times, and I must make you remember” (Unspoken Sermons, “Light”). The atonement theories came of “incapacity to believe that it is God’s chosen nature to forgive” (Unspoken Sermons, “Justice”). See punishment and justice.

  8. Unforgiving as murder. “It may be an infinitely less evil to murder a man than to refuse to forgive him. The former may be the act of a moment of passion: the latter is the heart’s choice. It is spiritual murder, the worst, to hate” (Unspoken Sermons, “It Shall Not be Forgiven”). “For how are we to receive the forgiving presence while we shut out our brother from our portion of the universal forgiveness, the final restoration” (same)? We are to be “fountains of love and forgiving tenderness to all” (Unspoken Sermons, “Man’s Difficulty Concerning Prayer”). See neighbour.

  9. The heaven of forgiveness. There can be “no delight for created soul—short, that is, of being one with the Father—so deep as that of seeing the heaven of forgiveness open, and disclose the shining stair that leads to its own natural home” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Final Unmasking”). See joy.

Neighbouring words

  • remission, sending away (its root sense). Sense 1; MacDonald reads aphesis as “a sending away of sins” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Remission of Sins”).
  • pardon (its legal name). Sense 6.
  • repentance (its human condition). Senses 2 and 3; see repentance.
  • punishment (not its opposite). Sense 7; see punishment.
  • mercy (its source). See mercy.
  • forgetting (God’s passing by). Sense 3.

Scripture. Matthew 12:31–32, the blasphemy against the Holy Ghost shall not be forgiven (sense 6); Matthew 5:45, he maketh his sun to rise on the evil and on the good (sense 3); Matthew 6:12–15, forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors (sense 8); Luke 23:34, Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do (sense 7); Mark 1:4, the baptism of repentance for the remission of sins (neighbours); 1 John 1:9, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins (sense 7).
