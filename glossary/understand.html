A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

understand

understanding

Frequency in the non-fiction: understand and its forms 367; understood 67.

In brief. The grasp of a thing’s meaning, especially the meaning of God’s words and ways. MacDonald does not despise it: the whole of life is to be “a growth in understanding”. But it follows obedience and cannot replace it, and when a man insists on understanding first he misunderstands. Real understanding comes from within, from likeness to what is understood.

Shades of meaning

  1. The fruit of doing. The governing rule, and the counterpart of the same rule under obedience: “It was not for our understandings, but our will, that Christ came. He who does that which he sees, shall understand; he who is set upon understanding rather than doing, shall go on stumbling and mistaking and speaking foolishness” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Last Farthing”).

  2. A real good, never despised. MacDonald guards against being read as anti-intellectual: “God forbid I should seem to despise understanding. The New Testament is full of urgings to understand. Our whole life, to be life at all, must be a growth in understanding” (The Hope of the Gospel, “Salvation from Sin”).

  3. Understanding first: the error of the theologians. The wrong order is a moral fault, the mark of those “who would understand, and even explain, rather than obey” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Truth in Jesus”). It is pride: “He that will understand first will believe a lie” (Unspoken Sermons, “Justice”).

  4. Understanding by likeness, from within. A man understands God, or anything God has made, by what is akin to it in himself: “He can behold and understand God in the least degree, as well as in the greatest, only by the godlike within him” (A Dish of Orts, “Essays on Some of the Forms of Literature”). So too of nature: “When we are one with God we may well understand in an hour things that no man of science, prosecuting his investigations from the surface with all the aids that keenest human intellect can supply, would reach in the longest lifetime” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Voice of Job”).

  5. Understanding as loving and being at one. In the flower passage of “The Truth” the verb sits in a row of near-synonyms: “If we understand, if we are at one with, if we love the flower, we have that for which the science is there” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Truth”).

  6. The outside of things. A false understanding that stops at the surface: “When we understand the outside of things, we think we have them” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Cause of Spiritual Stupidity”).

  7. Understanding a speaker. Words are understood by knowing the one who speaks them: “Recognizing the mind whence the words proceed, you know the scale by which they are to be understood. So the words of God cannot mean just the same as the words of man” (Unspoken Sermons, “It Shall Not be Forgiven”).

  8. Understanding as recognition of truth. To understand a true saying is to see that it is true: “to understand what is true is to know it true” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Knowing of the Son”). Where he cannot see the mind of Christ in a reported saying, MacDonald concludes not that the saying is false but that he has not yet understood it.

  9. The faithful heart’s faculty. Understanding depends on the state of the heart more than the power of the mind: “A faithful heart alone is capable of understanding the proof of the truest things” (Miracles, “The Raising of the Dead”).

In the fiction. The rule of sense 1 is given to plain people. In Annals of a Quiet Neighbourhood old Rogers says: “To know about a thing is of no use, except you do it. Besides, as I found out when I went to sea, you never can know a thing till you do do it” (Annals of a Quiet Neighbourhood, ch. XI). In Mary Marston: “It is not even understanding the Lord Jesus that makes one a Christian” (Mary Marston, ch. XI).

Neighbouring words

  • obey, do (condition). See sense 1 and obedience.
  • know (near-synonym, usually prior). Knowing a person comes first; understanding his words follows. Sense 8 shows the two meeting. See know.
  • explain, interpret (counterfeits). “The greatest obscuration of the words of the Lord, as of all true teachers, comes from those who give themselves to interpret rather than do them” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Last Farthing”).
  • intellect (instrument, not source). The parables are unintelligible “to the mere intellect” (same paragraph, “The Last Farthing”).
  • imagination (sister faculty). New words are “born of the imagination and not of the understanding” (A Dish of Orts, “The Imagination: Its Functions and its Culture”). See imagination.
  • stupidity (opposite). Not ignorance but the will’s refusal to see; see the sermon “The Cause of Spiritual Stupidity”, whose text is How is it that ye do not understand? (Mark 8:21).
  • child (the one who understands). “The Son alone can reveal God; the child alone understand him” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Yoke of Jesus”).

Scripture. John 7:17, If any man will do his will, he shall know of the doctrine; Mark 8:21, How is it that ye do not understand?; Matthew 11:25, thou hast hid these things from the wise and prudent, and hast revealed them unto babes, which is the text of “The Yoke of Jesus” (all KJV).
