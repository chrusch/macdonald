A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

judgment

judge · condemnation · the judgment of God

Frequency in the corpus: judge, judgment and condemn in about 180 paragraphs. The main sources are “Justice”, “Light”, “The Final Unmasking” and “It Shall Not be Forgiven” in Unspoken Sermons, with “Salvation from Sin” in The Hope of the Gospel.

In brief. MacDonald’s God judges as a father, never as a judge who has put off his fatherhood. No man is condemned for any sin but one, the refusal to leave his sins and come out of them, and the condemnation is of those who see the light and will not come to it. The great judgment is an unmasking: everything covered will be revealed, and the true man welcomes it, even when it shows him wrong. Men are told to love and not judge, since love alone judges a neighbour rightly. Yet we must judge the things of God, humbly and lovingly, and refuse any reading of his words that makes him unlike himself. To be his child is to judge as he judges.

Shades of meaning

  1. Father, not judge. The theology MacDonald attacks pictures God as one who has resigned his fatherhood for the bench. “They think of the father of souls as if he had abdicated his fatherhood for their sins, and assumed the judge. If he put off his fatherhood, which he cannot do, for it is an eternal fact, he puts off with it all relation to us” (Unspoken Sermons, “Justice”). Nothing but his being our father gives him any right over us. Even Job, crying out against God, pleads with the Judge as a child: “How would there be any right for the judge of all the earth to do if he owed nothing?” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Voice of Job”). See Father and justice.

  2. Condemned for one sin only. “I believe that no man is ever condemned for any sin except one—that he will not leave his sins and come out of them, and be the child of him who is his father” (Unspoken Sermons, “Justice”). The past is not the ground of judgment: “Not for any or all of his sins that are past shall a man be condemned; not for the worst of them needs he dread remaining unforgiven. The sin he dwells in, the sin he will not come out of, is the sole ruin of a man” (The Hope of the Gospel, “Salvation from Sin”). See sin and forgiveness.

  3. The condemnation of light refused. On John 3:19 MacDonald insists that God gives every man time, and that not everyone who has not yet come to the light has turned his face from it. “The condemnation is of those who, having seen Jesus, refuse to come to him, or pretend to come to him but do not the things he says” (Unspoken Sermons, “Light”). He rejects the teaching that “I am judged with the same severity as if I had been born in righteousness” (Unspoken Sermons, “Light”), as if God would judge us by our sinfulness instead of our guilt. See light and hell.

  4. The final unmasking. Judgment is revelation. It is “an unalterable principle in the government of the universe, that all such things as the unrighteous desire to conceal, and such things as it is a pain to the righteous to have concealed, shall come out into the light” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Final Unmasking”). The question is whether a man wants it. Those who wait for “the great judgment of God” to right their wrongs must ask, “will you welcome any discovery, even if it work for the excuse of others, that will make you more true, by revealing what in you was false?” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Final Unmasking”). If he can face the revelation of things hid, he need not be afraid. See truth and punishment.

  5. Love, and not judge. Between men, judgment belongs to love. “What man can judge his neighbour aright save him whose love makes him refuse to judge him? Therefore are we told to love, and not judge. It is the sole justice of which we are capable” (Unspoken Sermons, “Love Thy Neighbour”). The judgment of men is to be lightly held: “Let us fall, as, thank him, we must, into his hands. Let him judge us. Posterity may be wiser than we; but posterity is not our judge” (Miracles, “The Casting Out of Devils”). The man who speaks the truth he sees may be “content to be condemned for it; comforted that if he mistake, the Lord himself will condemn him” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Knowing of the Son”). See neighbour and love.

  6. Bound to judge the things of God. Refusing to judge can itself be unrighteous judgment. To the objector who asks whether it is for us to judge the character of our Lord, MacDonald answers: “This very thing he requires of us.” And “if we do not judge—humbly and lovingly—who is to judge for us?” (Unspoken Sermons, “It Shall Not be Forgiven”). We are “equally bound to refuse any interpretation which seems to us unlike him, unworthy of him” (Unspoken Sermons, “It Shall Not be Forgiven”). But only obedience clears the eye: “Only eyes opened by the sun of righteousness, and made single by obedience, can judge even the poor moony pearl of formulated thought” (Unspoken Sermons, “Justice”). See obedience and intellect.

  7. Judging as he judges. The end of sonship is a shared judgment. God’s children “are not his real, true sons and daughters until they think like him, feel with him, judge as he judges, are at home with him, and without fear before him” (Unspoken Sermons, “Abba, Father!”). See child and oneness.

In the fiction and poetry. (General knowledge, not drawn from the corpus.) In Lilith judgment takes the form of the final unmasking: Lilith is made to see herself as she is, and that sight is her torment and the beginning of her surrender.

Neighbouring words

  • justice (the principle). Justice is fair play and the destruction of sin, not the infliction of penalty assigned to offence.
  • punishment (the means). Judgment that helps the sinner to see what he has done.
  • unmasking · revelation (the form). The great judgment is the uncovering of what is hid.
  • the last farthing (the debt). The prison whose doors open only when the debt of love is paid.
  • condemnation (the outcome refused). It falls only on the will that will not come out of its sin.

Scripture. This is the condemnation, that light is come into the world, and men loved darkness rather than light (John 3:19). Judge not, that ye be not judged (Matthew 7:1). Shall not the Judge of all the earth do right? (Genesis 18:25). Yea, and why even of yourselves judge ye not what is right? (Luke 12:57).
