A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

prayer

pray

Frequency in the non-fiction: prayer and prayers 343; pray and its forms 147.

In brief. The child’s asking of the Father, and the beginning of communion with him. In his two sermons on Luke 18:1 MacDonald argues that prayer is natural and reasonable. A God who could not hear would not be God. Our wants exist so that we will come to him, and the answer, which may look like refusal, is shaped by love and may take long. Prayer for others is a real power and a need of love.

Shades of meaning

  1. The beginning of communion. “Communion with God is the one need of the soul beyond all other need; prayer is the beginning of that communion, and some need is the motive of that prayer. Our wants are for the sake of our coming into communion with God” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Word of Jesus on Prayer”). The first thing to pray for is oneness with God: “to pray for this is the first thing” (same sermon).

  2. Heard, because God is God. “A being that could not hear or would not answer prayer, could not be God” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Word of Jesus on Prayer”). Prayer is natural to man: the good men find in it “indicates that prayer is natural, and how could it be natural if inconsistent with the very mode of our being?” (Unspoken Sermons, “Man’s Difficulty Concerning Prayer”).

  3. Not for its own sake. Prayer helps the one who prays, but that is not its reason: “Prayer does react in good upon the praying soul, irrespective of answer. But to pray for the sake of the prayer, and without regard to there being no one to hear, would to me indicate a nature not merely illogical but morally false” (Unspoken Sermons, “Man’s Difficulty Concerning Prayer”).

  4. The answer shaped by love. “But it may be that the answer to prayer will come in a shape that seems a refusal. It may come even in an increase of that from which we seek deliverance” (Unspoken Sermons, “Man’s Difficulty Concerning Prayer”). Good gifts take time: “the better the gift we pray for, the more time is necessary to its arrival” (same sermon). To be heard is enough: “it is little that I should go without what I pray for” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Word of Jesus on Prayer”). God may hear “the deeper prayer” beneath the spoken one (Miracles, “The Casting Out of Devils”).

  5. Prayer for others. “In praying for another we pray for ourselves—for the relief of the needs of our love” (Unspoken Sermons, “Man’s Difficulty Concerning Prayer”). It joins people in God: “if prayer is like a pulse that sets this atmosphere in motion, we must then by prayer come closer to each other than are the parts of our body by their complex nerve-telegraphy” (same sermon). It does not stop at death: “Death is not an impervious wall; through it, beyond it, go the prayers” (same paragraph).

  6. Boldness. Prayer flows from belief in God’s willingness: “when prayer is dull, what makes it flow like the thought that God is waiting to give, wants to give us everything!” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Word of Jesus on Prayer”). “The true son-faith is that which comes with boldness” (same sermon). See faith.

Neighbouring words

  • communion (its end). Sense 1.
  • need, want (its motive). Sense 1.
  • answer, refusal (its result). Sense 4.
  • love (what shapes the answer). Sense 4. Love “which will yield to prayer is imperfect and poor” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Consuming Fire”). See love, sense 2.
  • Father, child (its relation). See Father.
  • faith, trust (its condition). Sense 6.
  • will of God (its accompaniment). Every request should carry “the low accompaniment, ’Thy will be done” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Word of Jesus on Prayer”).

Scripture. Luke 18:1, men ought always to pray, and not to faint, the text of both prayer sermons; Hebrews 4:16, Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, quoted in sense 6; Matthew 7:9–11, what man is there of you, whom if his son ask bread, will he give him a stone?; Matthew 6:10, Thy will be done.
