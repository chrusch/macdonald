A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

heaven

kingdom of heaven · heavenly

Frequency in the non-fiction: heaven, heavens and heavenly 321; kingdom of heaven 53. Mostly in the phrases Father in heaven, treasure in heaven, kingdom of heaven. The fullest passages are in “The Inheritance” and “The Hardness of the Way”.

In brief. MacDonald is impatient with the heaven of popular notion, a place of rest or endless church-going where friends may not know one another. Heaven is first a condition, not a place: continuous touch with God, a loving of all and a forgetting of self, the heaven of the Father’s peace. No spot in space is worth anything to one who remains as he is. The kingdom of heaven is the rule of love and service, entered by becoming a child and hard to enter for anyone who trusts in riches; it comes slowly, by obedience.

Shades of meaning

  1. Touch with God. “Heaven will be continuous touch with God. The very sense of being will in itself be bliss” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Inheritance”). That bliss will need “the mitigation of the loftiest joys of communion with our blessed fellows; the mitigation of art in every shape” (same), and of service to those who do not yet know their Father. See being and joy.

  2. Not a place to win. “If he knows the Lord, he will not trouble himself about heaven; if he does not know him, he will not be drawn to him by it. I would not care to persuade the feeble Christian that heaven was a place worth going to; I would rather persuade him that no spot in space, no hour in eternity is worth anything to one who remains such as he is” (same). The unfit “would not even know you were in heaven if you were in it” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Hardness of the Way”). See see · vision.

  3. The heaven of Christ. “The heaven of Christ is a loving of all, a forgetting of self, a dwelling of each in all, and all in each” (Unspoken Sermons, “Self-Denial”). Jesus did not die “to provide a man with the wretched heaven he may invent for himself, or accept invented for him by others; he died to give him life, and bring him to the heaven of the Father’s peace” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Truth in Jesus”). See self · self-denial.

  4. Dismal notions. “Children fear heaven, because of the dismal notions the unchildlike give them of it” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Inheritance”), such as the dreary fancy of continuous church-going. Of those who doubt they will know their friends: “The nirvana is grandeur contrasted with their heaven” (same). “Little would any promise of heaven be to me if I might not hope to say” sorry to those he has wronged (same). See oneness · harmony, sense 4.

  5. No heaven with hell in it. “There is no heaven with a little of hell in it—no plan to retain this or that of the devil in our hearts or our pockets. Out Satan must go, every hair and feather!” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Last Farthing”). See hell.

  6. The kingdom of heaven: rule of service. “What is the kingdom of Christ? A rule of love, of truth—a rule of service. The king is the chief servant in it” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Child in the Midst”). “It is like king like subject in the kingdom of heaven. No rule of force, as of one kind over another kind. It is the rule of kind, of nature, of deepest nature—of God” (same). “A man content to bear no witness to the truth is not in the kingdom of heaven” (Unspoken Sermons, “Kingship”). See king · kingship and child.

  7. Hard to enter. “It always was, always will be, hard to enter into the kingdom of heaven. It is hard even to believe that one must be born from above—must pass into a new and unknown consciousness” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Hardness of the Way”). “None can know how difficult it is to enter into the kingdom of heaven, but those who have tried—tried hard, and have not ceased to try” (same). See having · possession.

  8. Coming slowly. No power delegated to the Son “could have wrought the kingdom of heaven in one corner of our earth. Nothing but the obedience of the Son, the obedience unto the death” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Temptation in the Wilderness”). Our poor idea of Jesus “thwarts his progress to victory, delays the coming of the kingdom of heaven” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Displeasure of Jesus”). See growth, sense 8.

In the fiction and poetry. MacDonald’s fairy tales imagine heaven as the place behind the world: in At the Back of the North Wind the country is entered only through North Wind herself, and Diamond’s death is his going there. See art · fairytale.

Neighbouring words

  • Father in heaven (its commonest phrase). See Father.
  • kingdom, king (its rule). Senses 6 to 8; see king · kingship.
  • treasure in heaven (the rich young man’s offer). Sense 7.
  • inheritance, home (its other names). Senses 1 and 4; see inheritance.
  • touch, communion, peace (its content). Senses 1 and 3.
  • hell (its opposite). Sense 5.
  • heavenly things, earthly signs (its figures). “As the heavens are higher than the earth, so are the heavenly things higher than the earthly signs of them” (Unspoken Sermons, “Self-Denial”); see symbol.

Scripture. Matthew 18:3, Except ye be converted, and become as little children, ye shall not enter into the kingdom of heaven, the text of “The Child in the Midst” (sense 6); Matthew 19:23–24, the rich man and the needle’s eye (sense 7); Matthew 6:19–21, lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven, the text of “The Heart with the Treasure”; Psalm 73:25, Whom have I in heaven but thee?, quoted in “The Fear of God” (sense 1); Revelation 3:12, a pillar in the temple of my God (sense 4).
