A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

image of God

likeness · express image

Frequency in the corpus: image of God, his image, likeness and express image in about 45 paragraphs. The main sources are “Abba, Father!”, “The Voice of Job”, “Life” and “The Mirrors of the Lord” in Unspoken Sermons.

In brief. Man is made in the image of God, and for MacDonald this is the ground of everything else. It is why he is a child and not a machine, why the word of God can reach him, why he cannot live without God, and why God owes himself to his creature. The image may be defaced but never lost: what is spoiled is still God’s image, and that is why it can be evil and miserable. Christ is the express image of the Father, by which we, his imperfect images, learn to read him. The likeness is completed only by his indwelling, and every man is the image of God to every other.

Shades of meaning

  1. Child, not machine. If God were only a maker and not a father: “Then am I only a machine, and not a child—not a man! It is false to say I was created in his image!” (Unspoken Sermons, “Abba, Father!”). “I must be of a nature for the word of God to come to—yea, so far, of the divine nature, of the image of God!” (same). See child · sonship and Father.

  2. Defaced, not destroyed. “To be fit to receive his word implies being of his kind. No matter how his image may have been defaced in me: the thing defaced is his image, remains his defaced image—an image yet that can hear his word. What makes me evil and miserable is, that the thing spoiled in me is the image of the Perfect” (Unspoken Sermons, “Abba, Father!”). If man lost that image, then “did not a new process of creation begin from the point of that loss” (A Dish of Orts, “On Polish”)? See sin.

  3. Unable to live without him. “He that is made in the image of God must know him or be desolate: the child must have the Father!” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Word of Jesus on Prayer”). Man “is made in the image of God, and it is for him absolutely imperative that he should have in him the reality of which his being is the image” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Voice of Job”). See prayer.

  4. God owes himself. Job’s discovery is “the beginning of the greatest discovery of all—that God owes himself to the creature he has made in his image, for so he has made him incapable of living without him. This, his creatures’ highest claim upon him, is his divinest gift to them” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Voice of Job”). See justice.

  5. Made to share his life. “For he is in the image of God, intended to partake of the life of the most high, to be alive as he is alive” (Unspoken Sermons, “Life”). “There is nothing in the man made in the image of God alien from the most glorious of heavenly shows” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Fear of God”). “Body and soul are in the image of God” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Hope of the Universe”). See life.

  6. The express image. Christ “is the express image of the Father, by which we, his imperfect images, are to read and understand him: imperfect, we have yet perfection enough to spell towards the perfect” (Unspoken Sermons, “Abba, Father!”). “Jesus is the express image of God’s substance, and in him we know the heart of God” (Miracles, “The Raising of the Dead”). See Jesus · the Son.

  7. Wrought out by indwelling. “He will work until the same likeness is wrought out and perfected in us, the image, namely, of the humanity of God, in which image we were made at first, but which could never be developed in us except by the indwelling of the perfect likeness” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Mirrors of the Lord”). “When we receive his image into our spiritual mirror, he enters with it” (same). “Only the man in whom the image of God is perfected can live perfectly” (Unspoken Sermons, “Righteousness”). See growth.

  8. Each to each. “Every man is the image of God to every man, and in proportion as we love him, we shall know the sacred fact” (Unspoken Sermons, “Life”). “Surely then, inasmuch as man is made in the image of God nothing less than a love in the image of God’s love, all-embracing, quietly excusing, heartily commending, can constitute the blessedness of man” (A Dish of Orts, “Browning’s ‘Christmas Eve’”). See neighbour.

  9. Thinking after God. “But as man is made in God’s image, he may think about God’s work, and dim analogies may arise out of the depth of his nature which have some resemblance to the way in which God works” (Miracles, “The Government of Nature”). On the mount, “when man who is the image of God is filled with the presence of the eternal, he too, in virtue of his divine nature thus for the moment ripened to glory, radiates light from his very person” (Miracles, “The Transfiguration”). See imagination and Maker.

Neighbouring words

  • child, son (what the image makes him). Sense 1; see child · sonship.
  • divine nature (its content). Senses 1 and 9.
  • likeness (its completion). Sense 7.
  • express image (Christ as the perfect one). Sense 6.
  • humanity (the image as possessed). See humanity; Christ is the image of the humanity of God.
  • mirror (the way it is received). Sense 7; see symbol, sense 4.

Scripture. Genesis 1:26–27, Let us make man in our image, after our likeness (senses 1 and 5); Hebrews 1:3, the express image of his person (sense 6); 2 Corinthians 3:18, are changed into the same image from glory to glory (sense 7); John 10:34–35, he called them gods, unto whom the word of God came (sense 1); Acts 17:28–29, we are also his offspring (sense 9); Colossians 3:10, renewed in knowledge after the image of him that created him (sense 2).
