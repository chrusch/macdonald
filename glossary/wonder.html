A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

wonder

wonders · marvel

Frequency in the non-fiction: wonder and its forms 136, many as a verb of doubt; marvel and marvellous 41. Mostly in The Miracles of Our Lord, “The Temptation in the Wilderness” and A Dish of Orts.

In brief. MacDonald distinguishes the common wonders of God’s ordinary ways from the marvels men crave. The wonder of the growing corn is greater than the miracle of the loaves, and God is more honoured in common wonders than in miracles. A mere marvel is soon forgotten and cannot produce faith; signs and wonders only nourish a faith already there. True wonder, childlike and attendant on imagination, is the seed of knowledge, and the right response to God’s works, to truth once seen, and to the love of the Father and the Son.

Shades of meaning

  1. Common wonders. Jesus would honour his Father only as his Father chose, “who is far more honoured in the ordinary way of common wonders, than in the extraordinary way of miracles” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Temptation in the Wilderness”). “The wonder of the growing corn is to me greater than the wonder of feeding the thousands” (same). God is for ever doing his work “more widely, more slowly, and with more detail both of fundamental wonder and of circumstantial loveliness” (Miracles, “The Government of Nature”). See miracle.

  2. Grown stupid to wonder. “Much that was once scarce credible is now so ordinary that men have grown stupid to the wonder inherent in it” (A Dish of Orts, “A Sketch of Individual Development”). To how many more God “has given the marvel of vision than those blind whom the Lord enlightened!” (Miracles, “Miracles of Healing Unsolicited”). See stupidity.

  3. The mere marvel. “A mere marvel is practically soon forgotten, and long before it is forgotten, many minds have begun to doubt the senses, their own even, which communicated it” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Temptation in the Wilderness”). The disciples “were tenacious enough of the marvel” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Cause of Spiritual Stupidity”) but missed its revelation of God’s love. Too many miracles would let men “feast their eyes, and then cease to wonder or think” (Miracles, “The Raising of the Dead”).

  4. Signs and wonders cannot make faith. “Those who would not believe without signs and wonders, could never believe worthily with any number of them, and none should be given them!” (The Hope of the Gospel, “Jesus and his Fellow Townsmen”). To the nobleman’s need the Lord “did in this remark require of him a higher faith still” (Miracles, “Miracles Granted to the Prayer of Friends”). But an honest man meets a wonder rightly: the draught of fishes “wrought on the mind of Simon as every wonder tends to operate on the mind of an honest man: it brought his sinfulness before him” (Miracles, “The Government of Nature”). See faith.

  5. The seed of knowledge. Bacon “called wonder the seed of knowledge; but he would build nowhere but on the earth” (A Dish of Orts, “The History and Heroes of Medicine”). Wonder is “that faculty of the mind especially attendant on the child-like imagination” (A Dish of Orts, “The Imagination: Its Functions and its Culture”). See imagination and child.

  6. Wonder at truth seen. When a man at last sees a truth, “it is so plain that he wonders he could have lived without seeing it” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Consuming Fire”); he “wonders like a child that he cannot make other men see as he sees” (same). Blessed are they “to whom a wonder is not a fable, to whom a mystery is not a mockery” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Higher Faith”). See see · vision.

  7. Each a marvel to the other. In the communion of the saints “Each will behold in the other a marvel of revelation, a present son or daughter of the Most High, come forth from him to reveal him afresh” (Unspoken Sermons, “The New Name”). Of the love of the Father and the Son: “The more I regard it, the more I am lost in the wonder and glory of the thing” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Creation in Christ”). See name · new name and oneness · harmony.

  8. The babe to whom he reveals. Jesus says, in MacDonald’s paraphrase: “be his child like me; become a babe to whom he can reveal his wonders” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Yoke of Jesus”). God’s ways go “down into microscopic depths, as well as up into telescopic heights—and with more marvel, for there lie the beginnings of life” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Inheritance”). See yoke · burden · rest.

Neighbouring words

  • miracle, sign (the extraordinary wonder). Senses 1, 3 and 4; see miracle.
  • mystery, glory (its companions). Sense 6; see joy.
  • imagination, child (its faculty and type). Sense 5.
  • stupidity, dullness (its loss). Sense 2; see stupidity.
  • revelation (what it receives). Senses 7 and 8.
  • nature, the growing corn (its common object). Sense 1.

Scripture. John 4:48, Except ye see signs and wonders, ye will not believe (sense 4); Matthew 11:25, thou hast hid these things from the wise and prudent, and hast revealed them unto babes (sense 8); Psalm 107:24, These see the works of the LORD, and his wonders in the deep, echoed in “The Knowing of the Son”; Luke 5:8, Depart from me; for I am a sinful man, O Lord (sense 4); Psalm 139:14, I am fearfully and wonderfully made.
