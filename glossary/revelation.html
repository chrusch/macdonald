A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

revelation

reveal

Frequency in the non-fiction: reveal, revealed, revelation and their forms 308. The main sources are “The Consuming Fire”, “The Higher Faith”, “The Truth”, “The New Name” and “The Yoke of Jesus”.

In brief. God’s showing of himself, which MacDonald finds everywhere: in the fire on Sinai, in every fact of nature, in each human soul, in the body, and above all in Jesus, who is the inexhaustible Revelation of God to which the Bible leads. Revelation is always partial, fitted to what the receiver can bear, and it never stops: the Spirit has a revelation for every man. It is not the same as representation, since what is revealed is seen and loved and cannot be refused. Its most terrible form is the revelation of a man to himself.

Shades of meaning

  1. A face, not a mask. What God shows of himself is true of him, even in the fire on the mountain: “It was a revelation of himself. He will not put on a mask. He puts on a face” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Consuming Fire”). See consuming fire.

  2. Always partial. “No revelation can be other than partial. If for true revelation a man must be told all the truth, then farewell to revelation; yea, farewell to the sonship” (same). A nation at its lowest could receive only “a partial revelation, a revelation of fear” (same), and a full one would overwhelm: “a full revelation would not only be no revelation, but the destruction of all revelation” (same). See fear.

  3. Jesus the Revelation; the Bible the way to him. “The Bible leads us to Jesus, the inexhaustible, the ever unfolding Revelation of God” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Higher Faith”). It “nowhere lays claim to be regarded as the Word, the Way, the Truth” (same). See Jesus and truth.

  4. Not closed with the Bible. The old man who rebukes the child “believes in no revelation but the Bible” (same). MacDonald protests that “The mass of the Church does not believe that the Spirit has a revelation for every man individually—a revelation as different from the revelation of the Bible, as the food in the moment of passing into living brain and nerve differs from the bread and meat” (same). See Spirit.

  5. Revealed to babes. “To reveal is immeasurably more than to represent; it is to present to the eyes that know the true when they see it” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Yoke of Jesus”). Revelation is the Father’s own act: “No wisdom of the wise can find out God; no words of the God-loving can reveal him” (same). See see · vision, sense 6, and child.

  6. Nature a revelation. “I believe that every fact in nature is a revelation of God, is there such as it is because God is such as he is; and I suspect that all its facts impress us so that we learn God unconsciously” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Truth”). It is things as experienced, not as analysed, that reveal: “For things as they are, not as science deals with them, are the revelation of God to his children” (same). See nature and truth.

  7. Each man a revelation. “For each has within him a secret of the Divinity; each is growing towards the revelation of that secret to himself, and so to the full reception, according to his measure, of the divine” (Unspoken Sermons, “The New Name”). Hence a man may look to his brother “for what revelation of God thou and no one else canst give” (same). See name · new name and individuality.

  8. The body a means of revelation. The body “is the means of Revelation to us, the camera in which God’s eternal shows are set forth” (Unspoken Sermons, “The God of the Living”). In the resurrection “We need not only a body to convey revelation to us, but a body to reveal us to others” (same). See death.

  9. A man revealed to himself. At the last unmasking, “The only terrible, or at least the supremely terrible revelation is that of a man to himself” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Final Unmasking”). See judgment and self.

Neighbouring words

  • represent (the lesser word). Sense 5.
  • see, vision (its reception). Sense 5; see see · vision.
  • Word (its person). Sense 3. MacDonald takes the word of God to mean “every revelation of Himself in the heart and consciousness of man” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Temptation in the Wilderness”).
  • Bible, Scripture (a witness to it, not the whole of it). Senses 3 and 4.
  • symbol, sign (its vehicles). Senses 1 and 2.
  • manifest, show (near synonyms). The redeemed body is to be “a fit house and revelation of the indwelling spirit” (Unspoken Sermons, “Abba, Father!”).
  • mystery, secret (what it unveils, never wholly). Senses 2 and 7.

Scripture. Matthew 11:25–27, thou hast hid these things from the wise and prudent, and hast revealed them unto babes … neither knoweth any man the Father, save the Son, and he to whomsoever the Son will reveal him, the text of “The Yoke of Jesus” (sense 5); Romans 8:19, the manifestation of the sons of God; Romans 1:20, the invisible things of him from the creation of the world are clearly seen (sense 6); Luke 12:2, there is nothing covered, that shall not be revealed, the text of “The Final Unmasking” (sense 9).
