A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

opinion

doctrine · theology · notion

Frequency in the non-fiction: opinion and opinions 115; notion and notions 100; doctrine and doctrines 54; theology, theologian and theological 28. The main sources are “Justice”, “The Truth in Jesus”, “Kingship” and “The Voice of Job”.

In brief. What men think and say about God, as against God himself and the doing of his will. MacDonald does not despise thought, but he insists that opinion is not truth and does not save, that the knowledge of Christ is higher than any theology, and that much accepted doctrine, chiefly of adoption, of substitution and of a God with several offices, misrepresents the Father. Theologians, interpreting instead of doing, have hidden the gospel. A right opinion grows out of an obedient life; it cannot produce one.

Shades of meaning

  1. Opinion is not truth. “But when I say truth, I do not mean opinion: to treat opinion as if that were truth, is grievously to wrong the truth” (Unspoken Sermons, “Kingship”). Men serve God, they suppose, “by condemning the opinions of their neighbours” (same). See truth.

  2. Opinion does not save. “Right opinion on questions the most momentous will deliver no man. Cure for any ill in me or about me there is none, but to become the son of God I was born to be” (Unspoken Sermons, “Abba, Father!”). Of the man who believes false things about God: “Opinion, right or wrong, will do nothing to save him” (Unspoken Sermons, “Justice”). See salvation.

  3. Opinion grows from obedience. The order is life first: “Trust in God. Obey the word—every word of the Master. That is faith; and so believing, your opinion will grow out of your true life, and be worthy of it” (same). Paul gives “widest conceivable scope” to “honest opinion, even in things of grandest import” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Truth in Jesus”), so long as a man walks by what he has seen. See obedience and faith.

  4. Theology that hides the gospel. “The greatest obscuration of the words of the Lord, as of all true teachers, comes from those who give themselves to interpret rather than do them. Theologians have done more to hide the gospel of Christ than any of its adversaries” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Last Farthing”). “It was not for our understandings, but our will, that Christ came” (same). God “is neither schoolman nor theologian, but our Father in heaven” (Unspoken Sermons, “It Shall Not be Forgiven”).

  5. Knowing Christ above theology. “To know a primrose is a higher thing than to know all the botany of it—just as to know Christ is an infinitely higher thing than to know all theology, all that is said about his person, or babbled about his work” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Voice of Job”). See know.

  6. Theology from below. The fault of the common theology is its source: “a theology constructed out of man’s dull possible, and not out of the being and deeds and words of Jesus Christ” (same). Hence “the depth of foolishness is reached by that theology which talks of God as if he held different offices, and differed in each” (Unspoken Sermons, “Justice”). Paul is no system-maker: “never in anything he wrote was it St. Paul’s intention to contribute towards a system of theology” (Unspoken Sermons, “Abba, Father!”). See justice.

  7. Hideous doctrines. Particular doctrines are named and refused: the doctrine of adoption, “Such a cold wind blowing at the very gate of heaven” (Unspoken Sermons, “Abba, Father!”); and “the doctrine of vicarious sacrifice” (Unspoken Sermons, “Justice”), which he casts to the winds without offering a theory in its place. Good men who hold such teaching do not live by it: “Those hideous doctrines are outside of him; he thinks they are inside, but no matter; they are not true, and they cannot really be inside any good man” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Truth in Jesus”). See Father and child · son · sonship.

  8. “Dangerous doctrine.” MacDonald expects the charge: “If any one say this is dangerous doctrine, I answer” (Unspoken Sermons, “Self-Denial”) that the law of God is enough for him, and he will have none of the laws invented by man. The same objection, “That is a dangerous doctrine!”, is an objector’s cry in “Justice” (Unspoken Sermons, “Justice”).

Neighbouring words

  • truth (what opinion is not). Sense 1; see truth.
  • know, understand (the real knowing). Sense 5; see know.
  • obedience, doing (what comes first). Senses 3 and 4.
  • system, theory, formula (its finished form). Senses 6 and 7. In “Justice” he speaks of “the poor moony pearl of formulated thought” (Unspoken Sermons, “Justice”).
  • the letter (its anchor). The theologian “holds by the mooring-ring of the letter, fast in the quay of what he calls theology” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Knowing of the Son”).
  • faith (its true counterpart). Sense 3.

Scripture. John 7:17, If any man will do his will, he shall know of the doctrine, whether it be of God (sense 3); Philippians 3:15–16, if in any thing ye be otherwise minded, God shall reveal even this unto you. Nevertheless, whereto we have already attained, let us walk by the same rule, cited in “The Truth in Jesus” (sense 3); Matthew 11:25, hid these things from the wise and prudent.
