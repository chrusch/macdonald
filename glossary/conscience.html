A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

conscience

Frequency in the non-fiction: conscience 72.

In brief. The inward witness to right and wrong, which MacDonald treats as the voice of God in a man and as his final court of appeal. It is the faculty Christ’s teaching is addressed to, more than the intellect. No authority, however good, may require a man to believe of God what his conscience calls evil. Conscience is also the point where obedience starts: it always tells a man what to do next.

Shades of meaning

  1. God’s candle in man. “If he is told by his conscience, which is the candle of God within him, that he must do a thing, why he must do it” (A Dish of Orts, “True Christian Ministering”). In “The Knowing of the Son” it is “the word of God in your conscience” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Knowing of the Son”).

  2. Its birth in the child. In “A Sketch of Individual Development” conscience wakes with the first opposition between mother and child: “This new something—we call it Conscience—sides with his mother, and causes its presence and judgment to be felt not only before but after the event” (A Dish of Orts, “A Sketch of Individual Development”). Before that, “Conscience is not yet awake, and there is no conflict” (same essay).

  3. The court of appeal on God. No teaching about God binds a man against it: “he is not a true man who accepts men against his own conscience of God” (Unspoken Sermons, “Justice”). “We are nowise bound to accept any man’s explanation of God’s ways and God’s doings, however good the man may be, if it do not commend itself to our conscience” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Truth in Jesus”). “Neither let thy cowardly conscience receive any word as light because another calls it light, while it looks to thee dark” (Unspoken Sermons, “Light”).

  4. What Christ speaks to. The parables “are addressed to the conscience and not to the intellect, to the will and not to the imagination” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Last Farthing”). They “reveal to the live conscience, otherwise not to the keenest intellect” (same sermon). See understand.

  5. The guide to the next step. Conscience never leaves a willing man without direction: “If this moment you determine to start for purity, your conscience will at once tell you where to begin. If you reply, ‘My conscience says nothing definite’; I answer, ’You are but playing with your conscience” (The Hope of the Gospel, “God’s Family”). Judgment will go by it: “Are you following your conscience? By that, and not by the interest you take or do not take in a thing, shall you be judged” (A Dish of Orts, “A Sketch of Individual Development”).

  6. The deepest individuality. Christ heals by rousing it: he would knit the bond with each man “by arousing the man’s individuality, which is, in deepest fact, his conscience” (Miracles, “Miracles of Healing Solicited by the Sufferers”). Society depends on it too: “A community is the true development of individual relations. Its very possibility lies in the conscience of its men and women” (same chapter).

  7. The imprisoned conscience. In the man who refuses the light, conscience becomes the wall of his prison, on which he reads “the words, awful and glorious, Our God is a consuming fire” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Child in the Midst”). See consuming fire.

Neighbouring words

  • will (its ally). When a man sees he may claim God’s help, “then his will immediately sides with his conscience” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Remission of Sins”). See will.
  • intellect (what Christ does not chiefly address). Sense 4.
  • obedience, duty (what it asks). Sense 5; see obedience and duty.
  • authority, tradition (what it may overrule). Sense 3.
  • heart (its partner). Christ’s saying appeals “to the conscience and heart, not to the understanding” (Unspoken Sermons, “Freedom”). See heart.
  • individuality (its depth). Sense 6.

Scripture. Proverbs 20:27, The spirit of man is the candle of the LORD, behind sense 1; Luke 12:57, why even of yourselves judge ye not what is right?, quoted in “Light”; Acts 24:16, a conscience void of offence, echoed in “The Final Unmasking”.
