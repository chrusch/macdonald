A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

intellect

reason · logic · reasonable

Frequency in the corpus: intellect and intellectual in about 80 paragraphs; logic, logical and reason as a faculty in about 60 more; reasonable in about 25.

In brief. MacDonald does not despise reason. He wants a sufficient reason for what he believes, calls the condition of the gospel absolutely reasonable, and grounds prayer in what is reasonable. But the intellect is not the highest thing in man: intellect, imagination and reason all stand below the will. The mere intellect cannot find out what was born of the heart. The parables, the answer to Job and the Lord’s sayings are addressed to the conscience and the will, not to the logical faculty. To reason from words one does not yet understand is to walk into the mire. Intellectual creeds held against the heart, and a vaunted intellect set against the brother, divide men. The intellect follows obedience; it does not lead.

Shades of meaning

  1. Below the will. “The highest in man is neither his intellect nor his imagination nor his reason; all are inferior to his will, and indeed, in a grand way, dependent upon it” (The Hope of the Gospel, “Salvation from Sin”). “It was not for our understandings, but our will, that Christ came” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Last Farthing”). See will.

  2. The mere intellect. “The mere intellect can never find out that which owes its being to the heart supreme. The relation of the intellect to that which is born of the heart is an unreal except it be a humble one” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Truth”). The parables are marked by “their unintelligibility to the mere intellect. They are addressed to the conscience and not to the intellect, to the will and not to the imagination” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Last Farthing”). See heart and conscience.

  3. Addressed to the man, not the logical faculty. God’s answer to Job “seems addressed to Job himself, not to his intellect; to the revealing, God-like imagination in the man, and to no logical faculty whatever” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Voice of Job”). In the wilderness the Lord “does not quote the scripture for logical purposes—to confute Satan intellectually, but as giving even Satan the reason of his conduct” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Temptation in the Wilderness”). See imagination.

  4. Imagination the safer guide. On the many mansions: “Man’s own imagination enlightened of the spirit of truth, and working with his experience and affections, was a far safer guide than his intellect with the best schooling which even our Lord could have given it” (Miracles, “The Raising of the Dead”). “Spend not your labour on the stony ground of their intellect, endeavouring to explain what purity is; give their imagination the one pure man” (The Hope of the Gospel, “God’s Family”). Yet a reader’s meaning in a tale “may be a higher operation of your intellect than the mere reading of mine out of it” (A Dish of Orts, “The Fantastic Imagination”).

  5. Reasoning from what is not understood. “If you wait, your ignorance will not hurt you; if you presume to reason from them, you are a blind man disputing of that you never saw. To reason from a thing not understood, is to walk straight into the mire” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Knowing of the Son”). See understanding and obedience.

  6. Intellectual creed against the heart. “Better to refuse even the truth for a time, than, by accepting into our intellectual creed that which our heart cannot receive, not seeing its real form, to introduce hesitation into our prayers, a jar into our praises, and a misery into our love” (Unspoken Sermons, “It Shall Not be Forgiven”). The Pharisaic friends of Job had “traditional theories which have served their poor turn, satisfied their feeble intellectual demands” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Voice of Job”). See opinion · doctrine.

  7. Vaunted intellect and fools of logic. “What, I return, has the man no hand to grasp, no eyes into which yours may gaze far deeper than your vaunted intellect can follow?” (A Dish of Orts, “A Sermon”). Self and pride make us “such fools of logic and temper that we lie in the prison-houses of our own fancies, ideas, and experiences” (same). Some would make a show “by wealth, by taste, by intellect” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Heart with the Treasure”). See church, sense 7.

  8. Reason honoured. “We ought, I grant, to accept nothing for which we cannot see the probability of some sufficient reason, but I thank God that this sufficient reason is not for me limited to the realm of experience” (Miracles, “The Government of Nature”). A law is not yet a truth “until we descry the reason of its existence, its relation to mind and intent” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Truth”). The healed demoniac had “reason once more supreme on the throne of his humanity” (Miracles, “The Casting Out of Devils”). See truth.

  9. The reasonable. “If it be reasonable for me to cry thus, if I cannot but cry, it is reasonable that God should hear, he cannot but hear. A being that could not hear or would not answer prayer, could not be God” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Word of Jesus on Prayer”). Obedience is “the absolutely reasonable and necessary condition” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Truth in Jesus”) of deliverance. Fear of God is “as reasonable as it is natural” in a man not yet satisfied with himself (Unspoken Sermons, “The Fear of God”). See prayer and fear.

Neighbouring words

  • understanding (the intellect rightly used). See understand · understanding.
  • logic, argument (its instrument). Senses 3 and 7.
  • imagination (its superior partner). Senses 3 and 4; see imagination.
  • will, conscience (what the Lord addresses). Senses 1 and 2.
  • theory, creed (its products). Sense 6.
  • science (its special field). See flower, senses 1 and 2.

Scripture. 1 Corinthians 1:19–25, the foolishness of God is wiser than men (senses 2 and 7); Matthew 11:25, hid these things from the wise and prudent, and hast revealed them unto babes (senses 2 and 5); John 7:17, If any man will do his will, he shall know of the doctrine (senses 1 and 5); Job 38–41, the answer from the whirlwind (sense 3); Romans 12:1, your reasonable service (sense 9); Isaiah 1:18, Come now, and let us reason together (sense 8).
