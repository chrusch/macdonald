A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

care

cares · anxiety · thought for the morrow

Frequency in the corpus: care in its anxious sense and anxiety in about 60 paragraphs. The sermon “The Cause of Spiritual Stupidity” is the main source.

In brief. Care, in MacDonald, is anxious fretting over provision: thought for the morrow, or for the next minute, which is equally beyond our reach and equally in God’s hands. It is distrust in its daily form, and distrust is atheism. Care fills the mind so that it cannot understand the Lord, as the disciples could not, worrying about one loaf after two miracles of bread. Only forethought that turns into present duty is right. Care is not a better spur to work than the call of God, and every care should be taken straight to him.

Shades of meaning

  1. The next minute as much as the morrow. “The care of the disciples was care for the day, not for the morrow; the word morrow must stand for any and every point of the future. The next hour, the next moment, is as much beyond our grasp and as much in God’s care, as that a hundred years away” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Cause of Spiritual Stupidity”). “In neither can we do anything, in both God is doing everything” (same).

  2. Care blinds understanding. “Their minds were so full of care about the day’s bread, that they could not think with simplicity about anything else; the mere mention of leaven threw them floundering afresh in the bog of their unbelief” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Cause of Spiritual Stupidity”). “He who trusts can understand; he whose mind is set at ease can discover a reason” (same). See stupidity.

  3. They remembered the loaves but forgot the Father. The miracles of feeding “revealed the loving care without which he would not be God”, yet “they remembered the loaves but forgot the Father” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Cause of Spiritual Stupidity”). “They knew the number of the men each time, the number of the loaves each time”, but “forgot the Love that had so broken the bread that its remnants twenty times outweighed its loaves” (same). See miracle.

  4. Distrust is atheism. “Distrust is atheism, and the barrier to all growth” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Cause of Spiritual Stupidity”). “Full of care, as if he had none, we think this and that escapes his notice” (same). “To-morrow makes to-day’s whole head sick, its whole heart faint” (same). See faith · trust.

  5. Things, had or not had. “In the former case it was the possession of wealth, in the latter the not having more than a loaf, that rendered incapable of receiving the word of the Lord: the evil principle was precisely the same. If it be Things that slay you, what matter whether things you have, or things you have not?” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Cause of Spiritual Stupidity”). See having · possession.

  6. Forethought that becomes duty. “If a man lay himself out to do the immediate duty of the moment, wonderfully little forethought, I suspect, will be found needful. That forethought only is right which has to determine duty, and pass into action” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Cause of Spiritual Stupidity”). “Work done is of more consequence for the future than the foresight of an archangel” (same). See duty.

  7. Not the spur of good work. He asks what work it can be “which will be better done by the greedy or anxious than by the free, fearless soul? Can care be a better inspirer of labour than the sending of God?” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Cause of Spiritual Stupidity”). See freedom.

  8. The rust of cares. “If God sees that heart corroded with the rust of cares, riddled into caverns and films by the worms of ambition and greed, then your heart is as God sees it” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Heart with the Treasure”). “The soul compact of harmonies has more life, a larger being, than the soul consumed of cares” (Unspoken Sermons, “Life”). See heart and life.

  9. Go to God with it. “With every haunting trouble then, great or small, the loss of thousands or the lack of a shilling, go to God, and appeal to him, the God of your life, to deliver you.” If you let thought for the morrow distress you, “you set open your windows to the mosquitoes of care, to drown with their buzzing the voice of the Eternal!” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Cause of Spiritual Stupidity”). Of the new name: “Gone then will be all anxiety as to what his neighbour may think about him” (Unspoken Sermons, “The New Name”). See prayer and name.

Neighbouring words

  • distrust, unbelief (its root). Sense 4.
  • forethought, prudence (its lawful form). Sense 6.
  • things (its object). Sense 5; see having · possession.
  • fear (its sharper kin). The vaguer God’s demands are felt, “the more room is there for anxiety” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Fear of God”); see fear.
  • care (of God) (its answer). Sense 3: the loving care without which he would not be God.
  • duty of the moment (its cure). Sense 6.

Scripture. Matthew 6:25–34, Take therefore no thought for the morrow (senses 1 and 6); Luke 12:29, neither be ye of doubtful mind (sense 3); Mark 8:14–21, the leaven of the Pharisees and the baskets (senses 2 and 3); 1 Peter 5:7, Casting all your care upon him; for he careth for you (sense 9); Matthew 13:22, the care of this world … choke the word (sense 8).
