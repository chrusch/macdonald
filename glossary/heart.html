A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

heart

hearts · pure in heart

Frequency in the non-fiction: heart 661; hearts 156.

In brief. The inmost self, the place where a man’s being springs from God. In MacDonald the heart is not mainly the seat of feeling. It is the deeper self that loves, that is shaped by what it loves, and that knows things the intellect cannot reach. It is also used of God: his heart is the source of all, and the heart in man is made after it. The heart becomes what it clings to, so the pure in heart see God and the heart that haunts earthly treasure rusts with it.

Shades of meaning

  1. The inmost self. “The Heart with the Treasure” asks whether the heart in the text means more than the bodily heart: “Does it not mean a deeper heart, the heart of your own self, not of your body?” It is “a heart which is the inmost chamber wherein springs the divine fountain of your being” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Heart with the Treasure”).

  2. Shaped by its treasure. The heart shares the fate of what it loves: “what is with the treasure must fare as the treasure; that the heart which haunts the treasure-house where the moth and rust corrupt, will be exposed to the same ravages as the treasure, will itself be rusted and moth-eaten” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Heart with the Treasure”). “Many a man, many a woman, fair and flourishing to see, is going about with a rusty moth-eaten heart within that form of strength or beauty” (same sermon). The sound heart is safe: “He whose heart is sound because it haunts the treasure-house of heaven may be tempted of the devil, but will be first led up of the Spirit into the wilderness” (same sermon).

  3. The heart that knows. The heart has acquaintance with things the brain can only define: “Your brain does not know what purity is; your heart has some acquaintance with purity itself” (The Hope of the Gospel, “God’s Family”). Christ is lord “of our intellects as well as of our more precious hearts” (Unspoken Sermons, “Justice”). A doctrine the heart cannot receive should not be taken in by the intellect: “Better to refuse even the truth for a time, than, by accepting into our intellectual creed that which our heart cannot receive, not seeing its real form, to introduce hesitation into our prayers” (Unspoken Sermons, “It Shall Not be Forgiven”). See know and understand.

  4. Pure in heart. The condition of seeing God. “None but the pure in heart see God; only the growing-pure hope to see him” (The Hope of the Gospel, “God’s Family”). Purity of heart is not a definition held in the mind: “even to know perfectly, were that possible, what purity of heart is, would not be to be pure in heart” (same chapter).

  5. The heart of God. The heart is first God’s. “The mere intellect can never find out that which owes its being to the heart supreme” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Truth”). It is tender and it is angry: “his heart is glad when thou dost arise” to go to the Father (Unspoken Sermons, “The Eloi”), and against the man who will not come to the light “his fatherly heart is moved with indignation” (Unspoken Sermons, “Light”). The Son is one with the Father in it: “the Father and he are one, have one thought, one mind, one heart” (Unspoken Sermons, “Freedom”).

  6. The heart awakened. Christ’s promise in “Freedom” is to wake a heart like his own: “I will wake in you the heart that my father put in you, the same kind of heart that I have, and it will grow to love the Father” (Unspoken Sermons, “Freedom”).

In the poetry. The Diary pictures the heart as the garden where God’s life grows: “Thou in my heart hast planted, gardener divine, / A scion of the tree of life” (Diary of an Old Soul, January).

Neighbouring words

  • self (near-equivalent). Sense 1; see self, sense 4.
  • intellect, brain (the lesser knower). Sense 3.
  • treasure (what forms it). Sense 2.
  • purity (its health). Sense 4.
  • will (its partner). The Word reveals “the vital relation of soul and body, heart and will” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Heart with the Treasure”). See will.
  • conscience (its witness). “If this moment you determine to start for purity, your conscience will at once tell you where to begin” (The Hope of the Gospel, “God’s Family”).
  • Father (whose heart it shares). Sense 5.

Scripture. Matthew 6:21, where your treasure is, there will your heart be also, the text of “The Heart with the Treasure”; Matthew 5:8, Blessed are the pure in heart: for they shall see God, the text of “God’s Family”; 1 Corinthians 2:9, neither have entered into the heart of man, quoted in “The Higher Faith”.
