A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

life

alive · eternal life

Frequency in the non-fiction: life 1,153; lives 323 (plural noun and verb); eternal life 55.

In brief. What God is and what he gives, of which all our needs are lack. In the sermon “Life”, on John 10:10, life is the only reality, death a mere negation, and the cure for every ill is “more life”. Real life is self-willed: in God it wills itself, and in man it begins when he wills to live by the life that made him. Eternal life is oneness with God, not endless duration.

Shades of meaning

  1. God himself. “God is life, and the will-source of life” (Unspoken Sermons, “Life”). Christ is “the life” in a deeper sense than he is light: “The life is Christ himself” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Creation in Christ”). See light, sense 5.

  2. More life. All need is need of life. “In a word, He came to supply all our lack—from the root outward; for what is it we need but more life?” (Unspoken Sermons, “Life”). “‘More life!’ is the unconscious prayer of all creation” (same sermon).

  3. The only reality. Death is not life’s equal: “Life is the only reality; what men call death is but a shadow—a word for that which cannot be—a negation” (Unspoken Sermons, “Life”). “We who are, have nothing to do with death; our relations are alone with life” (same sermon).

  4. Degrees of life. Some are more alive than others, according to what they live by: “the man who loves his fellow is infinitely more alive than he whose endeavour is to exalt himself above him” (Unspoken Sermons, “Life”).

  5. Willed life. Real life wills itself, and man must share in his own being. “He must live in and by willing to live” (Unspoken Sermons, “Life”). The consciousness of life is not the thing: “the consciousness of life is not life; it is only the outcome of life” (same paragraph). See will, sense 7.

  6. Eternal life. Oneness, not duration: “This life, this eternal life, consists for man in absolute oneness with God and all divine modes of being, oneness with every phase of right and harmony” (Unspoken Sermons, “Life”). It is reached through knowing God: “to know him is eternal life” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Truth in Jesus”).

  7. Life by giving it up. The way into life is Christ’s way of laying it down: “That way is, to give up our life. This is the one supreme action of life possible to us for the making of life in ourselves” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Creation in Christ”).

In the fiction and poetry. Lilith builds a whole house on sense 3 and sense 7, where to die is to come alive: “some have but just begun to come alive and die. Others had begun to die, that is to come alive, long before they came to us” (Lilith, ch. VII, “The Cemetery”), and “no one can die who does not long to live” (Lilith, ch. XLII, “I Sleep the Sleep”). The Diary prays the same: “Lord of essential life, help me to die” (Diary of an Old Soul, March).

Neighbouring words

  • death (negation, not opposite). Sense 3.
  • light (its outside). “But light is not enough; light is for the sake of life” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Creation in Christ”); see light, sense 5.
  • will (its act). Sense 5.
  • love (its content). Sense 6; see love.
  • consciousness (its outcome). Sense 5.
  • know (the way to it). Sense 6; see know.
  • sonship (its human form). By obedience a man “receives into himself the very life of the Father” (Unspoken Sermons, “Life”).

Scripture. John 10:10, I am come that they might have life, and that they might have it more abundantly (RV may have it abundantly, the form MacDonald quotes), the text of “Life”; John 17:3, this is life eternal, that they might know thee; John 1:4, In him was life; and the life was the light of men; Matthew 16:25 (sense 7).
