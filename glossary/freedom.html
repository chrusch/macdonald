A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

freedom

free · liberty

Frequency in the non-fiction: free 166; liberty 80; freedom 64.

In brief. The condition of a son in his Father’s house. MacDonald defines freedom against both licence and servitude: the man who does as he likes is a slave of sin, and the man who obeys God only from duty or fear is a slave too, though a good one. The only free man is the child who does the Father’s will because he loves it. Freedom comes by the truth, which is Christ, and by dying to self. It is a fullness of bonds of love, not an absence of bonds.

Shades of meaning

  1. Sons or slaves. The sermon “Freedom”, on John 8:32–36, sorts men by this distinction. “They must be sons or slaves. They cannot rid themselves of their owner” (Unspoken Sermons, “Freedom”). “The only free man, then, is he who is a child of the Father. He is a servant of all, but can be made the slave of none” (same sermon).

  2. The good slave. Obedience from duty alone is not yet freedom: “so long as they serve God, as they call it, from duty, and do not know him as their father, the joy of their being, they are slaves—good slaves, but slaves” (Unspoken Sermons, “Freedom”). Duty is a stage to pass through: men “must change the garments of their slavery for the robes of liberty” (Unspoken Sermons, “Abba, Father!”).

  3. Made free by the truth. Christ, the truth, frees by making men what he is: “I am the truth, and you shall be free as I am free. To be free, you must be sons like me. To be free you must be that which you have to be, that which you are created” (Unspoken Sermons, “Freedom”). The free man is free “in virtue of his truth” (same sermon). See truth and king, sense 3.

  4. Freedom by self-death. The way to freedom is Christ’s death to self: “He died that we might live—but live as he lives, by dying as he died who died to himself that he might live unto God” (Unspoken Sermons, “Freedom”). Christ is “the way from slavery, conscious or unconscious, into liberty” (Unspoken Sermons, “Self-Denial”).

  5. Not freedom from bonds. Liberty grows with obligation: “It is the vulgar, slavish, worldly idea of freedom, that it consists in being bound to nothing. Not such is God’s idea of liberty!” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Voice of Job”). The same paragraph argues that the more claims God lays on himself by creating, “the freer is he as creator”.

  6. The freedom of God. God is not bound by dead law: “See the freedom of God in his sunsets—never a second like one of the foregone!” (Unspoken Sermons, “Man’s Difficulty Concerning Prayer”).

  7. The freedom of creation. The liberty of the sons is the hope of all things: “then shall the creation, subjected to vanity for their sakes, find its freedom in their freedom, its gladness in their sonship” (Unspoken Sermons, “Abba, Father!”).

  8. Freedom of speech with God. Liberty also means boldness before God. “God speaks not a word of rebuke to Job for the freedom of his speech” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Voice of Job”); “he gives us liberty to speak, and will hear with absolute fairness” (same sermon).

Neighbouring words

  • slave, slavery, bondage (opposite). Senses 1 and 2.
  • son, child (its holder). Sense 1; see child · son · sonship.
  • duty (the stage before it). Sense 2.
  • truth (its cause). Sense 3; see truth.
  • free will (its inward form). See will, sense 6.
  • self (what must die for it). Sense 4; see self.
  • king (its dignity). Sense 3; see king.

Scripture. John 8:32, 36, the truth shall make you free … If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed, the text of “Freedom”; Galatians 5:1, the liberty wherewith Christ hath made us free, echoed in “Abba, Father!”; Romans 8:21, the glorious liberty of the children of God (RV the liberty of the glory of the children of God, the form MacDonald quotes in “Self-Denial”), sense 7.
