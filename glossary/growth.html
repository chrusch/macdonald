A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

growth

grow · growing

Frequency in the non-fiction: grow and its forms, with growth, 235. The main sources are “The Consuming Fire”, “Life”, “The New Name” and “A Sketch of Individual Development”.

In brief. MacDonald thinks of men as growing creatures. God regards them not merely as they are but as they are growing towards the image after which he made them, and so values the thousand imperfect stages of the way. Growth is the more and more life a Christian receives; it comes by obedience and trust, needs strife, and is God’s care rather than ours. Whatever stops it, a final revelation, a cramping creed, distrust, is against the divine order, which is founded for growth.

Shades of meaning

  1. Growing towards the image. God “regards men not as they are merely, but as they shall be; not as they shall be merely, but as they are now growing, or capable of growing, towards that image after which he made them that they might grow to it” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Consuming Fire”). So “a thousand stages, each in itself all but valueless, are of inestimable worth as the necessary and connected gradations of an infinite progress. A condition which of declension would indicate a devil, may of growth indicate a saint” (same). See image of God.

  2. More and more life. “All the growth of the Christian is the more and more life he is receiving” (Unspoken Sermons, “Life”). “Our souls shall be vessels ever growing, and ever as they grow, filled with the more and more life proceeding from the Father and the Son” (same). Only life can grow: “life alone can assimilate life, can change food into growth” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Cause of Spiritual Stupidity”). See life.

  3. Growing into the secret. “Each has within him a secret of the Divinity; each is growing towards the revelation of that secret to himself, and so to the full reception, according to his measure, of the divine” (Unspoken Sermons, “The New Name”). The sermon ends in prayer: “make our being grow into thy likeness. If through ages of strife and ages of growth, yet let us at last see thy face, and receive the white stone from thy hand” (same). See name · new name and individuality.

  4. By obedience, not by understanding first. “Our whole life, to be life at all, must be a growth in understanding. What I cry out upon is the misunderstanding that comes of man’s endeavour to understand while not obeying” (The Hope of the Gospel, “Salvation from Sin”). Walking by what we have seen is the condition: “In such walking, and in such walking only, love will grow, truth will grow” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Truth in Jesus”). See obedience.

  5. God’s care, not ours. “We are able to turn our faces to the light, and come out of the darkness; the Lord will see to our growth” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Displeasure of Jesus”). A man cannot “will my growth” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Word of Jesus on Prayer”) any more than he can rule his thoughts. See care · anxiety.

  6. Strife the condition. The child meets a will other than his own: “Thereupon begins the strife without which there never was, and, I presume, never can be, any growth, any progress” (A Dish of Orts, “A Sketch of Individual Development”). Doubts also serve: “a man may be haunted with doubts, and only grow thereby in faith” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Voice of Job”). See doubt and temptation.

  7. What stops growth. A revelation that would “preclude development and growth, thus chaining the man to its incompleteness” would be “but a false revelation fighting against all the divine laws of human existence” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Consuming Fire”). “Distrust is atheism, and the barrier to all growth” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Cause of Spiritual Stupidity”). Teachers who press their theories on others “do their unintentional worst to stop all growth, all life” (Unspoken Sermons, “Justice”). See revelation and opinion · doctrine · theology.

  8. An order founded for growth. God’s world is “the divine order of things, founded for growth and gradual betterment” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Right Hand and the Left”). The kingdom itself must grow: “by aeons of labour, must he grow into the hearts of the sons and daughters of his Father in heaven” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Temptation in the Wilderness”). “The wonder of the growing corn is to me greater than the wonder of feeding the thousands” (same). Destruction serves it, “for the inferior must ever be giving way for the growth of the superior” (Miracles, “Miracles of Destruction”). See miracle and nature.

Neighbouring words

  • life (what grows). Sense 2.
  • likeness, image (its goal). Senses 1 and 3.
  • progress, development, gradations (near synonyms). Senses 1 and 6.
  • child, childhood (its type). The father is pleased with the child’s “first tottering attempt” but wants “the manly step of the full-grown son” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Way”); see child.
  • obedience, trust (its conditions). Senses 4, 5 and 7.
  • strife, doubt (its occasions). Sense 6.
  • seed, root, blossom (its images). Senses 3 and 8.

Scripture. Mark 4:28, first the blade, then the ear, after that the full corn in the ear (sense 8); 2 Peter 3:18, grow in grace, and in the knowledge of our Lord (sense 4); Ephesians 4:13, 15, unto the measure of the stature of the fulness of Christ … may grow up into him in all things (sense 1); Philippians 3:13–16, whereto we have already attained, let us walk by the same rule, quoted in “The Truth in Jesus” (sense 4).
