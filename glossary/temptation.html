A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

temptation

tempt · tempter

Frequency in the non-fiction: tempt and its forms 131. The central source is “The Temptation in the Wilderness”, with “The Eloi” on the last temptation at the cross.

In brief. A real pull on the will toward something less than the will of God. MacDonald’s key claim is that the Son of God was tempted not with evil but with good, with inferior forms of good pressing in while the higher waited for God’s time; to yield would have been evil in him. The three temptations in the wilderness are the contest of seen and unseen, outer and inner, show and reality, and each is answered by the principle that the will of God is life. The last and fullest temptation comes in the darkness of the cross. Men must have temptation to rise above it, and must never tempt each other or God.

Shades of meaning

  1. Real, or nothing. “If any one say that he was not moved by those temptations, he must be told that then they were no temptations to him, and he was not tempted” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Temptation in the Wilderness”). An unfelt temptation would have been “a mere stage-show” (same), meaning nothing “For human need, struggle, and hope” (same).

  2. Tempted with good. “He was not tempted with Evil but with Good; with inferior forms of good, that is, pressing in upon him, while the higher forms of good held themselves aloof, biding their time, that is, God’s time” (same). “I do not believe that the Son of God could be tempted with evil, but I do believe that he could be tempted with good—to yield to which temptation would have been evil in him” (same). So “the devil tempted him to evil, but not with evil” (same). See sin · evil.

  3. The three temptations. “The first was to help himself in his need; the second, perhaps, to assert the Father; the third to deliver his brethren” (same). Taken together, “the whole Temptation may be regarded as the contest of the seen and the unseen, of the outer and inner, of the likely and the true, of the show and the reality” (same). The last lay in “a temptation to save his brethren, instead of doing the Will of his Father” (same).

  4. The answer: God’s will is life. Each reply rests on one principle, “that to the Son of God the will of God is Life” (same). Signs would only have forced belief: “Inward sight alone can convince of truth; signs and wonders never” (same). See will, life and see · vision, sense 5.

  5. The last temptation at the cross. “Without this last trial of all, the temptations of our Master had not been so full as the human cup could hold” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Eloi”). The temptations of the desert came “to the young, strong man with his road before him and the presence of his God around him” (same); the last came when that presence could not be felt. See see · vision, sense 8.

  6. Needed by men. At Cana, “Enforced virtue is unworthy of the name. That men may rise above temptation, it is needful that they should have temptation” (Miracles, “The Beginning of Miracles”). But “God chooses that men should be tried, but let a man beware of tempting his neighbour” (Miracles, “The Beginning of Miracles”). See freedom.

  7. Tempting God. Bunyan and other good people “have been tempted to tempt the Lord their God” on the strength of the promise about moving mountains, “just as Satan sought to tempt our Lord on the strength of the passage he quoted from the Psalms” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Temptation in the Wilderness”). Of prayer used as a test: “God waits to be gracious not to be tempted” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Word of Jesus on Prayer”). See prayer.

  8. Daily temptations. In “The Hands of the Father” the morning prayer meets “the business and turmoil of the day, where so many temptations may come to do less honourably, less faithfully, less kindly, less diligently than the Ideal Man would have me do” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Hands of the Father”). The end of salvation is a man raised “into such a condition that, be the temptation what it might, he would not yield” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Hardness of the Way”).

Neighbouring words

  • devil, tempter (its agent). Senses 2 and 3.
  • good, evil (its material and its end). Sense 2; see sin · evil.
  • trial (its divine use). Sense 6.
  • sign, wonder (the second temptation). Sense 4.
  • will of God (the answer to it). Sense 4; see will.
  • seen, unseen (the terms of the contest). Sense 3.
  • wilderness (its place). He who haunts the treasure-house of heaven “may be tempted of the devil, but will be first led up of the Spirit into the wilderness” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Heart with the Treasure”).

Scripture. Matthew 4:1–11, Then was Jesus led up of the Spirit into the wilderness to be tempted of the devil, the text of “The Temptation in the Wilderness”; Deuteronomy 8:3 and Matthew 4:4, man shall not live by bread alone (sense 4); Matthew 4:7, Thou shalt not tempt the Lord thy God (sense 7); Hebrews 4:15, in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin (senses 1 and 5); James 1:12–13, God cannot be tempted with evil (sense 2).
