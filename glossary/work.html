A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

work

works · labour · daily work

Frequency in the corpus: work and labour in about 400 paragraphs of the non-fiction, many in other senses (a work of art, the works of a poet). The main sources are Unspoken Sermons, especially “Righteousness”, “The Truth in Jesus” and “Kingship”, and Miracles of Our Lord.

In brief. For MacDonald, work is first God’s: the Father is always working, and Jesus did the works of the Father in little so that men might see his greater works in the corn and the vine. Men are to work as he works. The place of a man’s labour, not the church he attends on his holiday, is the place of divine service, and he is to bear witness in bank, farm, shop and workshop. MacDonald scorns the cry of “the doctrine of works” raised against doing what Jesus said. Faith saves, but a man who would be delivered from evil must himself begin to cast it out. Works done to earn God’s favour are dead works. Daily labour done well is an offering.

Shades of meaning

  1. The works of the Father. “He is the Son of God come to do the works of his Father” (Miracles, “The Cure of Simon’s Wife’s Mother”). His miracles were smaller than what they stood for: “his miracles in bread and in wine were far less grand and less beautiful than the works of the Father they represented, in making the corn to grow in the valleys, and the grapes to drink the sunlight on the hill-sides of the world” (Miracles, “Introduction”). “Jesus did in little the great work of the Father” (Miracles, “Miracles of Healing Unsolicited”). See miracle and Father.

  2. God’s hard labour. Making sons is a labour even for God: “Let no soul think that to say God undertook a hard labour in willing that many sons and daughters should be sharers of the divine nature, is to abate his glory! The greater the difficulty, the greater is the glory of him who does the thing he has undertaken” (Unspoken Sermons, “Life”). See Maker and child.

  3. The place of labour is the place of service. “The world in which you move, the place of your living and loving and labour, not the church you go to on your holiday, is the place of divine service. Serve your neighbour, and you serve him” (Unspoken Sermons, “Righteousness”). The question of witness is asked of every workplace: “Do we carry ourselves in bank, on farm, in house or shop, in study or chamber or workshop, as the Lord would, or as the Lord would not?” (Unspoken Sermons, “Kingship”). See church, neighbour and king.

  4. Not “the doctrine of works”. To those who dislike being told to do what Jesus said, the father of lies whispers that this “is the doctrine of works!” But “one word of the Lord humbly heard and received will suffice to send all the demons of false theology into the abyss” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Truth in Jesus”). It is common sense, MacDonald says, that “if a man would be delivered from the evil in him, he must himself begin to cast it out, himself begin to disobey it, and work righteousness” (The Hope of the Gospel, “Salvation from Sin”), and look for his Father’s help, since “alone, he might labour to all eternity and not succeed” (same). See obedience and faith.

  5. Dead works. “Our wrong deeds are our dead works; our evil thoughts are our live sins” (The Hope of the Gospel, “Salvation from Sin”). The resurrection Jesus taught was “the resurrection from dead works to serve the living and true God” (Miracles, “The Raising of the Dead”). See sin and life.

  6. The work of the moment. Work belongs to the present: “the moment which coincides with work to be done, is the moment to be minded; the next is nowhere till God has made it” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Cause of Spiritual Stupidity”). And labour continues in heaven: the bliss of the blessed is right “of which a great part consists in labour that others may share it” (Unspoken Sermons, “Self-Denial”). See care and duty.

In the fiction and poetry. In “Within and Without” daily labour is an offering: “I must go forth and do my daily work. / I thank thee, God, that it is hard sometimes / To do my daily labour” (Poetical Works I, “Within and Without”, Part III), and the speaker tries “to do / My day’s work well to thee,—my offering” (same). In The Vicar’s Daughter a boy fit only for manual labour, who “takes to carpentering, and works well”, is not degraded by doing the best he can (The Vicar’s Daughter, ch. XXII, “My Second Dinner-Party”). (General knowledge, not drawn from the corpus:) the novels honour craftsmen and labourers, such as the shoemaker in Salted with Fire and the shepherd and farm folk of Sir Gibbie and Donal Grant, and MacDonald often recalls that Jesus worked as a carpenter.

Neighbouring words

  • duty (the work to hand). The thing that lies next to be done.
  • service · divine service (true name). Work for one’s neighbour is service of God.
  • works · merit (false sense). Works done to earn salvation are dead works.
  • care · anxiety (enemy). Thought for tomorrow, which takes the mind from today’s work.
  • rest (partner). The rest Jesus gives is not idleness but work without care.

Scripture. My Father worketh hitherto, and I work (John 5:17). I must work the works of him that sent me, while it is day (John 9:4). Whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men (Colossians 3:23).
