A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

salvation

save · saved

Frequency in the non-fiction: salvation 78; save, saved, saves and saving about 210 (many in the sense “except”); Saviour 52. The main sources are “Justice”, “The Truth in Jesus”, “The Hardness of the Way” and “Salvation from Sin”.

In brief. Deliverance from sin itself, not from its punishment: the making of a man good, a true son of the Father, at one with God. MacDonald attacks the “low” notion of a salvation from consequences as a corruption of the gospel. Salvation is from ourselves, from the possibility of doing wrong again; it is hard, and nothing less than “absolute oneness with God” counts. It cannot be had by correct opinion or a plan, only by faith in the living Christ and the doing of his will, and it is wrought out by us as God works it in us.

Shades of meaning

  1. From sin, not from its consequences. “The notion that the salvation of Jesus is a salvation from the consequences of our sins, is a false, mean, low notion. The salvation of Christ is salvation from the smallest tendency or leaning to sin” (Unspoken Sermons, “Justice”). To read he shall save his people from their sins as salvation from punishment is a “miserable fancy” which “has terribly corrupted the preaching of the gospel” (The Hope of the Gospel, “Salvation from Sin”). “He came to make us good, and therein blessed children” (same). See sin · evil and punishment.

  2. From ourselves. “Christ died to save us, not from suffering, but from ourselves; not from injustice, far less from justice, but from being unjust” (Unspoken Sermons, “Freedom”). What he came to deliver us from is “not the things we have done, but the possibility of doing such things any more” (The Hope of the Gospel, “Salvation from Sin”). See self.

  3. Oneness with God, nothing less. “If by salvation they mean less than absolute oneness with God, I count it no salvation, neither would be content with it if it included every joy in the heaven of their best imagining” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Hardness of the Way”). “This faith is salvation; it is God and the man one” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Truth in Jesus”). See oneness · harmony.

  4. Saved, not merely safe. The drowning man seized by the hair may cry out that he is saved, “and he may be safe, but he is not saved; this is far from a salvation to suffice” (Unspoken Sermons, “Abba, Father!”). Those content “to be saved” do not care “for being perfect as their Father in heaven is perfect, but for being what they call saved” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Hardness of the Way”). A man is saved only when raised to a state in which, “be the temptation what it might, he would not yield” (same).

  5. One plan only. “There is but one plan of salvation, and that is to believe in the Lord Jesus Christ” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Truth in Jesus”), which means doing what he says. Paul’s “design was not to teach any plan of salvation other than obedience to the Lord of Life” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Mirrors of the Lord”). Faith is in a person, not a transaction: “it is faith in the man who did and is doing everything for us that will save him” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Truth in Jesus”). See faith and Jesus · elder brother.

  6. Not by opinion. God will not let a good man’s wrong opinions damn him, “No more than he will let the correct opinions of another, who lives for himself, save him. The best salvation even the latter could give would be but damnation” (same). See opinion · doctrine · theology, sense 2.

  7. God our salvation. Job’s friends are “men who would rather receive salvation from God, than God their salvation” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Voice of Job”). The frightened child must run “into the salvation of the Father’s arms” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Fear of God”). See Father.

  8. Worked out with God. Salvation is not done to a man without him. Any system that teaches “that a man can be made good, as a good dog is good, without his own willed share in the making” is false (Unspoken Sermons, “The Last Farthing”). Our full sonship cannot come “but by our working out the salvation he is working in us” (Unspoken Sermons, “Abba, Father!”). We are “saved into the original necessity and bliss of our being, only by choosing God for the father he is, and doing his will” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Creation in Christ”). See will and obedience.

Neighbouring words

  • sin (what it saves from). Senses 1 and 2; see sin · evil.
  • punishment, consequences (what it is not chiefly from). Sense 1.
  • oneness, sonship (what it is). Senses 3 and 8.
  • safe (the lesser word). Sense 4.
  • plan, scheme, system (its counterfeit). Sense 5.
  • faith, obedience (its means). Senses 5 and 8.
  • consuming fire (one of its instruments). “the man who acquiesces in the burning is saved by the fire” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Consuming Fire”); see consuming fire.

Scripture. Matthew 1:21, he shall save his people from their sins, the text of “Salvation from Sin” (sense 1); Philippians 2:12–13, work out your own salvation with fear and trembling. For it is God which worketh in you (sense 8); Acts 16:31, Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ, and thou shalt be saved (sense 5); Mark 10:26–27, Who then can be saved? … with God all things are possible, behind sense 4; 1 Corinthians 3:15, he himself shall be saved; yet so as by fire.
