A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

law

commandment · law of liberty

Frequency in the non-fiction: law and laws 371. The main sources are “Love Thy Neighbour”, “The Way”, “The Truth” and the Miracles.

In brief. MacDonald uses law of the moral law, of the laws of nature, and of the deepest law of the universe, which is the will of God. The moral law is good but infinite: no one keeps it except by love, and its keeping only shows a hunger it cannot fill. Man is not made for law but for love; love is the creator of law and fills it full. The laws of nature are facts, not yet truths, and are the waving of God’s garments; miracles fulfil the deepest of them. At the end there will be no law but the perfect law of liberty.

Shades of meaning

  1. Love the creator of law. “Love is law, because it is infinitely more than law. It is of an altogether higher region than law—is, in fact, the creator of law” (Unspoken Sermons, “Love Thy Neighbour”). “We are not made for law, but for grace—or for faith, to use another word so much misused” (same). See love and neighbour, sense 2.

  2. Infinite, and so unkept. “The law itself is infinite, reaching to such delicacies of action, that the man who tries most will be the man most aware of defeat” (same). “As he cannot keep the law without first rising into the love of his neighbour, so he cannot love his neighbour without first rising higher still” (same), into the love of God.

  3. The commandments the road. The law is not to be bypassed: “Then the road to eternal life is the keeping of the commandments! Had the Lord not said so, what man of common moral sense would ever dare say otherwise?” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Way”). But “the keeping of the law had served to develop a hunger which no law or its keeping could fill” (same). See obedience and eternal, sense 5.

  4. Filled full and set aside. The kingdom is “the all-embracing love that fills the law full and sets it aside” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Hardness of the Way”). In heaven there will be “no law but the perfect law of liberty” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Inheritance”). See freedom.

  5. The law of liberty in creation. God’s world moves “by no dead law, but in the harmony of the vital law of liberty, God’s creative perfection—all ordered from within” (Unspoken Sermons, “Man’s Difficulty Concerning Prayer”). God can answer prayer without breaking a law: to deny that he can “modify his plans” is “to me absurd” (same). See prayer.

  6. Laws of nature are not yet truths. Of an invariable fact: “Call it a law if you will—a law of nature if you choose—that it always is so, but not a truth” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Truth”). Laws exist for an end: “neither fact nor law is there for the sake of fact or law; each is but a mean to an end” (same). See truth and nature.

  7. Miracles fulfil deeper law. Those who see only the surface of the miracles “will, most likely, see in them only a violation of the laws of nature: when they behold the heart of them, they will recognize there at least a possible fulfilment of her deepest laws” (Miracles, “Introduction”). A cure shows “his law of life conquering the law of death” (Miracles, “The Cure of Simon’s Wife’s Mother”). See miracle.

  8. The one law of the universe. Behind all laws is God’s will. The painful consequences of sin “exist by the one law of the universe, the true will of the Perfect” (The Hope of the Gospel, “Salvation from Sin”). “The creature’s liberty is what his obedience to the law of his existence, the will of his maker, effects for him” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Hope of the Universe”). “Labour is a law of the universe, and is not an evil. Death is a law of this world at least, and is not an evil. Torture is the law of no world but the hell of human invention” (same). See will and hell.

Neighbouring words

  • love, grace, faith (what man is made for instead). Sense 1.
  • commandments (its moral form). Sense 3.
  • liberty, freedom (its final form). Senses 4 and 5; see freedom.
  • fact, truth (what a natural law is and is not). Sense 6; see truth.
  • will of God (the law behind laws). Sense 8; see will.
  • justice (its relation). “We are made on too large a scale altogether to have any pure relation to mere justice” (Unspoken Sermons, “Love Thy Neighbour”); see justice.
  • miracle (its fulfilment). Sense 7.

Scripture. Romans 13:10, love is the fulfilling of the law, quoted in “Love Thy Neighbour” (sense 1); Matthew 19:17, if thou wilt enter into life, keep the commandments (sense 3); James 1:25 and 2:12, the perfect law of liberty (sense 4); Galatians 6:7, whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap (sense 8); Romans 8:2, the law of the Spirit of life … hath made me free from the law of sin and death (sense 7).
