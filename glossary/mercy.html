A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

mercy

merciful

Frequency in the non-fiction: mercy 63; merciful 26. Concentrated in “Justice” and in the chapter on the Beatitudes, “The Reward of Obedience”.

In brief. God’s loving dealing with the sinner, and the same disposition in man toward his neighbour. MacDonald’s chief claim is that mercy is not a relaxation of justice but the same thing: God is merciful in rendering to every man according to his work. Mercy is therefore not always gentle; it may punish and compel. And it must pass through a man: he cannot receive what he will not give.

Shades of meaning

  1. One with justice. The text of “Justice” is Psalm 62:12, Also unto thee, O Lord, belongeth mercy: for thou renderest to every man according to his work. MacDonald reads the “for” literally: “I believe that justice and mercy are simply one and the same thing; without justice to the full there can be no mercy, and without mercy to the full there can be no justice” (Unspoken Sermons, “Justice”). God “is absolutely just, and there is no deliverance from his justice, which is one with his mercy” (same). See justice.

  2. Infinite in God. Mercy cannot be limited without making a man better than God. To those who would limit it he answers with irony: “Then mercy is not a divine attribute, for it may exceed and be too much; it must not be infinite, therefore cannot be God’s own” (same). “He cannot be sometimes merciful, and not always merciful” (same). The same argument ends hell: “shall a man be more merciful than God?” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Consuming Fire”). See hell, sense 4.

  3. Merciful because severe. God’s mercy deals with what a man has made of himself: “A man’s work is his character; and God in his mercy is not indifferent, but treats him according to his work” (Unspoken Sermons, “Justice”). “If God punish sin, it must be merciful to punish sin” (same). Even small losses are “of the mercy of God; it comes to teach us to let them go” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Cause of Spiritual Stupidity”). See wrath.

  4. Mercy that must go out to come in. On Blessed are the merciful: for they shall obtain mercy: “Mercy cannot get in where mercy goes not out. The outgoing makes way for the incoming” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Reward of Obedience”). This is not a bargain but a capacity: “The reward of the merciful is, that by their mercy they are rendered capable of receiving the mercy of God—yea, God himself, who is Mercy” (same).

  5. The greatest mercy. What God most wants to give is the disposition itself: “The greatest mercy that can be shown to man, is to make him merciful; therefore, if he will not be merciful, the mercy of God must compel him thereto” (same). It matters most to the giver: “It is a small thing to a man whether or not his neighbour be merciful to him; it is life or death to him whether or not he be merciful to his neighbour” (same).

  6. Human mercy. The merciful seldom get mercy back from men, “None the less, being the children of their father in heaven, will they go on to show mercy, even to their enemies” (same). Toward a wrongdoer “our mercy must ever be within call” (The Hope of the Gospel, “God’s Family”). Righteousness itself “requires of us love and helping mercy as our highest obligation and justice to our fellow men” (Unspoken Sermons, “Righteousness”).

  7. Uncovenanted mercy. MacDonald mocks the phrase for grace given outside the terms of a scheme: God’s “uncovenanted mercy is larger than that of his ministers” (The Hope of the Gospel, “Sorrow the Pledge of Joy”).

In the fiction and poetry. Falconer distrusts the sentimental kind: “surely our tender mercies are cruel” (Robert Falconer, ch. XVII, “In the Country”).

Neighbouring words

  • justice (the same thing). Sense 1; see justice.
  • forgiveness (its chief act). Senses 3 and 4: If ye forgive not men their trespasses, neither will your Father forgive your trespasses.
  • punishment, wrath (its severe form). Sense 3; see wrath.
  • pity (its weaker cousin). Pity can be weak (In the fiction); mercy is strong.
  • love (its source). See love.
  • merciful, unmerciful (the disposition). Senses 4 to 6.

Scripture. Psalm 62:12, the text of “Justice” (sense 1); Matthew 5:7, Blessed are the merciful: for they shall obtain mercy (sense 4); Matthew 18:23–35, the unmerciful servant (sense 5); Luke 6:36, Be ye therefore merciful, as your Father also is merciful; Hebrews 4:16, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need.
