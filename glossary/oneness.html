A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

oneness

harmony · unity · at one

Frequency in the non-fiction: oneness 26; harmony and its forms 107; unity 32. The main sources are “Life”, “The Creation in Christ” and “Freedom”.

In brief. Oneness with God is for MacDonald the end of creation and the content of eternal life and salvation. It is not sameness. God made individuals by separating them from himself so that they might freely become one with him and with each other, and there can be no oneness where there is only one. The pattern and bond of all oneness is the love of the Son for the Father. Harmony is the same thing heard as music: the right relation of every part to the whole, of the creature’s will to the creator’s, of man’s being with itself. Sin is the absence of that harmony; joy is its presence.

Shades of meaning

  1. The end of creation. God separated the creature from himself to make individuality, consciousness and choice possible. “Hence the final end of the separation is not individuality; that is but a means to it; the final end is oneness—an impossibility without it. For there can be no unity, no delight of love, no harmony, no good in being, where there is but one. Two at least are needed for oneness” (Unspoken Sermons, “Life”). See individual · individuality and Maker · creation.

  2. Oneness is eternal life. “This life, this eternal life, consists for man in absolute oneness with God and all divine modes of being, oneness with every phase of right and harmony” (same). “If by salvation they mean less than absolute oneness with God, I count it no salvation” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Hardness of the Way”). “Without oneness with God, the continuance of existence would be to me the all but unsurpassable curse” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Way”). See eternal and salvation.

  3. The Son’s love the bond of the universe. “It is not the fact that God is all in all, that unites the universe; it is the love of the Son to the Father. For of no onehood comes unity; there can be no oneness where there is only one” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Creation in Christ”). That devotion is “the bond of the universe, the chain that holds it together, the one active unity, the harmony of things” (same). A child’s life is “perfect response to his parents? thorough oneness with them?” (same). See Jesus · elder brother and child.

  4. Not sameness. “Is not the knowledge of difference essential to the deepest love? Can there be oneness without difference? harmony without distinction? Are all to have the same face? then why faces at all?” (The Hope of the Gospel, “Sorrow the Pledge of Joy”). “Otherness is the essential ground of affection” (Unspoken Sermons, “Love Thy Neighbour”). See neighbour.

  5. One with each other in God. “Only in God can two or many truly meet; only as they recognize their oneness with God can they become one with each other” (Miracles, “Miracles of Healing Solicited by the Sufferers”). Atonement is being made at one: Christ is “my atonement, making me at one with God and my fellows” (Unspoken Sermons, “Justice”). See justice.

  6. Harmony of wills. The creature is free in the “vital and claimant oneness with the life of all life” (Unspoken Sermons, “Freedom”), and “there is, in truth, no mid way between absolute harmony with the Father and the condition of slaves” (same). “All liberty must of course consist in the realization of the ideal harmony between the creative will and the created life” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Hope of the Universe”). What suffices for heaven is “that my will and desires keep time and harmony with his music” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Inheritance”). See freedom and will.

  7. Harmony within, and its loss. Sin is “the absence in the man of harmony with the being whose thought is the man’s existence” (The Hope of the Gospel, “Salvation from Sin”); “the one cure for any organism, is to be set right—to have all its parts brought into harmony with each other” (same). Obeying, a man’s nature becomes “almost in harmony with itself” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Truth”). “What is joy but the harmony of the spirit!” (Unspoken Sermons, “Self-Denial”). Jesus, more man than we, shows that we are “out of harmony, more or less, with everything in the house of our birth and habitation” (The Hope of the Gospel, “Jesus in the World”). See sin · evil and joy.

  8. The harmony of things. God’s world moves “in the harmony of the vital law of liberty” (Unspoken Sermons, “Man’s Difficulty Concerning Prayer”); “everything in God’s doing comes harmoniously with and from all the rest” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Temptation in the Wilderness”). Pain is felt as “that breach of the harmony of things whose sound is torture” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Eloi”). See law and nature.

Neighbouring words

  • individuality, difference, otherness (its condition). Senses 1 and 4.
  • love (its bond). Senses 3 and 4; see love.
  • atonement, at one (its making). Sense 5.
  • liberty, freedom (its fruit). Sense 6.
  • music, time, tune (its images). Senses 6 and 8.
  • discord, breach, strife (its opposites). To make the Lord’s will inconsistent with what we worship in him “is to introduce discord into that harmony whose end is to unite our hearts, and make them whole” (Unspoken Sermons, “It Shall Not be Forgiven”).
  • eternal life (its other name). Sense 2.

Scripture. John 17:21, That they all may be one; as thou, Father, art in me, and I in thee, that they also may be one in us (senses 3 and 5); John 10:30, I and my Father are one (sense 3); 1 Corinthians 15:28, that God may be all in all, which MacDonald qualifies (sense 3); Romans 5:11, by whom we have now received the atonement (sense 5).
