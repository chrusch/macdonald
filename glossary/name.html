A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

name

in my name · new name

Frequency in the corpus: name and names in about 220 paragraphs; the sermon “The New Name” is the main source. For the white stone and each man’s secret with God, see individual · individuality.

In brief. A true name says what a person is: the character, the nature, the meaning of the one who bears it. Only God can give a man his true name, because only God sees what he was made to be. To act or receive “in the name” of Christ is to act as one who represents him and is like him. God himself is a name only, unless we know him. Because each man’s name is between him and God, there is no room for comparison or ambition, only for aspiration.

Shades of meaning

  1. The label and the true name. “A name of the ordinary kind in this world, has nothing essential in it. It is but a label by which one man and a scrap of his external history may be known from another man and a scrap of his history” (Unspoken Sermons, “The New Name”). “The true name is one which expresses the character, the nature, the being, the meaning of the person who bears it. It is the man’s own symbol,—his soul’s picture, in a word” (same). See symbol.

  2. God alone can give it. “Who can give a man this, his own name? God alone. For no one but God sees what the man is” (Unspoken Sermons, “The New Name”). God’s name for a man is “his own idea of the man, that being whom he had in his thought when he began to make the child” (same). See Maker · creation.

  3. Given when he has become it. God knows the name from the first, “as surely as he sees the oak which he put there lying in the heart of the acorn”. But “it is only when the man has become his name that God gives him the stone with the name upon it, for then first can he understand what his name signifies” (Unspoken Sermons, “The New Name”). To tell the name “is to seal the success” (same). See growth.

  4. Aspiration, not ambition. “Here is room for endless aspiration towards the unseen ideal; none for ambition. Ambition would only be higher than others; aspiration would be high” (Unspoken Sermons, “The New Name”). “Gone then will be all anxiety as to what his neighbour may think about him. It is enough that God thinks about him” (same). See self.

  5. In my name. “For it is In my name. This means as representing me; and, therefore, as being like me” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Child in the Midst”). “Then to receive a child in the name of Jesus is to receive Jesus; to receive Jesus is to receive God” (same). So “worst of all is division in the name of Christ who came to make one” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Salt and the Light of the World”). See child.

  6. A name only. “To say Thou art God, without knowing what the Thou means—of what use is it? God is a name only, except we know God” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Temptation in the Wilderness”). Those who use Christ’s name for a pagan scheme get only a word: “such assertions going by the name of Christianity, are nothing but the poor remnants of paganism” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Truth in Jesus”). See know · knowledge.

  7. Hallowed. “Hallowed be thy name, for thou only art true; thou only lovest; thou only art holy, for thou only art humble!” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Final Unmasking”). With the children of the kingdom “we praise and magnify and laud his name in itself, saying Our Father” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Child in the Midst”). See prayer and Father.

Neighbouring words

  • label (the outward name). Sense 1.
  • symbol, picture (what a true name is). Sense 1; see symbol.
  • white stone (its vehicle). Sense 3; see individual · individuality.
  • idea (God’s thought behind it). Sense 2.
  • ambition, aspiration (false and true seeking). Sense 4.
  • representing (what “in my name” means). Sense 5.

Scripture. Revelation 2:17, To him that overcometh … I will give him a white stone, and in the stone a new name written, which no man knoweth saving he that receiveth it (senses 1 to 4); Mark 9:37, Whosoever shall receive one of such children in my name, receiveth me (sense 5); Matthew 6:9, Hallowed be thy name (sense 7); Exodus 3:13–14, Moses asking the name of God (sense 6).
