A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

love

loving

Frequency in the non-fiction: love 1,025; love, loved, loves and loving together about 1,470.

In brief. The nature of God and the law of everything he has made. MacDonald knows “nothing deeper” in God than love, and treats every other attribute, justice, wrath, the consuming fire, as a form of it. In man love is not a feeling to be worked up but the condition he was made for, which God’s love produces in him and which the love of one’s neighbour makes possible.

Shades of meaning

  1. The essence of God. “In one word, God is Love. Love is the deepest depth, the essence of his nature, at the root of all his being” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Creation in Christ”). Creation follows from it: “when I am told that he is love, I see that if he were not love he would not, could not create. I know nothing deeper in him than love” (Unspoken Sermons, “Life”).

  2. Inexorable. Divine love does not give way to entreaty that would harm the one it loves: “Nothing is inexorable but love. Love which will yield to prayer is imperfect and poor” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Consuming Fire”). Its terrors are its other face: “The terror of God is but the other side of his love; it is love outside the house, that would be inside” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Cause of Spiritual Stupidity”). See consuming fire.

  3. Above law. Love makes law and goes beyond it: “We are not made for law, but for love. Love is law, because it is infinitely more than law” (Unspoken Sermons, “Love Thy Neighbour”). It is also the condition of justice: “Love is the law of our condition, without which we can no more render justice than a man can keep a straight line walking in the dark” (Unspoken Sermons, “Love Thine Enemy”).

  4. Love of the neighbour. Possible, and the way out of the self. “It is possible to love our neighbour as ourselves. Our Lord never spoke hyperbolically” (Unspoken Sermons, “Love Thy Neighbour”). It is “the only door out of the dungeon of self” (same sermon). The enemy is included; when a man is the child of his Father in heaven, “Then shall he love his neighbour as himself, even if that neighbour be his enemy” (Unspoken Sermons, “Love Thine Enemy”).

  5. Unselfishness. Love is not a merit but the opposite of self-seeking: “Love is unselfishness. In the main we love because we cannot help it. There is no merit in it” (Unspoken Sermons, “Self-Denial”).

  6. Forgiveness. “Forgiveness is love towards the unlovely” (Unspoken Sermons, “It Shall Not be Forgiven”), and love comes first: “God’s love is ever in front of his forgiveness” (same sermon). It loves “according to needs and not according to” deserts, being “then most divine” (same sermon).

  7. Love like his. The end of sonship is not only to be loved or to love God but to love as he loves: God must have sons and daughters “in the sense that they love like him, love as he loves” (Unspoken Sermons, “Abba, Father!”).

In the fiction. The novels repeat sense 1 in many voices. In Annals of a Quiet Neighbourhood God’s appeal is “love me, for love is life, and I love you” (Annals of a Quiet Neighbourhood, ch. XXX, “A Sermon to Myself”). In Donal Grant love surrounds every life: “Every life is between two great fires of the love of God” (Donal Grant, ch. XLI, “The Music-Nest”).

Neighbouring words

  • God, Father (identity). Sense 1; see Father.
  • law, justice (what it makes and exceeds). Sense 3; see justice.
  • consuming fire, terror, wrath (its forms to the impure). Sense 2.
  • neighbour, brother, enemy (its objects). Sense 4.
  • self, selfishness (opposite). Sense 5; see self.
  • forgiveness (its act toward the wrong-doer). Sense 6.
  • fear (what it casts out). “So long as love is imperfect, there is room for torment” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Fear of God”).
  • life (its expression). Eternal life “consists in a love as deep as it is universal” (Unspoken Sermons, “Life”). See life.

Scripture. 1 John 4:8, God is love, behind sense 1; Leviticus 19:18 and Matthew 22:39, thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself, the text of “Love Thy Neighbour”; Romans 13:10, love is the fulfilling of the law (sense 3); Matthew 5:44, Love your enemies; 1 John 4:18, perfect love casteth out fear.
