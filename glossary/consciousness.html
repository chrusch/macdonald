A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

consciousness

conscious · self-consciousness

Frequency in the non-fiction: conscious, consciously, consciousness and their compounds 231. The main sources are “Life” and the essay “A Sketch of Individual Development”.

In brief. Awareness, and especially awareness of being. MacDonald treats consciousness as a growing thing that God means to enlarge without limit, and as the place where God’s presence is felt. It is not life itself, only its outcome; and the consciousness of self is far less than the self, which God knows better. The passage from consciousness to self-consciousness is a second birth, but self-consciousness can also be diseased, a turning inward that needs to give way to the consciousness of what God thinks of us.

Shades of meaning

  1. The aim of creation. God set in motion “that division from himself which in its grand result should be individuality, consciousness, choice, and conscious choice—choice at last pure, being the choice of the right, the true, the divinely harmonious” (Unspoken Sermons, “Life”). See Maker · creation, sense 3.

  2. Enlarged without limit. “We have no conception of what life might be, of how vast the consciousness of which we could be made capable” (same). The victor in pain cries “for more power, more consciousness of being, more God in him” (same). See life and being.

  3. Not life itself. “I have been speaking as if life and the consciousness of it were one; but the consciousness of life is not life; it is only the outcome of life” (same). The real life “is life because it wills itself” (same). So we must “not glory in the mere consciousness of health and being” (Unspoken Sermons, “Self-Denial”). See will.

  4. Less than the self. “The being of which we are conscious, is not our full self; the extent of our consciousness of our self is no measure of our self; our consciousness is infinitely less than we” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Remission of Sins”). God is “more necessary even to that poor consciousness of self than our self-consciousness is necessary to our humanity” (same). See self.

  5. Self-consciousness a second birth. In the growth of the child, “from consciousness he passes to self-consciousness. This is a second birth, for now a higher life begins. When a man not only lives, but knows that he lives, then first the possibility of a real life commences” (A Dish of Orts, “A Sketch of Individual Development”). The next stage is conscience: he “knows that he is self-conscious—that he has a conscience” (same). See conscience.

  6. Paltry and diseased self-consciousness. The other face of the word: “To have a consciousness of our own ideal being flashed into us from the thought of God! Surely for this may well give way all our paltry self-consciousnesses, our self-admirations and self-worships!” (Unspoken Sermons, “The New Name”). In faith it takes the form of “that diseased self-consciousness which nowadays is always asking” whether it has faith (Miracles, “Miracles of Healing Solicited by the Sufferers”). See name · new name.

  7. The consciousness of God. Contentment depends on it: “no man can have the consciousness of God with him and not be content” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Hardness of the Way”). Its loss is the deepest trial, when “all consciousness of well-being shall have vanished” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Eloi”) and the will must hold to God without it. See see · vision, sense 8.

  8. The unconscious. Much of God’s work in us is below awareness. The cry More life! is “the unconscious prayer of all creation” (Unspoken Sermons, “Life”); from nature “we learn God unconsciously” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Truth”); and the poet’s thoughts appear “upon the wall of his consciousness” from the unknown (A Dish of Orts, “The Imagination: Its Functions and its Culture”). See imagination.

Neighbouring words

  • life (its source). Senses 2 and 3; see life.
  • self, self-consciousness (its object and its danger). Senses 4 to 6; see self.
  • conscience (its moral stage). Sense 5; see conscience.
  • individuality, choice (its companions in creation). Sense 1.
  • content, well-being (its fruit). Sense 7.
  • know (its higher form). Sense 5: knowing that one knows.

Scripture. John 10:10, I am come that they might have life, and that they might have it more abundantly, behind sense 2; Psalm 139:23–24, Search me, O God, and know my heart, behind sense 4; Romans 8:22, the whole creation groaneth, behind sense 8.
