A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

self

selfishness · self-denial

Frequency in the non-fiction: self 357, selfish and selfishness 107, myself 89, individuality 38, self-denial and deny himself and similar 24; true self, real self, deeper self and deepest self 18.

In brief. The conscious “I”, which God gives in order that it may be given back. MacDonald uses the word for two things that must be kept apart: the self that rules and must be denied, and the true self that God made and that appears only when the other is abandoned. God is the “deeper, deepest self” of every person, so denying self is not a loss of identity but the way to it.

Shades of meaning

  1. A gift, to be offered. “The self is given to us that we may sacrifice it; it is ours that we like Christ may have somewhat to offer—not that we should torment it, but that we should deny it” (Unspoken Sermons, “Self-Denial”).

  2. Self as ruler. What is denied is self in the governing place. “We must refuse, abandon, deny self altogether as a ruling, or determining, or originating element in us. It is to be no longer the regent of our action” (Unspoken Sermons, “Self-Denial”). The capital in the same sermon marks this sense: “the cunning and deceitful Self—ever cunning and deceitful until it is informed of God” (Unspoken Sermons, “Self-Denial”).

  3. Not self-conquest. Thwarting one’s likings for their own sake can feed a worse self: “a far worse than even the unchained animal self—the demoniac self. True victory over self is the victory of God in the man, not of the man alone” (Unspoken Sermons, “Self-Denial”).

  4. The true self. The person God made, hidden by the evil in him and freed by its removal. “That person, with the evil thing cast out of him, will be yet more the person, for he will be his real self” (Unspoken Sermons, “Love Thine Enemy”). “And I will wait until the true self looks out of thine eyes, and knows the true self in me” (same sermon). After the fire: “The young, ever young self, will remain” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Consuming Fire”).

  5. God the deepest self. The self is finally possessed by “the indwelling God, who is its deeper, its deepest self” (Unspoken Sermons, “Self-Denial”). In “The Mirrors of the Lord” the Spirit rises “into the Self we call I and me, but which lives immediately from him” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Mirrors of the Lord”). The yielded self is raised “then first and only to its true individuality, freedom, and life” (Unspoken Sermons, “Self-Denial”).

  6. The prison of self. Self alone is the worst condition MacDonald can imagine. The love of our neighbour “is the only door out of the dungeon of self” (Unspoken Sermons, “Love Thy Neighbour”). In “The Last Farthing” the outer darkness is “the fearful, endless, unavoidable presence of that self” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Last Farthing”).

In the fiction and poetry. Lilith stages sense 4 against sense 2 at the climax. Lilith refuses to be remade: “I will be myself and not another!” (Lilith, ch. XXXIX, “That Night”). Asked “Will you not be your real self?” (same chapter), she answers, “I will be what I mean myself now” (same chapter). The Diary names the two selves in its own terms, “the bastard self” and “the Christ-self in us” (Diary of an Old Soul, February), and prays to be set free “From the low self loathed of the higher me” (Diary of an Old Soul, June).

Neighbouring words

  • selfishness (its working). The self acting as ruler (sense 2).
  • will (its rival). Self and will are distinct, and self-denial cuts the line between them; see will, neighbouring words.
  • individuality (its fruit). Sense 5.
  • cross, deny (the daily act). “Every day till then we have to take up our cross; every hour to see that we are carrying it” (Unspoken Sermons, “Self-Denial”).
  • soul, life (the thing saved by losing). The Lord’s words on losing life are read of the self: “Whosoever shall lose his life, for my sake, the same shall save it” (Unspoken Sermons, “Self-Denial”).
  • neighbour, brother (the way out). Sense 6.
  • son (what the self becomes). The Self “must be the ‘slave of Christ,’ that the Son may make it also the free son of the same Father” (Unspoken Sermons, “Self-Denial”).

Scripture. Matthew 16:24–25, If any man will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow me. For whosoever will save his life shall lose it, the text of “Self-Denial”; Luke 9:23, which adds daily (sense 2).
