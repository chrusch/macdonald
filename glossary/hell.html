A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

hell

hell-fire · outer darkness

Frequency in the non-fiction: hell 84. There is no sermon on hell as such; the word is spread through “The Consuming Fire”, “Justice”, “The Last Farthing” and “Salvation from Sin”.

In brief. Hell is real for MacDonald, and terrible, but it is God’s and serves his purpose. It is the condition of a self shut in with itself, away from God, and it is used by God to free his child from sin. It is not endless torment for the sake of punishment, and it is not the devil’s kingdom. He also uses hell for any state of the heart closed against God and its neighbour, here and now.

Shades of meaning

  1. God’s hell, not the devil’s. “For hell is God’s and not the devil’s. Hell is on the side of God and man, to free the child of God from the corruption of death” (The Hope of the Gospel, “Salvation from Sin”). It is a means of mercy: “I believe that no hell will be lacking which would help the just mercy of God to redeem his children” (Unspoken Sermons, “Justice”). See mercy and justice.

  2. The last resort. God uses everything gentler first. If nothing else will do, “then hell-fire” (Unspoken Sermons, “Man’s Difficulty Concerning Prayer”). A man has a claim “to be sent out into the outer darkness, whether what we call hell, or something speechlessly worse, if nothing less will do” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Voice of Job”).

  3. A school, with an end. What a man would not learn on earth he will learn there, and come out: “when thou hast learned of God in hell what thou didst refuse to learn of him upon the gentle-toned earth” (Unspoken Sermons, “Love Thine Enemy”). So, strangely, “a man is nearer heaven when in deepest hell than just ere he begins to reap the reward of his doings” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Last Farthing”). See consuming fire.

  4. Hell itself destroyed. In the end God will undo hell: “wilt thou not cast Death and Hell into the lake of Fire—even into thine own consuming self?” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Consuming Fire”). An endless hell would make the saved unhappy: “shall a man be more merciful than God? Shall, of all his glories, his mercy alone not be infinite?” (same sermon). See death.

  5. The principle of hell. Hell is self-ownership: the one principle of hell is “I am my own” (Unspoken Sermons, “Kingship”). Its opposite is to share in the right: “To be made greater than one’s fellows is the offered reward of hell” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Reward of Obedience”). See self and king.

  6. The hell of the heart. Hell names inward states now: men must be saved from “the hell of their hearts” (The Hope of the Gospel, “Salvation from Sin”), and the rich young man “shall not be left in the hell of possession!” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Hardness of the Way”). The other side is also true: “When a man is true, if he were in hell he could not be miserable” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Truth”).

  7. The hell of human invention. The theologians’ hell of endless torture is a man-made thing: “Torture is the law of no world but the hell of human invention” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Hope of the Universe”). Bare endless existence would be “worthless as hell” (Unspoken Sermons, “Life”).

In the fiction and poetry. Falconer attacks a prison system that keeps a man “in a confinement as like their notion of hell as they dare to make it—namely, a place whence all the sweet visitings of the grace of God are withdrawn” (Robert Falconer, ch. XVII, “In the Country”). When Thomas Worboise faces his real self at sea, “Hell had come at last; and he burned in its fire” (Guild Court, ch. XLIII, “What Thomas Was About”): the novel’s hell is the beginning of his cure.

Neighbouring words

  • outer darkness (its deepest form). Sense 2; see consuming fire, where MacDonald describes it.
  • consuming fire, hell-fire (its agent). Senses 2 to 4.
  • punishment, the uttermost farthing (its purpose and term). Sense 3; see justice.
  • mercy (what it serves). Sense 1; see mercy.
  • self, possession (its principle). Senses 5 and 6.
  • death (its companion). Sense 4; see death.

Scripture. Revelation 20:14, And death and hell were cast into the lake of fire, behind sense 4; Matthew 5:26, Thou shalt by no means come out thence, till thou hast paid the uttermost farthing, read as a promise that one will come out (sense 3); Matthew 8:12, the outer darkness; Psalm 139:8, if I make my bed in hell, behold, thou art there.
