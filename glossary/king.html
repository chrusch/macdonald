A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

king

kingship

Frequency in the non-fiction: king 93; kingship 12; kingdom 168. The sermon “Kingship” holds most of the doctrine.

In brief. One who bears witness to the truth. MacDonald takes Christ’s answer to Pilate, To this end was I born … that I should bear witness unto the truth, as a definition: Jesus is king because he witnesses, and every man who stands for the truth is a king of the same kind. Kingship in this sense is service, and its throne is the cross. The opposite kingship is the self’s claim to be its own king.

Shades of meaning

  1. King by witness. “Jesus is a king because his business is to bear witness to the truth” (Unspoken Sermons, “Kingship”). His title rests on nothing else: “Jesus is a king in virtue of no conquest, inheritance, or election, but in right of essential being” (same sermon).

  2. A kingdom of kings. Christ reigns only over his own kind: “What then is the kingdom over which the Lord cares to reign, for he says he came into the world to be a king? I answer, A kingdom of kings, and no other” (Unspoken Sermons, “Kingship”).

  3. Every true man a king. “The true king is the man who stands up a true man and speaks the truth, and will die but not lie. The robes of such a king may be rags or purple; it matters neither way” (Unspoken Sermons, “Kingship”). The free son is one: “He is in himself, in virtue of his truth, free. He is in himself a king” (Unspoken Sermons, “Freedom”).

  4. Crowned on the cross. Christ’s kingship is clearest when it is mocked: “Just because of those robes and that crown, that sceptre and that throne of ridicule, he was the only real king that ever sat on any throne” (Unspoken Sermons, “Kingship”).

  5. King as servant. “What is the kingdom of Christ? A rule of love, of truth—a rule of service. The king is the chief servant in it” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Child in the Midst”).

  6. The self as king. The rival kingship is hell’s: “For the one principle of hell is—’I am my own. I am my own king and my own subject” (Unspoken Sermons, “Kingship”). See self, sense 2.

  7. Not the first name of God. MacDonald will not let kingship stand for God’s deepest character: “If he were a king, a governor; if the name that described him were The Almighty, thou mightst well doubt whether there could be light enough in him for thee” (Unspoken Sermons, “Light”). Where he does use it, God reigns from within: “Let your king possess his own. Let God throne himself in you, that his liberty be your life” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Remission of Sins”).

In the fiction. In Lilith the sleepers in the house of death include one whom the narrator “thought he must be a king who had died fighting for the truth” (Lilith, ch. VII, “The Cemetery”), sense 3 in a single figure. The sick king in The Princess and Curdie, misled by his courtiers, cries “I am a king, and I will be a king” (The Princess and Curdie, ch. 19, “The King’s Chamber”) before the true kingship is restored to him.

Neighbouring words

  • truth, witness (its ground). Sense 1; see truth.
  • kingdom (its realm). Sense 2.
  • servant, service (its form). Sense 5.
  • freedom (its condition). Sense 3.
  • Father (the higher name). Sense 7; see Father.
  • self (the usurper). Sense 6.

Scripture. John 18:37, Thou sayest that I am a king. To this end was I born, and for this cause came I into the world, that I should bear witness unto the truth, the text of “Kingship”; Matthew 20:25–28, The princes of the Gentiles exercise dominion over them … it shall not be so among you (sense 5); John 19:5, Behold the man!, and 19:14, Behold your King! (sense 4).
