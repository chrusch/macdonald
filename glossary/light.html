A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

light

darkness · candle of the Lord

Frequency in the non-fiction: light 563, darkness 162, shine and shining 138, candle 18, lamp 22. The sermon “Light” is the centre.

In brief. First, what God is, on the word of 1 John 1:5; then whatever lets things be seen as they are, from sunlight to the inward light of conscience and reason. MacDonald insists that God’s light is light in our sense of the word, so that nothing which looks to us like darkness can be true of him. Light is also the visible side of life: Christ is light so that men may have life.

Shades of meaning

  1. What God is. The text of the sermon “Light” is for MacDonald the whole gospel: “If God be light, what more, what else can I seek than God, than God himself! Away with your doctrines!” and “I am saved—for God is light!” (Unspoken Sermons, “Light”).

  2. Light in our sense. God’s light is not a different thing called by the same name: “never will anything prove to be light that is not of the same kind with that which we mean by light, with that in a thing which makes us call it light” (Unspoken Sermons, “Light”). So “Whatever seems to me darkness, that I will not believe of my God” (same sermon).

  3. The candle of the Lord. The light in man by which he judges, called conscience or reason. The reader of the Gospels needs “the candle of the Lord—that is, the light of truth in his inward parts” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Way”); “reason must humbly watch over all—reason, the candle of the Lord” (Unspoken Sermons, “Man’s Difficulty Concerning Prayer”). It is never quite put out: “His candle yet burns dim in the man’s soul; that candle must shine as the sun” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Voice of Job”).

  4. The light that judges. Condemnation is refusing to come into the light, not having been in the dark. “No man is condemned for anything he has done; he is condemned for continuing to do wrong. He is condemned for not coming out of the darkness” (Unspoken Sermons, “Light”). God gives his children everything but one thing: “The only thing he will not give them is—leave to stay in the dark” (same sermon).

  5. Light for the sake of life. In “The Creation in Christ” light is the outside of life: “The light is what we see and shall see in him; the life is what we may be in him” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Creation in Christ”). “But light is not enough; light is for the sake of life” (same sermon).

  6. The disciples’ light. The light of the world is God’s will shown in a life, never the man himself. “God alone is the light, and our light is the shining of his will in our lives” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Salt and the Light of the World”). “A candle is not lighted for itself; neither is a man” (same chapter).

  7. Bodily light. The literal sense, which MacDonald keeps close to the rest. Of the man born blind: “the light of the world invaded his astonished soul” (Miracles, “Miracles of Healing Unsolicited”).

In the fiction and poetry. The vicar of Annals makes sense 2 an argument for God’s existence: “a being that was not all light would be no God at all” (Annals of a Quiet Neighbourhood, ch. XIII, “Young Weir”). In The Princess and the Goblin the great-grandmother keeps “a lamp that never went out” (ch. 14, “That Night Week”), the lamp “which guided her pigeons home through the darkest night” (same chapter). The Diary prays for sense 3: “Be thou, O Life eternal, in me light” (Diary of an Old Soul, February).

Neighbouring words

  • darkness (opposite). Darkness in God is denied (sense 2); darkness in man is chosen (sense 4). The two kinds of error are not equal: “He will not let it hurt me to mistake the light for darkness, while I take not the darkness for light” (Unspoken Sermons, “Light”).
  • life (what it serves). Sense 5.
  • truth (what it shows). Sense 3, “the light of truth”.
  • conscience, reason (its inward forms). Sense 3.
  • consuming fire (its other face). See consuming fire, sense 6.
  • see, vision (its use). See know.
  • fear (a sign of untruth). “To fear the light is to be untrue, or at least it comes of untruth” (Unspoken Sermons, “Light”).

Scripture. 1 John 1:5, God is light, and in him is no darkness at all, and John 3:19, light is come into the world, and men loved darkness rather than light, the two texts of “Light”; John 1:9, the true Light, which lighteth every man that cometh into the world; Proverbs 20:27, The spirit of man is the candle of the Lord (sense 3); Matthew 5:14–16, Ye are the light of the world (sense 6).
