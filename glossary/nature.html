A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

nature

natural · Nature

Frequency in the non-fiction: nature 495; natural 85. “Nature” is often capitalized when it means the visible world.

In brief. Two main senses. First, the outward world of earth, sky and growth, which MacDonald reads as God’s continuing work and speech; the miracles show in a moment what nature does slowly. Second, the inner constitution of a being: God’s nature, which is love, and man’s nature, whose deepest layer is God in him. The two meet: the truth of nature, outward or inward, is divine.

Shades of meaning

  1. Nature as God’s work. The miracles are “an epitome of God’s processes in nature beheld in immediate connection with their source” (Miracles, “Introduction”), and they are less than “those mighty goings on of nature with God beheld at their heart” (same). There is no half-way worship: “Half to an insensate nature, half to a living God, is a worship that cannot stand. God is all in all, or no God at all” (Miracles, “The Cure of Simon’s Wife’s Mother”).

  2. Nature as revelation. “I believe that every fact in nature is a revelation of God, is there such as it is because God is such as he is” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Truth”). Its look matters more than its mechanism: “the appearances of nature are the truths of nature, far deeper than any scientific discoveries” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Voice of Job”). See truth.

  3. Nature as symbol and mood. “Nature is brimful of symbolic and analogical parallels” (Miracles, “Miracles Granted to the Prayer of Friends”). Its work is on the feelings rather than the reason: “The best Nature does for us is to work in us such moods in which thoughts of high import arise” (A Dish of Orts, “The Fantastic Imagination”). “Wordsworth is the high priest of nature thus regarded” (A Dish of Orts, “Wordsworth’s Poetry”). See imagination.

  4. The nature of God. God’s nature is love, and to doubt it is the root error: Job’s friends imagined “that his nature is not absolute love, absolute self-devotion” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Voice of Job”). God means us to share it: “God is ever seeking to lift us up into the sharing of his divine nature” (Unspoken Sermons, “Man’s Difficulty Concerning Prayer”).

  5. Man’s deepest nature. Below a man’s faults is a nature that is God’s: “his nature is of God, and the deepest in him is the divine” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Final Unmasking”). Love of neighbour comes from “our deepest nature, the presence, namely, of God in us” (Unspoken Sermons, “Love Thy Neighbour”). And that nature is filial: “our true nature is childhood to the Father” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Creation in Christ”). See child · son · sonship and being, sense 6.

  6. Evil is not nature. Sin spoils a nature but is not part of it: “no evil is, or ever could be, of the essential being and nature of the creature God made!” (Unspoken Sermons, “Man’s Difficulty Concerning Prayer”). Salvation removes “the sins in his being which spoil his nature” (The Hope of the Gospel, “Salvation from Sin”).

  7. The highest nature is righteousness. Natural and moral law are one at the top, so that the miracles fulfil “that righteousness which is highest nature” (Miracles, “The Government of Nature”). The rule of the kingdom is “the rule of kind, of nature, of deepest nature—of God” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Child in the Midst”). See righteousness.

In the fiction and poetry. In Within and Without Julian finds nature an incomplete revelation without the Son: “I see the man; I cannot find the God” (Poetical Works, “Within and Without”).

Neighbouring words

  • law, laws of nature (its regularity). MacDonald denies that a law of nature is itself a truth; it is God’s way of working (senses 1 and 7).
  • miracle (its quickened form). Sense 1.
  • truth, fact (its meaning). Sense 2; see truth.
  • symbol, mood, imagination (how it speaks). Sense 3; see imagination.
  • being, idea (its inward counterpart). Senses 5 and 6; see being.
  • kind (its old English synonym). Sense 7.
  • natural (the adjective). Often neutral (natural fear, sense 1 of fear), sometimes the lower life not yet risen into the divine.

Scripture. Romans 1:20, the invisible things of him from the creation of the world are clearly seen, being understood by the things that are made, behind sense 2; 2 Peter 1:4, that by these ye might be partakers of the divine nature, behind sense 4; Psalm 19:1, The heavens declare the glory of God.
