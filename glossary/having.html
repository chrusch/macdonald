A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

having

possession · own · things

Frequency in the non-fiction: possess and its forms 145; own 746 (mostly in the ordinary sense). The main sources are “The Hardness of the Way”, “The Way” and “The Heirs of Heaven and Earth”.

In brief. The right and the wrong ways of having. Things are given to train a man to “true possession”; held wrongly, they possess him and make him a slave. MacDonald turns the ordinary sense around: only life can possess, only the man who can do without a thing truly has it, and the meek, who claim nothing, inherit the earth. The deepest possession is God himself, the one thing that can be the soul’s own. The opposite principle, “I am my own”, is the principle of hell.

Shades of meaning

  1. Possessing and being possessed. “Things are given us, this body first of things, that through them we may be trained both to independence and true possession of them. We must possess them; they must not possess us” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Hardness of the Way”). The rich young man “would have that as a possession which must possess him” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Way”).

  2. Slavery to things. “The man who for consciousness of well-being depends upon anything but life, the life essential, is a slave; he hangs on what is less than himself” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Hardness of the Way”). The same is said in “The Way”: “a man is in bondage to what ever he cannot part with that is less than himself” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Way”). Those who lack money and fret are slaves too: “it is not the rich man only who is under the dominion of things” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Hardness of the Way”). See freedom.

  3. By life we possess. The man stripped by death may learn “that having never was, never could be well-being; that it is not by possessing we live, but by life we possess” (same). Only what lives can be owned: “life alone can be the possession of life. The dead possess their dead only to bury them” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Heirs of Heaven and Earth”). See life.

  4. Having God, having all. “Things can never be really possessed by the man who cannot do without them” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Hardness of the Way”). “He who has God, has all things, after the fashion in which he who made them has them” (same). God is the one true possession: the soul is at last “with the only that can be its own, the one that it can possess, the one that can possess it. For God is the heritage of the soul in the ownness of origin” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Truth in Jesus”).

  5. The meek inherit. Ownership by purchase is a delusion: “There does not exist such a power of possessing as he would arrogate” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Heirs of Heaven and Earth”). The meek man inherits the earth and “possesses it as his father made him capable of possessing, and the earth of being possessed” (same). “The child of the maker is naturally the inheritor” (same).

  6. The hell of possession. God will not leave his child in it: “his child shall not be left in the hell of possession!” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Hardness of the Way”). Death strips the things and leaves “the full passion of possession, the slavery of prizing the worthless part of the precious” (same). See hell, sense 6.

  7. Things and understanding. Possessions dull the mind as well as the heart: “With the disciples as with the rich youth, it was Things that prevented the Lord from being understood” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Cause of Spiritual Stupidity”). See understand.

In the fiction and poetry. The title of What’s Mine’s Mine is the creed of the incomer Mr Palmer, who turns an old woman out of her cottage: “I choose to turn her off my land. What’s mine’s mine, as I mean every man jack of you to know—chief and beggar!” (What’s Mine’s Mine, ch. VIII, “Mistress Conal”). The novel sets against him the chief who holds his land as a trust for his people.

Neighbouring words

  • things (its objects). Senses 1, 2 and 7; MacDonald often capitalizes Things.
  • own, ownness (its root). Sense 4; the false form is “I am my own” (see king and hell, sense 5).
  • inherit, heir (its true form). Sense 5.
  • slavery, bondage (its result). Sense 2; see freedom.
  • riches, money (its commonest form). Senses 2 and 6.
  • life (what alone possesses). Sense 3; see life.
  • content (its test). Sense 4.

Scripture. Mark 10:17–25, the rich young man, he went away grieved: for he had great possessions, the text of “The Way” and “The Hardness of the Way”; Matthew 5:5, Blessed are the meek: for they shall inherit the earth (sense 5); 1 Corinthians 3:21–23, all things are yours … and ye are Christ’s; and Christ is God’s; Luke 12:15, a man’s life consisteth not in the abundance of the things which he possesseth (sense 3).
