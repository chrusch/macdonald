A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

hope

hoping

Frequency in the non-fiction: hope, hopes and hoped 604. Besides the title The Hope of the Gospel, the chief sources are its last chapter, “The Hope of the Universe”, and “The Higher Faith”.

In brief. The forward lean of faith: expectation, grounded in the Father’s character, of more than we can see or prove. MacDonald makes hope a duty of largeness. Because God is greater than our thoughts, we must hope “to the full extent God has made possible to us”, and a small hope is a small idea of God. Hope reaches beyond death, to the lost, and to the animals and the whole creation. It is not faith, but it is not idle either: it must be let appear.

Shades of meaning

  1. Hoping to the full. Ignorance is a reason for hope, not caution: “That we do not know, is the best reason for hoping to the full extent God has made possible to us. If then we go wrong, it will be in the direction of the right” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Hope of the Universe”). “Those that hope little cannot grow much. To them the very glory of God must be a small thing, for their hope of it is so small as not to be worth rejoicing in” (same).

  2. Beyond our thoughts. We cannot out-hope God: “Shall God’s thoughts be surpassed by man’s thoughts? God’s giving by man’s asking? God’s creation by man’s imagination? No” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Higher Faith”). Climb as high as we will, “still shall we find the heavens higher than the earth” (same). See imagination.

  3. Hope and faith. “Hope is not faith, but neither is faith sight; and if we have hope we are not miserable men” (Miracles, “The Raising of the Dead”). Hope of God himself may matter more than proof: Jesus’ word Be not afraid; only believe aims “To arouse the higher, the hopeful, the trusting nature of a man” (same). See faith.

  4. Hope for the lost. The mother must “Hope in God, mother, for the deadest of thy children, even for him who died in his sins” (The Hope of the Gospel, “Sorrow the Pledge of Joy”). Our hardness toward others comes from lack of it: “It is because we hope not for them in thee, not knowing thee, not knowing thy love, that we are so hard and so heartless to the brothers and sisters whom thou hast given us” (Unspoken Sermons, “Love Thy Neighbour”). See hell and mercy.

  5. The hope of the creation. Following Romans 8:20–21, God subjected the creation to vanity “in the hope that the creation itself shall be delivered from the bondage of corruption into the glorious liberty of the children of God” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Hope of the Universe”). MacDonald extends it to the animals: “I know of no reason why I should not look for the animals to rise again, in the same sense in which I hope myself to rise again” (same). See Maker · creation, sense 7.

  6. The hope of seeing him. Its centre is likeness to Christ: the climber’s courage “is the hope that one day he shall be like him, seeing him as he is” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Hardness of the Way”). The first reward of doing well is “To grow purer in heart, and stronger in the hope of at length seeing God” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Right Hand and the Left”).

  7. Hope made visible. “If we do indeed expect better things to come, we must let our hope appear” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Salt and the Light of the World”). “We should never talk as if death were the end of anything” (same). See death.

In the fiction and poetry. The Diary makes hope an act of surrender to work it cannot follow: “What though thy work in me transcends my sense— / Too fine, too high, for me to understand— / I hope entirely. On, Lord, with thy labour grand” (Diary of an Old Soul, April).

Neighbouring words

  • faith, trust (its root). Sense 3; see faith.
  • imagination (its instrument). Senses 1 and 2: refusing to imagine is refusing to hope.
  • despair (its opposite). Sense 3.
  • resurrection, life beyond death (its object). Senses 5 and 7; see death.
  • reward (its true form). Sense 6.
  • the gospel (its content). The title phrase: the hope the good news gives.

Scripture. Romans 8:20–21, the creature was made subject to vanity … in hope, Because the creature itself also shall be delivered from the bondage of corruption into the glorious liberty of the children of God (sense 5); 1 John 3:2–3, we shall be like him; for we shall see him as he is. And every man that hath this hope in him purifieth himself (sense 6); 1 Corinthians 2:9, Eye hath not seen … the things which God hath prepared, behind sense 2; 1 Corinthians 15:19, If in this life only we have hope in Christ, we are of all men most miserable (sense 3).
