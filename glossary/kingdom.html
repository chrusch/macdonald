A Glossary of MacDonald’s Terms

kingdom

kingdom of heaven · kingdom of God · the kingdom of the Father

Frequency in the non-fiction: kingdom in about 100 paragraphs, mostly as the kingdom of heaven or of God. The main source is “The Heirs of Heaven and Earth” in The Hope of the Gospel. The novels talk about it constantly, most directly The Marquis of Lossie, Paul Faber, Thomas Wingfold and The Elect Lady.

In brief. For MacDonald the kingdom of heaven is the kingdom of the Father in the midst of His family. It is founded on principles opposite to those of the kingdoms and families of the world: it has no likeness to them except to repudiate what distinguishes their rulers, and its greatest is the humblest servant. It is the home of perfect brotherhood, and the poor in spirit, the unambitious and those who give themselves away are its freemen. It is near, and the way to prepare for it is to begin now to do as must be done in it, sending away one’s sins one by one. One is in it by obedience, not by feeling: faith is obedience, not confidence, and there are a thousand more doors to knock at after one is in. It cannot be shaken because it has nothing weak in it, and it comes not with observation, like leaven. Its scale of rank is being, not station. Possessing it and inheriting the earth take the same spirit. It is within men, not around them, and it is not the Church. It is come not when God’s will is our law but when it is our will. Rule and kingship in it are treated under king, and what it will be like hereafter under heaven.

Shades of meaning

  1. The Father’s kingdom in His family. “The good news of Jesus was just the news of the thoughts and ways of the Father in the midst of his family” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Heirs of Heaven and Earth”). The kingdom of heaven “was founded, and must at length show itself founded on very different principles from those of the kingdoms and families of the world” (same), and “the greatest in the kingdom of heaven is the man who is humblest and serves his fellows the most” (same). See Father and humility.

  2. Unlike the kingdoms of this world. “That kingdom has no relation with or resemblance to the kingdoms of this world, deals with no one thing that distinguishes their rulers, except to repudiate it” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Heirs of Heaven and Earth”). It will not open its gates even to the most miserable man “who would rise above his equal in misery” (same). See king and world.

  3. The home of perfect brotherhood. MacDonald heaps up names for it: “The kingdom of God, the refuge of the oppressed, the golden age of the new world, the real Utopia, the newest yet oldest Atlantis, the home of the children” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Heirs of Heaven and Earth”). “It is the home of perfect brotherhood” (same). Its freemen and citizens are the poor, the humble, “the men who give themselves away” (same). See neighbour and home.

  4. Begin to do as is done in it. The Baptist’s hearers “must send away their sins; and each must begin with the fault that lay next him. The kingdom of heaven was at hand: they must prepare the way of the Lord by beginning to do as must be done in his kingdom” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Remission of Sins”). “Send away your sins, for the kingdom of heaven is near” is still “the one message to the world” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Remission of Sins”). See repentance and obedience.

  5. In by obedience, not by feeling. Clementina wishes she were in the kingdom; Malcolm answers that she must be in it, or she could not wish it to be such as it is. “Faith is obedience, not confidence” (The Marquis of Lossie, “A Visitation”). “There are a thousand more doors to knock at after you are in, my lady. No one content to stand just inside the gate will be inside it long” (same). “He who does what his Lord tells him, is in the kingdom, if every feeling of heart or brain told him he was out” (same). See faith and growth.

  6. Being is rank. Mr Graham tells the rich man in his pew that the man he looks down on may be far above him, “according to the absolute facts of creation, the scale of the kingdom of God, in which being is rank” (The Marquis of Lossie, “Hope Chapel”). In the kingdom “people do not think whether I am promoted, or whether you are promoted” (A Dish of Orts, “True Christian Ministering”). See being and essential.

  7. A kingdom that cannot be moved. “The kingdom he has given us cannot be moved, because it has nothing weak in it: it is of the eternal world, the world of being, of truth. We, therefore, must worship him with a fear pure as the kingdom is unshakeable” (Unspoken Sermons, “The Consuming Fire”). See consuming fire and worship.

  8. Not with observation. “The kingdom cometh not with observation, and the working of the leaven of its approach must be chiefly unseen” (The Miracles of Our Lord, “Miracles of Healing Unsolicited”). Our poor idea of Jesus and our disobedience delay its coming. See growth and miracle.

  9. The kingdom and the earth. “The same spirit, then, is required for possessing the kingdom of heaven, and for inheriting the earth” (The Hope of the Gospel, “The Heirs of Heaven and Earth”). “If we are the Lord’s, we possess the kingdom of heaven, and so inherit the earth. How many who call themselves by his name, would have it otherwise: they would possess the earth and inherit the kingdom!” (same). See inheritance and having · possession.

  10. Within, not around. The rector of The Seaboard Parish wants churchmen to have “high thoughts about the kingdom of heaven as within men and not around them” (The Seaboard Parish, “A Sunday Evening”). A dumb boy can be in it before a poet: “Gibbie was already in the kingdom of heaven, and Donal would have to suffer, before he would begin even to look about for the door by which a man may enter into it” (Sir Gibbie, “Rumours”). And a curate can be outside it: Wingfold “had all this time been skirting the wall of the kingdom of heaven without even knowing that there was a wall there” (Thomas Wingfold, Curate, “An Incident”). See heart and child.

  11. Not the Church. Wingfold “never mistook it for the kingdom of God” (There and Back, “Wingfold and Barbara”), and the history of the kingdom is “a very different thing from what is called church-history” (Paul Faber, Surgeon, “The Old Garden”). Church-pride is “a carrying into the kingdom of Heaven of the habits and judgments of the kingdom of Satan. I am wrong! such things can not be imported into the kingdom of Heaven: they can only be imported into the Church” (Paul Faber, Surgeon, “The Pottery”). See church.

  12. Near the gate, and far. “Many a thief is a better man than many a clergyman, and miles nearer to the gate of the kingdom” (Thomas Wingfold, Curate, “A Sermon to Leopold”). “Men who strive to make money are unconsciously pulling instead of pushing at the heavy gate of the kingdom” (A Rough Shaking, “The Baker’s Cart”). Many stay out “because they will not believe the tiny key that is handed them, fit to open its hospitable gate” (What’s Mine’s Mine, “Granny Angry”), and until we learn that the only way to serve God is to serve our neighbour, “we may have knocked at the wicket-gate, but I doubt if we have got one foot across the threshold of the kingdom” (Guild Court, “A Comparison”). See neighbour and duty.

  13. When his will is our will. “The kingdom of heaven is not come, even when God’s will is our law: it is come when God’s will is our will” (David Elginbrod, “A Sunday with Falconer”). Obedience is still its law, but “while obedience is the law of the kingdom, it is of considerable importance that that which is obeyed should be in very truth the will of God” (Robert Falconer, “Robert’s Plan of Salvation”). “The world will never be right till the mind of God is the measure of things, and the will of God the law of things. In the kingdom of Heaven nothing else is acknowledged” (Annals of a Quiet Neighbourhood, “Tom’s Story”). See will and freedom.

In the fiction and poetry. Besides senses 5, 6 and 10 to 13, Malcolm says that “as a child I thought about what the kingdom of heaven could mean” (The Marquis of Lossie, “St Ronan’s Well”), and Mr Graham calls young Marshal “very nigh the kingdom of heaven, if not indeed within its gate” (The Marquis of Lossie, “A New Pupil”). At sea, Florimel’s morning is “a fore-scent—even this could not be called a foretaste, of the kingdom of heaven” (The Marquis of Lossie, “Mid Ocean”). Rank in it runs the other way from the world’s. “It is in the kingdom of heaven only we are bound to rise” (The Elect Lady, “The Crawfords”), and “the only way to rise in the kingdom of heaven is to do the work given us to do” (same), and “in the kingdom of heaven it is all looking up” (Malcolm, “An Honest Plot”). In the dream of a shop in heaven, “The man here that doeth most service” is the one who “standeth highest with the Lord of the place” (Thomas Wingfold, Curate, “A Shop in Heaven”). Gibbie works for “the kingdom whose end is unity” (Sir Gibbie, “Needful Odds and Ends”), and its coming “is retarded by defect of courtesy” (Paul Faber, Surgeon, “The Heath at Nestley”). Of a girl who longs for a house where all love one another: “If men’s wishes are not always for what the kingdom of heaven would bring them, their miseries at least are all for the lack of that kingdom” (Castle Warlock, “A Winter Idyll”). And when it comes, says Mary Marston, “things will always look what they are. My father used to say people will grow their own dresses there, as surely as a leopard his spots” (Mary Marston, “Honor”). The narrator of The Marquis of Lossie scorns the reverence that rebukes “the childlike spirit that does not know another kingdom than that of God and that of Mammon” (The Marquis of Lossie, “The Peacemaker”).

Neighbouring words

  • king and kingship (the ruler). A kingdom of kings, where the king is chief servant.
  • heaven (its fulfilment). Touch with God, the kingdom come.
  • the kingdoms of this world (opposite). Founded on what the kingdom repudiates.
  • brotherhood (its form). The home of perfect brotherhood.
  • Mammon (the other kingdom). The only other choice the childlike spirit knows of.
  • the gate and the door (the figure of entry). Entered by obedience, and then many more doors; pushed, not pulled.
  • the Church (not to be mistaken for it). Its pride is the kingdom of Satan’s, imported.

Scripture. Repent ye: for the kingdom of heaven is at hand (Matthew 3:2). Seek ye first the kingdom of God, and his righteousness (Matthew 6:33). The kingdom of God cometh not with observation (Luke 17:20). Wherefore we receiving a kingdom which cannot be moved, let us have grace (Hebrews 12:28). Blessed are the meek: for they shall inherit the earth (Matthew 5:5). Not every one that saith unto me, Lord, Lord, shall enter into the kingdom of heaven; but he that doeth the will of my Father (Matthew 7:21). Behold, the kingdom of God is within you (Luke 17:21). It is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle, than for a rich man to enter into the kingdom of God (Matthew 19:24).
